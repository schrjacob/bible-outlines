# The Church and the Spirit

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

Chapell, theology sections I ("The Church") and K ("Other Questions," chiefly the Holy Spirit). Standards: **WCF 25-26, 30-31; WLC 61-66, 69, 82-83, 86** (Chapell lists no Shorter Catechism questions for section I). Hot topics here: **role of women in the church**, **confessional subscription**, and **charismatic gifts**. On hot topics, state and defend your **own** view; the notes give the Standards' position and the main alternatives. The **Tier** column ranks each question (1 = know cold, 2 = second pass, 3 = if time allows).

## I. The Church

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the universal or catholic church? | The **whole number of the elect**, past, present, and future, gathered into one under Christ, the head: his body, his bride, and "the fulness of him that filleth all in all" (WCF 25.1). Eph. 1:22-23; 5:23-32; Heb. 12:22-24; Rev. 7:9. Also called the **invisible church**, since only God knows who the elect are (2 Tim. 2:19). |
| 1 | What is the visible church? | All throughout the world who **profess the true religion**, together with their **children** (WCF 25.2; Acts 2:39; 1 Cor. 7:14; Gen. 17:7), the kingdom of the Lord Jesus Christ, the house and family of God, out of which there is **no ordinary possibility of salvation**. It is **mixed**: wheat and tares grow together (Matt. 13:24-30, 47-50). Local congregations are its parts. |
| 1 | What are the attributes of the church? Prove from Scripture. | Traditionally (Nicene Creed): **one** (Eph. 4:3-6; John 17:20-23), **holy** (1 Pet. 2:9; Eph. 5:25-27), **catholic** or universal (Matt. 28:19-20; Rev. 7:9), and **apostolic** (Eph. 2:20; Acts 2:42). Reformed writers also speak of its **unity, purity, and perpetuity** (WCF 25.4-5; Matt. 16:18). |
| 1 | What are the marks of the church? Prove from Scripture. | The marks by which a true church is known: (1) the **faithful preaching of the Word**, (2) the **right administration of the sacraments**, and (3) the faithful exercise of **church discipline** (WCF 25.4-5; Acts 2:42; Matt. 28:19-20; 18:15-20; John 10:27; Gal. 1:8-9). The visible church is **more or less pure** according to how well the gospel is taught and worship and discipline are kept. |
| 1 | Who is the head of the church? Prove from Scripture. | **Jesus Christ alone** (WCF 25.6; Eph. 1:22; 4:15; 5:23; Col. 1:18). No pope or other man is head of the church. |
| 1 | Define three basic forms of church government. | **Episcopal:** government by **bishops** in a hierarchy (Roman Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican/Episcopal, Methodist). **Presbyterian:** government by **elders** (presbyters) in representative **courts** (session, presbytery, General Assembly). **Congregational:** government by the **local congregation**, each church self-governing (Baptist, independent churches). |
| 1 | What are the principles of Presbyterian church government? Support them from Scripture. | (1) **Christ is the only head**, and Scripture the only rule (Eph. 1:22; Isa. 8:20). (2) A **plurality of elders** in every church, of equal rank, both **teaching and ruling** (Acts 14:23; 20:17, 28; Titus 1:5-9; 1 Tim. 5:17). (3) **Representation**: officers elected by the people (Acts 6:3-6; 14:23). (4) **Courts and connectionalism**: the church is one body, with appeal to higher courts (Acts 15; 1 Cor. 12:12-13). (5) **Two ordinary offices** in the local church, elder and deacon (Phil. 1:1; 1 Tim. 3). (6) The church's power is **ministerial and declarative**, under Christ (Matt. 16:19; 18:18). |
| 1 | What is the role of women in the church? Support your answer from Scripture. | Women are **equal** members and heirs of salvation (Gal. 3:28; Acts 2:17-18), gifted by the Spirit and called to serve widely: prophesying in worship (1 Cor. 11:5), teaching women and children (Titus 2:3-5), assisting in ministry (Rom. 16:1-7; Phil. 4:2-3; Acts 18:26). The PCA reserves the **ordained offices of teaching and ruling elder** to qualified men (1 Tim. 2:11-14; 3:1-7; Titus 1:5-9; 1 Cor. 14:33-35). Views differ on the specifics of what roles beyond office are open (leading, teaching men, diaconal service). **Hot topic: Role of women in the church.** |
| 1 | May women serve as officers in the church? Support your answer from Scripture. | The PCA says **no** to the ordained offices of **elder** (teaching or ruling) **and deacon**: "The ordinary and perpetual classes of office in the Church are elders and deacons... **In accord with Scripture, these offices are open to men only**" (BCO 7-2). The qualifications are masculine (1 Tim. 3:2, 12 "husband of one wife"; Titus 1:6), and Paul does not permit a woman to teach or exercise authority over a man (1 Tim. 2:12), grounding it in creation order (2:13). Yet "godly men **and women**" may be appointed by the session "to assist the deacons in caring for the sick, the widows, the orphans, the prisoners, and others in need"; they "are **not officers** of the church" and are not ordained (BCO 9-7; Rom. 16:1; 1 Tim. 3:11 are debated). Unordained people may not be given the titles of the ordained offices (7-3). Egalitarians reply that the texts are culture-bound, which complementarians deny. |
| 2 | What is "subscription?" | The **vow** by which an officer declares his adherence to the church's doctrinal standards. In the PCA an officer is asked, "Do you **sincerely receive and adopt the Confession of Faith and the Catechisms** of this Church, as containing the **system of doctrine** taught in the Holy Scriptures; and do you further promise that if at any time you find yourself **out of accord with any of the fundamentals** of this system of doctrine, you will on your own initiative make known to your Presbytery (or Session) the change which has taken place in your views?" (the second ordination vow, BCO 21-5 for teaching elders; 24-6 for ruling elders and deacons; 19-3 for licensure). |
| 2 | Why is there disagreement on this subject? | Over **how strictly** the Standards bind. The BCO says "our Constitution does **not require the candidate's affirmation of every statement and/or proposition** of doctrine in our Confession of Faith and Catechisms," but it is the **right and responsibility of the presbytery** to determine whether the candidate is "out of accord with any of the **fundamentals**" of the standards (BCO 21-4.f). The candidate **must state the specific instances** in which he differs, and the court "may grant an exception to any difference of doctrine **only if** in the court's judgment the declared difference is **not out of accord with any fundamental** of our system of doctrine because the difference is **neither hostile to the system nor strikes at the vitals of religion**" (21-4.g; 13-6.c for ministers transferring in). Disputes concern **what counts as a fundamental** and **what exceptions are allowed** (for example, on creation days or the regulative principle), and whether an exception must be a matter of **language or a matter of substance**; the alternatives are **strict (full) subscription** (every proposition), **system subscription** (the system of doctrine), and **loose** (*quatenus*) subscription, which the PCA rejects. The PCA's approach is often called **good-faith subscription**. **Hot topic: Confessional subscription.** |
| 2 | What view of subscription do you hold and why? | State your view plainly: as a PCA officer you vow to the Standards as containing the **system of doctrine** of Scripture, with exceptions declared to the presbytery and judged by it. Be ready to explain what you hold, give reasons from the nature of a confession (accountability, unity, honesty, and the church's testimony), and distinguish **declared** exceptions from private ones. |
| 1 | What privileges do Christians share? | "**Communion of the saints**" (WCF 26.1): union with Christ and with each other in love, sharing in his graces, sufferings, death, and resurrection, and in each other's gifts and graces; fellowship in the means of grace, public and private worship, and the sacraments. 1 John 1:3, 7; Eph. 4:3-6; 1 Cor. 12:12-27; Acts 2:42-47; Rom. 12:4-5. |
| 1 | What duties do Christians owe one another? | To **love** one another (John 13:34-35), **bear** burdens (Gal. 6:2), **forgive** (Col. 3:13), **serve** with their gifts (1 Pet. 4:10), **exhort** and **encourage** (Heb. 3:13; 10:24-25), **pray** (James 5:16), and **relieve** each other's outward needs (WCF 26.2; 1 John 3:16-18; Acts 2:44-45; James 2:15-16; Gal. 6:10). |
| 1 | Does the "communion of the saints" deny the right of private property? Explain. | **No.** The communion of saints "take away or infringe the title or propriety which each man hath in his goods and possessions" (WCF 26.3). Acts 5:4 (Peter to Ananias: "Was it not your own?"); the early sharing was **voluntary** (Acts 2:44-45; 4:32-37); Ex. 20:15, 17 (the eighth and tenth commandments assume property); Eph. 4:28; 2 Cor. 8-9. |
| 1 | What authority does the church possess? | A **spiritual, ministerial, and declarative authority** given by Christ, who is the church's only head: the **keys of the kingdom** (Matt. 16:19; 18:18; John 20:23), exercised through the Word, sacraments, and discipline (WCF 30.1-2; 31.3). It has no authority to bind the conscience beyond Scripture (WCF 20.2; 31.3) or to wield the sword (John 18:36). Believers are to obey their leaders (Heb. 13:17; 1 Thess. 5:12-13). |
| 1 | What is the purpose of church censures? | WCF 30.3: "for the reclaiming and gaining of offending brethren; for deterring of others from the like offenses; for purging out of that leaven which might infect the whole lump; for vindicating the honour of Christ, and the holy profession of the gospel; and for preventing the wrath of God, which might justly fall upon the Church, if they should suffer his covenant, and the seals thereof, to be profaned by notorious and obstinate offenders." The primary aim is the **offender's restoration**. |
| 1 | What censures may the church impose? | WCF 30.4: **admonition, suspension from the Lord's Supper** for a season, and **excommunication**. The BCO (30-1) lists five: **admonition, suspension from the Sacraments, excommunication, suspension from office, and deposition from office** (the last two for officers). See [Discipline and Review](../church-government/discipline.md). |
| 1 | Identify some Scripture passages that support the practice of church discipline. | **Matt. 18:15-20** (the steps of discipline); **1 Cor. 5:1-13**; 2 Cor. 2:5-11; 2 Thess. 3:6, 14-15; Titus 3:10; Gal. 6:1; 1 Tim. 5:19-20; Heb. 12:5-11; Rom. 16:17; Rev. 2:14-16, 20. |
| 1 | Who may properly call church assemblies, synods, or councils? For what purpose(s)? | **Church officers**, by virtue of their office and the authority of Christ's Word (Acts 15:2, 6; WCF 31.1-2). Their purpose is to **determine controversies of faith and cases of conscience**, set down rules for the better ordering of worship and government, **receive complaints**, and give **authoritative judgments** (WCF 31.3). They deal only with what is **ecclesiastical** (WCF 31.4). |
| 1 | What authority do church councils possess? How should Christians respond to them? | Their decrees, if **agreeable to the Word**, are to be received **with reverence and submission**, not only for their agreement with the Word but also for the **authority** by which they are made, as an ordinance of God appointed in his Word (WCF 31.2; Acts 15:28-31; 16:4; Heb. 13:17). But **all councils since the apostles may err**, and many have, so they are not the rule of faith or practice, only a **help** in both (31.3). |

## K. Other Questions: The Holy Spirit and the Cults

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | What does the Bible teach about the Holy Spirit's work in the Old Testament? | The Spirit is **active** from creation (Gen. 1:2; Job 33:4; Ps. 104:30); **inspires** the prophets and writers (2 Pet. 1:21; Ezek. 2:2); **empowers** leaders, judges, kings, and craftsmen (Ex. 31:1-5; Judg. 3:10; 1 Sam. 16:13); **renews** and brings believers to faith (Ps. 51:10-12; 143:10; Ezek. 36:26-27; see John 3:10); and is promised to be **poured out** on all flesh (Joel 2:28-29; Isa. 44:3). In the OT the Spirit's presence in individuals was more selective and could be taken away (Ps. 51:11); in the NT he indwells all believers permanently (John 14:17). |
| 2 | What is the Holy Spirit's function today? Is it different than in the past? | The **same Spirit** does the same kind of work, with a **fuller measure** since Christ's ascension and Pentecost (John 7:39; Acts 2:33). Today he **applies Christ's redemption** (regeneration, John 3:5-8; Titus 3:5; faith; adoption, Rom. 8:15; sanctification, 2 Thess. 2:13; assurance, Rom. 8:16), **indwells** every believer (Rom. 8:9-11; 1 Cor. 6:19), **illumines** Scripture (1 Cor. 2:10-14; Eph. 1:17-18), **gives gifts** (1 Cor. 12), **produces fruit** (Gal. 5:22-23), **empowers witness** (Acts 1:8), **glorifies Christ** (John 16:14), and **intercedes** (Rom. 8:26-27). His revelatory work of inspiring Scripture is complete. |
| 1 | What happened at Pentecost? How does this relate to the church today? | In Acts 2 the risen Christ, now ascended, **poured out the promised Holy Spirit** on the gathered disciples (Joel 2:28-32; Luke 24:49; Acts 1:4-8): a sound like wind, tongues of fire, and the speaking of **known languages** (2:6-11), after which Peter preached and about **3,000** were converted. It is a **unique, once-for-all event** that inaugurated the new-covenant age, formed the church as one body (1 Cor. 12:13), and began the mission to all nations (reversing Babel). For the church today: the Spirit now **permanently indwells** all believers (Acts 2:38-39), and Pentecost is **not repeated**, though believers are to be continually "filled" (Eph. 5:18). Acts 8, 10, and 19 extend the one Pentecost to Samaritans, Gentiles, and disciples of John. |
| 2 | Distinguish between the gifts and the fruit of the Spirit. | **Gifts** (*charismata*) are **abilities for service** the Spirit gives sovereignly to each believer for the common good, varied (1 Cor. 12:4-11; Rom. 12:3-8; Eph. 4:7-13; 1 Pet. 4:10-11). **Fruit** is the Spirit-produced **Christlike character** in **all** believers (Gal. 5:22-23). Gifts are for **service**; fruit is for **being**. Love is greater than gifts (1 Cor. 13), and gifts without love are worthless. |
| 1 | Does the Spirit give gifts today? Explain. | **Yes**: he gives every believer gifts for the building up of the church (1 Cor. 12:7, 11; Eph. 4:11-13; 1 Pet. 4:10). The debate is about **which** gifts (see the next row). |
| 2 | Are there gifts mentioned in Scripture that are not given today? Explain and defend. | **Cessationists** hold that **apostleship** and **revelatory and sign gifts** (new prophecy, tongues, healing, miracles) accompanied the founding of the church and **ceased** when Scripture was completed (1 Cor. 13:8-10; Eph. 2:20; 2 Cor. 12:12; Heb. 2:3-4; WCF 1.1: "those former ways of God's revealing his will unto his people being now ceased"). **Continuationists** argue that nothing in Scripture says they cease (1 Cor. 1:7; 12-14), while holding that Scripture is closed and sufficient. The PCA has tolerated both, provided the officer holds to the **sufficiency and finality of Scripture**. Know the Assembly's study reports and your presbytery's view. **Hot topic: Charismatic gifts.** |
| 1 | What do New Testament passages teach us concerning the following work of the Holy Spirit: how he comes, how he works, what he does, what his ministry is today? | **How he comes:** promised by the Father and sent by Christ (John 14:16, 26; 15:26; Acts 2:33), through the preached Word received in faith (Gal. 3:2; Acts 10:44), given to all believers at conversion (Rom. 8:9; 1 Cor. 12:13; Eph. 1:13-14). **How he works:** sovereignly (John 3:8), through the Word, convicting of sin (John 16:8-11), regenerating, illumining, sanctifying. **What he does:** seals (Eph. 1:13-14), assures (Rom. 8:16), prays (Rom. 8:26), bestows gifts and fruit. **His ministry today:** glorifies Christ (John 16:14), applies redemption, builds the church, and empowers mission. |
| 2 | How would you answer charismatic claims from Scripture? | With **gentleness**, and by Scripture: test everything (1 Thess. 5:19-22; 1 John 4:1-3; Acts 17:11); Scripture is **final and sufficient** (Heb. 1:1-2; 2 Tim. 3:16-17; Jude 3; Rev. 22:18); **no second blessing**: all believers are baptized in the Spirit at conversion (1 Cor. 12:13; Rom. 8:9); not all speak in tongues (1 Cor. 12:29-30); **suffering and weakness** are not signs of sin or faithlessness (2 Cor. 12:7-9; Phil. 2:25-27; 1 Tim. 5:23); worship must be **orderly** and aimed at **edification** (1 Cor. 14:26-40); the Spirit **glorifies Christ** rather than experiences (John 16:14); and **love** is the test (1 Cor. 13). |
| 2 | Answer from the Scriptures: A Mormon. | **God is one**, eternal, and has always been God, not a man who became a god (Isa. 43:10-11; 44:6, 8; Ps. 90:2; Num. 23:19; Mal. 3:6), and the Trinity is one God, not three gods. **Christ** is the eternal Creator (John 1:1-3; Col. 1:15-17), not a created spirit-brother of Lucifer. Salvation is by **grace through faith**, not works (Eph. 2:8-9). The **canon is closed** (Gal. 1:8-9; Jude 3; Rev. 22:18-19), so the Book of Mormon cannot add to Scripture. There is **no second chance** after death (Heb. 9:27); 1 Cor. 15:29 does not teach baptism for the dead. Humans do not become gods (Isa. 43:10). |
| 2 | Answer from the Scriptures: A Jehovah's Witness. | **Jesus is God**, not a created being (John 1:1; 8:58; 20:28; Col. 1:15-17; 2:9; Titus 2:13; Heb. 1:8); "firstborn" (Col. 1:15) means preeminent, not first created. The **Holy Spirit is a person**, not a force (Acts 5:3-4; John 16:13). Salvation is **by grace through faith**, not by works or service (Eph. 2:8-9; Rom. 3:28). **Christ rose bodily** (Luke 24:39; John 2:19-21). **Hell is real** (Matt. 25:46; Rev. 14:11). Note the New World Translation's alterations (John 1:1 "a god"; Col. 1:16 "other things"). |
