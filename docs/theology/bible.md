# The Bible

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

Chapell, theology sections A and B. Standards: **WCF 1; WLC 1-6, 154-160; WSC 3, 88-90.** The **Tier** column ranks each question (1 = know cold, 2 = second pass, 3 = if time allows).

## A. Introduction

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is meant by "Systematic Theology"? What are its divisions? | The organized study of what the whole Bible teaches, topic by topic (as opposed to the book-by-book study of biblical theology). Classic divisions: **theology proper** (God), **anthropology** (man), **Christology**, **soteriology** (salvation), **pneumatology** (the Spirit), **ecclesiology** (the church), **eschatology** (last things), plus the doctrine of Scripture (**bibliology**) first. The Westminster Standards follow this order. |
| 1 | What is the "Reformed Faith"? (Explain as to a new Christian.) | The branch of Protestant Christianity that grew out of the Reformation in Switzerland and Europe (Zwingli, Calvin, Knox) and that stresses the **sovereignty of God in all things, including salvation**, the **authority of Scripture alone**, and **salvation by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone, to the glory of God alone**. It is summed up in confessions such as the Westminster Standards and the Three Forms of Unity (Heidelberg Catechism, Belgic Confession, Canons of Dort). |
| 1 | What is "Covenant Theology"? Why is it important? (Explain as to a new Christian.) | The way of reading the Bible that sees God dealing with people through **covenants**: a covenant of works with Adam (broken), and the **covenant of grace** by which God saves sinners through Christ, promised in Gen. 3:15 and unfolding through Abraham, Moses, David, and the new covenant (Jer. 31:31-34). It is important because it shows the **unity of the Bible** (one people of God, one way of salvation by grace through faith, one Christ at the center) and it grounds Reformed teaching on baptism, the law, and the church (WCF 7). |
| 1 | List and explain the "Five Points of Calvinism." Where did they come from? | See [Core Doctrines and Proofs](core-doctrines.md): total depravity, unconditional election, limited atonement, irresistible grace, perseverance of the saints. They answer the five articles of the **Remonstrance** (1610) and were set out in the **Canons of Dort** (1618-19). |

## B. The Bible (WCF 1)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define and distinguish "general revelation" and "special revelation." | **General revelation:** God's self-disclosure to all people through creation, providence, and conscience (Ps. 19:1-6; Rom. 1:18-20; 2:14-15; Acts 14:15-17; 17:26-28). It shows his power, glory, and goodness and leaves everyone without excuse, but it does not give the saving knowledge of Christ. **Special revelation:** God's redemptive self-disclosure through his words and acts, supremely in Christ and in Scripture (Heb. 1:1-2; 2 Tim. 3:15-17). WCF 1.1. |
| 1 | Define and defend (including Scripture proofs) the "inspiration" of Scripture. | Scripture is **God-breathed**: God so superintended the human authors, using their personalities, vocabulary, and circumstances, that what they wrote is his Word, without error (**verbal, plenary** inspiration). **2 Tim. 3:16** ("All Scripture is breathed out by God"); **2 Pet. 1:20-21** ("men spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit"); 1 Cor. 2:13; 1 Thess. 2:13; Matt. 5:18; John 10:35. WCF 1.2, 1.4. |
| 2 | Define and defend the "necessity" of Scripture. | Because general revelation is not enough to give the knowledge of God and his will that is **necessary for salvation**, God was pleased to reveal himself and commit it all to writing, to preserve and propagate the truth, and to guard against the corruption of the flesh and the malice of Satan and the world (WCF 1.1). Rom. 10:14-17 (faith comes through hearing the word of Christ); 2 Tim. 3:15; Prov. 22:19-21. |
| 1 | What is the "canon"? Defend it scripturally. | The **canon** is the list (the "rule") of the 66 books that are the inspired, authoritative Word of God (WCF 1.2-3). It is **recognized by the church, not conferred by it**: Jesus affirmed the OT (Luke 24:44; Matt. 5:17-18; John 10:35), he promised the Spirit to bring the apostles to remember and teach (John 14:26; 16:13), Peter treats Paul's letters as Scripture (2 Pet. 3:15-16), and Paul cites Luke as Scripture (1 Tim. 5:18; Luke 10:7). The Apocrypha is not inspired (WCF 1.3). |
| 1 | Define and defend the "authority" of Scripture. | Scripture's authority rests **not on the testimony of any man or church** but wholly on **God, its author** (WCF 1.4). It is the final rule of faith and life, and the supreme judge in all controversies (1.10). Ps. 119:160; Isa. 8:20; Matt. 4:4; Matt. 15:3-9; 2 Pet. 1:19-21; 2 Tim. 3:16-17. |
| 1 | Define and defend the "sufficiency" of Scripture. | Scripture contains all things necessary for God's glory, man's salvation, faith, and life; nothing is to be added to it by new revelation or tradition (WCF 1.6). **2 Tim. 3:16-17** (it equips the man of God "for every good work"); Deut. 4:2; Ps. 19:7-9; Prov. 30:5-6; Rev. 22:18-19; Jude 3. Matters of worship and church government not spelled out are governed by Scripture's general rules (1.6). |
| 2 | Define and defend the "perspicuity" of Scripture. | Scripture is **clear** enough that its message of salvation can be understood by ordinary believers using the ordinary means; not everything is equally plain, but what is needful for salvation is clear somewhere in Scripture (WCF 1.7). Ps. 19:7; 119:105, 130; Deut. 29:29; 2 Pet. 3:16 (some things "hard to understand"); Acts 17:11. Obscurity is in our sin and ignorance, not in God's word. |
| 2 | What is distinctive about Reformed interpretation of Scripture? | **Scripture interprets Scripture**: the infallible rule for interpreting a passage is the rest of Scripture, and a clearer text explains a darker one (WCF 1.9). The supreme judge is the **Holy Spirit speaking in Scripture** (1.10), not the church, tradition, or private spirit. The Spirit's inward work is needed to understand (1.5-6). Rome binds interpretation to the church's teaching authority; Anabaptists and enthusiasts appeal to private revelation. |
| 2 | What principles should guide our interpretation of Scripture? | Read each passage in its **context** (immediate, book, whole Bible); honor its **genre** and **original meaning** (grammatical-historical method); let clear texts interpret unclear ones; see the **unity** of the Bible and its **progressive revelation**; read the OT in light of Christ (Luke 24:27, 44); distinguish **commands from narrative description**; depend on the Spirit's illumination (1 Cor. 2:12-14); and test applications by the analogy of faith (WCF 1.9). |
| 1 | Define and defend (including Scripture proofs) the "inerrancy" of Scripture. | The Bible, in its original manuscripts, is **without error in all that it affirms** (history, science as it speaks, as well as doctrine), because God, who cannot lie, is its author. Num. 23:19; Titus 1:2; Ps. 119:160; Prov. 30:5; John 10:35 ("Scripture cannot be broken"); John 17:17 ("Your word is truth"); Matt. 5:18. WCF 1.4-5 calls it "the infallible truth." (**Infallible**: not able to mislead; **inerrant**: not mistaken.) Related: the Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy (1978). |
| 1 | What do the Scriptures principally teach? | WSC 3: "The Scriptures principally teach what man is to believe concerning God, and what duty God requires of man." |

!!! tip "Memorize in order"
    **Inspiration > Inerrancy > Authority > Necessity > Sufficiency > Perspicuity** (and the canon). Each has a one-line definition and one proof text: 2 Tim. 3:16; John 17:17; 2 Pet. 1:21; Rom. 10:14; 2 Tim. 3:17; Ps. 119:105.
