# Salvation

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

Chapell, theology sections F ("Salvation Accomplished") and G ("Salvation Applied"). Standards: **WCF 10-18; WLC 57-60, 67-68, 70-81, 153; WSC 29-36, 85-87.** The **Tier** column ranks each question (1 = know cold, 2 = second pass, 3 = if time allows). Chapell's first two questions here are answered on [Core Doctrines and Proofs](core-doctrines.md).

## F. Salvation Accomplished (WCF 10-13)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the ordo salutis? Support your answer from Scripture. | The **logical order** in which the Spirit applies redemption to the elect: election, effectual calling, regeneration, faith and repentance, justification, adoption, sanctification, perseverance, glorification. Rom. 8:29-30 is the skeleton. See [Core Doctrines and Proofs](core-doctrines.md). |
| 1 | How would you explain the plan of salvation to an unbeliever? | **God** is holy and made us for himself; we are **sinners**, under his judgment; **Christ** died for sinners and rose; we must **repent and believe**. See the gospel outline on [Core Doctrines and Proofs](core-doctrines.md). |
| 1 | Define and defend (include Scripture proofs) the doctrine of "effectual calling." | WSC 31: "Effectual calling is the work of God's Spirit, whereby, convincing us of our sin and misery, enlightening our minds in the knowledge of Christ, and renewing our wills, he doth persuade and enable us to embrace Jesus Christ, freely offered to us in the gospel." It is the Spirit's **inward, powerful** call, which accompanies the outward gospel call and always succeeds (WCF 10.1). Rom. 8:30; 1 Cor. 1:9, 23-24; 2 Tim. 1:9; Acts 16:14; John 6:44; 10:3, 27; Eph. 1:18-19. It does not coerce; it **changes the heart** so that the sinner freely and gladly comes (Ezek. 36:26-27; Ps. 110:3). Contrast the **general call**, which many hear but reject (Matt. 22:14). |
| 1 | On what is God's decision to call a certain person based? | **Solely on his free and special grace and love**, "not from anything at all foreseen in man" (10.2) (WCF 10.1-2): not on foreseen faith, merit, or readiness. Rom. 9:11-16; Eph. 1:4-5; 2 Tim. 1:9; Deut. 7:7-8; 1 Cor. 1:26-29. |
| 2 | How can infants be "called"? | WCF 10.3: **elect infants dying in infancy** are regenerated and saved by Christ through the Spirit, "who worketh when, and where, and how he pleaseth," as are other elect persons incapable of being outwardly called. John 3:8; Luke 1:15, 41-44; Ps. 22:9-10; 2 Sam. 12:23. The Spirit's regeneration does not depend on the hearing of words. For covenant children, see also Acts 2:39. |
| 1 | Can anyone not called by the Spirit be saved? Explain and defend (include Scripture proofs). | **No** (WCF 10.4): without the Spirit's call there is no saving faith. John 6:44, 65; 1 Cor. 2:14; Rom. 10:14-17; Eph. 2:1, 8-9. (Exception: elect infants and others incapable of external calling, WCF 10.3.) |
| 1 | Is Jesus really the only way of salvation? Explain and defend (include Scripture proofs). | **Yes.** John 14:6 ("I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me"); Acts 4:12 ("there is no other name under heaven given among men by which we must be saved"); 1 Tim. 2:5; 1 John 5:11-12; John 3:18, 36. Neither the light of nature nor religious sincerity can save (WCF 10.4). |
| 2 | What about those who never had the chance to hear about Jesus? | All have **general revelation**, and it leaves them **without excuse** and under condemnation (Rom. 1:18-21; 2:12-16); it does not give saving knowledge. Faith comes by hearing (Rom. 10:13-17), which is why Christ commands **missions** (Matt. 28:19-20). We do not know every way God works (the elect who die in infancy; Deut. 29:29), but he is just (Gen. 18:25), and Scripture gives **no hope of salvation apart from Christ**. Beware speculation; answer with humility and urgency. |
| 2 | What about the sincere Buddhist (or Muslim, or Unitarian)? | **Sincerity does not save**: the object of faith matters. Rom. 10:2-3 ("a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge"); John 5:23 (whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father); 1 John 2:22-23; 2 John 9; Acts 4:12. The gospel call goes to them in love: they need repentance and faith in the Christ of Scripture. |
| 1 | Define and defend (include Scripture proofs) the doctrine of "justification." | WSC 33: "Justification is an act of God's free grace, wherein he pardoneth all our sins, and accepteth us as righteous in his sight, only for the righteousness of Christ imputed to us, and received by faith alone." It is a **legal declaration** (forensic), not an infusion of righteousness (contra Rome). Rom. 3:21-28; 4:1-8; 5:1, 9, 18-19; Gal. 2:16; Phil. 3:8-9; Titus 3:7. WCF 11.1. |
| 1 | What is the basis of justification? | **Christ's righteousness**, his active and passive obedience, credited (imputed) to the believer and received by faith alone, not our works and not faith itself as a work (WCF 11.1; Rom. 5:19; 2 Cor. 5:21; Phil. 3:9; 1 Cor. 1:30; Jer. 23:6). |
| 1 | Explain this statement: "Faith alone saves, but saving faith is never alone." | **Sola fide**: we are justified by faith alone, as the **instrument** that receives Christ. But the faith that justifies is a **living** faith, never without the other saving graces, and it works through love (WCF 11.2; Gal. 5:6; Eph. 2:8-10; 1 John 3:7-10). **Paul and James:** Paul asks how a sinner is declared righteous before God (by faith, apart from works, Rom. 3:28); James asks what kind of faith is genuine (a faith that shows itself in works, James 2:14-26). |
| 1 | When is a Christian justified? | At the moment he **first truly believes**, once for all (WCF 11.4; Rom. 5:1; 8:1; John 5:24; Acts 13:39). God decreed from eternity to justify the elect and Christ died and rose for them, "nevertheless they are not justified, until the Holy Spirit doth, in due time, actually apply Christ unto them" (WCF 11.4). Our justification will be publicly **declared** at the last judgment (Matt. 12:36-37; Rom. 2:13, 16). |
| 1 | What happens when a Christian sins after being justified? | He **remains justified** (Rom. 8:1, 33-34; 1 John 2:1-2), but falls under his Father's **fatherly displeasure** and may lose the sense of God's favor until he repents (WCF 11.5; Ps. 89:30-33; 51:12; Heb. 12:5-11; 1 John 1:9). Justification is not repeated; **fellowship** is restored. |
| 1 | How were believers justified under the old covenant? | The **same way**: by faith in the coming Messiah (WCF 11.6; Gen. 15:6; Rom. 4:1-8 [Abraham and David]; Hab. 2:4; Heb. 11). |
| 1 | Define and defend (include Scripture proofs) the doctrine of "adoption." | WSC 34: "Adoption is an act of God's free grace, whereby we are received into the number, and have a right to all the privileges of the sons of God." John 1:12; Rom. 8:14-17; Gal. 4:4-7; Eph. 1:5; 1 John 3:1. Privileges: God's name, the Spirit of adoption, access to the throne of grace, his pity, protection, and provision, and fatherly discipline; never cast off; heirs of eternal life (WCF 12). Distinguish from Christ's **natural** sonship. |
| 1 | Define and defend (include Scripture proofs) the doctrine of "sanctification." | WSC 35: "Sanctification is the work of God's free grace, whereby we are renewed in the whole man after the image of God, and are enabled more and more to die unto sin, and live unto righteousness." **Definitive** (set apart once for all: 1 Cor. 6:11; Acts 20:32) and **progressive** (a lifelong growth: 2 Cor. 3:18; Rom. 6:1-14; 8:13; Phil. 2:12-13; 1 Thess. 5:23; Heb. 12:14; John 17:17). WCF 13.1-3. **Compare justification:** justification is a declaration, once for all, and complete; sanctification is a work within us, lifelong, and incomplete. |
| 1 | Can anyone become perfect (completely sanctified) in this life? Defend (include Scripture proofs). | **No** (WCF 13.2; WSC 82): "There abideth still some remnants of corruption in every part," so there is a continual war between flesh and Spirit. 1 John 1:8-10; Phil. 3:12-14; Rom. 7:14-25; Gal. 5:17; Prov. 20:9; Eccl. 7:20; James 3:2. Errors: **Wesleyan** "Christian perfection," **Keswick** higher-life teaching, and Pelagianism. Perfection comes at glorification (1 John 3:2; Heb. 12:23). |

## G. Salvation Applied (WCF 14-18)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is saving faith? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | WSC 86: "Faith in Jesus Christ is a saving grace, whereby we receive and rest upon him alone for salvation, as he is offered to us in the gospel." It involves **knowledge** (of the gospel), **assent** (to its truth), and **trust** (personal reliance on Christ): *notitia*, *assensus*, *fiducia*. John 1:12; Acts 16:31; Gal. 2:16; Phil. 3:9; Heb. 11:1; WCF 14.2. |
| 1 | Where does saving faith come from? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | It is **the gift of God**, the work of the Holy Spirit in the heart, ordinarily wrought by the **ministry of the Word** (WCF 14.1). Eph. 2:8-9; Phil. 1:29; John 6:44, 65; Acts 18:27; Rom. 10:17; 2 Pet. 1:1. |
| 1 | What does saving faith believe? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | The whole Word of God as true, but its **principal acts** are "accepting, receiving, and resting upon Christ alone for justification, sanctification, and eternal life" (WCF 14.2). It trusts the gospel promises and Christ's person and work, his deity, atonement, and resurrection: Rom. 10:9-10; 1 Cor. 15:1-4; John 20:31; Heb. 11:6. |
| 2 | Is all faith alike? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **Same in kind, different in degree**: faith may be weak or strong, and may be assailed and shaken, yet it gets the victory (WCF 14.3). Matt. 8:10; 14:31; Mark 9:24; Luke 17:5; Rom. 4:19-20; 14:1. Distinguish from **false** or **temporary** faith, which has no root and does not persevere (Matt. 13:20-21; Acts 8:13, 20-23; James 2:19). |
| 1 | Define "repentance." Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | WSC 87: "Repentance unto life is a saving grace, whereby a sinner, out of a true sense of his sin, and apprehension of the mercy of God in Christ, doth, with grief and hatred of his sin, turn from it unto God, with full purpose of, and endeavour after, new obedience." Acts 11:18; 2 Cor. 7:9-11; Joel 2:12-13; Acts 2:38; 20:21. It involves a change of mind, sorrow for sin, and a turning to God. |
| 1 | In what sense is repentance "necessary?" Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | It is **required of everyone** (Luke 13:3, 5; Acts 17:30; 2 Pet. 3:9) and, though not a satisfaction for sin or the ground of pardon, "none may expect pardon without it" (WCF 15.3). It is a **gift of God** (Acts 5:31; 2 Tim. 2:25) and is a **continuing** habit of the Christian's life (Luke 17:3-4; 1 John 1:9; Rev. 2:5). |
| 2 | How ought we to confess our sins? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | To **God**, specifically, humbly, and with sorrow and forsaking of sin (Ps. 32:5; 51; Prov. 28:13; 1 John 1:9; WCF 15.5-6); to **those we have wronged**, seeking reconciliation (Matt. 5:23-24; Luke 19:8; James 5:16); and **publicly** where the sin has been public, so that offended persons may be reconciled (WCF 15.6). No priest is needed (1 Tim. 2:5). |
| 1 | What are good works? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | Works "as God hath commanded in his holy Word," not those "devised by men out of blind zeal" (WCF 16.1), done from **faith**, in **obedience** to God's commands, for his **glory** (1 Cor. 10:31; Eph. 2:10; Matt. 5:16; Titus 2:14; 3:8; Mic. 6:8; Heb. 11:6). |
| 1 | What is the relation of good works to faith? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | They are the **fruits and evidences** of a true and lively faith (WCF 16.2). James 2:17-18, 26; Gal. 5:6; Eph. 2:10; Matt. 7:16-20; 1 John 2:3-6. They do not **produce** faith or justification; faith produces them. |
| 1 | Are good works necessary for salvation? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **Not** as the **ground** of justification (Rom. 3:28; Gal. 2:16; Eph. 2:8-9); **yes**, as the necessary **fruit and evidence** of true faith, and as the path God ordains for the saved (Eph. 2:10; Heb. 12:14; Matt. 7:21; James 2:17). Necessary as a consequence, not as a cause. |
| 1 | Can our good works save us? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **No**: our best works are imperfect and defiled (Isa. 64:6), we cannot merit God's pardon or reward (Luke 17:10; Rom. 4:4-5; WCF 16.5); we are saved by grace (Eph. 2:8-9; Titus 3:5). Yet God graciously accepts the good works of believers **in Christ** (WCF 16.6; Heb. 13:20-21; 1 Pet. 2:5). |
| 2 | Can unbelievers do good works? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | They may do what is **outwardly good and useful** (WCF 16.7; 2 Kings 10:30; Luke 6:33; Rom. 2:14), but their works are **not truly good** because they do not come from a heart purified by faith, are not done in the right way, according to the Word, nor to the right end, the glory of God. Heb. 11:6; Rom. 14:23; Titus 1:15; Matt. 7:17-18. Yet neglecting them is **more sinful** and displeasing to God (WCF 16.7). |
| 1 | Define and defend (include Scripture proofs) the doctrine of "perseverance." | Those whom God has accepted in Christ, effectually called and sanctified by his Spirit, **can neither totally nor finally fall away** from the state of grace, but will certainly persevere to the end (WCF 17.1). John 10:27-29; Rom. 8:29-39; Phil. 1:6; 1 Pet. 1:5; Jude 24; 1 John 2:19. |
| 1 | Can a saved person later be eternally lost? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **No**: true believers are kept (John 6:39; 10:28-29; Rom. 8:38-39; 1 Pet. 1:5). Those who finally fall away show they **never truly belonged** (1 John 2:19; Matt. 7:22-23; John 6:66-71). Believers can fall into **grievous sins** for a time (David, Peter; WCF 17.3) and God disciplines them. The **warnings** (Heb. 6:4-9; 10:26-31; 1 Cor. 10:12) are the means by which God preserves his people. Arminian view: believers may lose their salvation. |
| 1 | On what does a believer's persevering depend? Defend with Scripture proofs. | Not on his own free will but on **God**: the **unchangeable decree of election**, the **efficacy of Christ's merit and intercession**, the **abiding of the Spirit and of the seed of God** within him, and the **nature of the covenant of grace** (WCF 17.2). Rom. 8:30-34; John 17:11, 24; Heb. 7:25; 1 John 3:9; Jer. 32:40. Believers persevere **by** faith and diligence (Heb. 3:14; 2 Pet. 1:10), but those are the means by which God keeps them. |
| 1 | Can a person be sure he is saved? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **Yes**: true believers may be **infallibly assured** (WCF 18.1-3). 1 John 5:13 ("that you may know that you have eternal life"); Rom. 8:16; 2 Pet. 1:10; Heb. 6:11, 18-19; 1 Thess. 1:4-5. Assurance is **not of the essence of faith**; a true believer may wait long for it and have it shaken (18.3-4). |
| 1 | Should a person be sure he is saved? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **Yes**: it is the believer's **duty** to give diligence to make his calling and election sure (2 Pet. 1:10; WCF 18.3), so that his heart may be enlarged in peace, joy, love, gratitude, and obedience. False assurance (Matt. 7:21-23; Rev. 3:1) is **presumption**, which the Word warns against (WCF 18.1). |
| 1 | How could a person know he is saved? On what could such assurance be based? | WCF 18.2: assurance is founded upon (1) **the divine truth of the promises of salvation**, (2) the **inward evidence of those graces** to which the promises are made (1 John 2:3; 3:14, 19; 2 Pet. 1:5-11), and (3) the **testimony of the Spirit** witnessing with our spirit that we are God's children (Rom. 8:15-16). |
| 1 | What should a person do who lacks assurance of salvation? | **Examine himself** (2 Cor. 13:5; Ps. 139:23-24), **look to Christ and the promises**, not to feelings (Heb. 12:2; John 6:37), use the **means of grace** (Word, prayer, sacraments, fellowship), **repent** of known sin, give diligence in holiness (2 Pet. 1:5-11), and seek **pastoral counsel**. He need not wait for extraordinary revelation (WCF 18.3). True believers may be assailed by doubts but are never utterly destitute of the seed of God (18.4; Ps. 77; 42:11). |
