# Theology

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

This section is built around Bryan Chapell and Jim Meek, ***Preparing for Licensure and Ordination Exams*** (PCA Christian Education and Publications, 2002), section II, "Study Questions on Theology." Chapell's theology questions **follow the order of the Westminster Confession of Faith** and are largely drawn from it, so this guide does too: each page answers one or more of his lettered sections (A-L) in a short, crisp form with Scripture proofs and references to the Confession (WCF) and the Larger and Shorter Catechisms (WLC, WSC).

These are study answers, not an official position of the PCA. Where Reformed Christians differ and the Standards leave room (the length of the days of creation, the millennium, cessationism, and so on), the answer says so: **know your own view and be ready to defend it.**

## What the PCA Requires

| | Licensure (BCO 19-2) | Ordination (BCO 21-4) |
|---|---|---|
| **Theology** | "Basic knowledge of biblical doctrine as outlined in the Confession of Faith and Larger and Shorter Catechisms" | "Theology" (and "Sacraments," examined separately: see the [Sacraments](../sacraments/index.md) tab) |

Chapell opens the section with two questions to answer honestly and plainly:

- *Have you read all of the Westminster Confession of Faith and the Larger and Shorter Catechisms?*
- *Are you aware of any of its teachings about which you have reservations, or to which you take exception? Explain.*

Read the Standards straight through, with the Scripture proofs, before the exam. Keep a list of any point where you hesitate, find out whether your presbytery treats it as a stated exception, and be ready to say exactly what you hold and why.

## How Chapell Says to Prepare

- **Answer from memory, crisply.** Presbyteries want short, objective answers, not term-paper essays. Aim for one clear sentence, then a proof text, then detail only if asked.
- **Have the "Doctrines and Proofs" down pat.** Chapell says these come up so often that you should be able to give them with reference *and* full quotation: the **Five Points**, the **Ordo Salutis**, and a **Gospel outline**. See [Core Doctrines and Proofs](core-doctrines.md).
- **Memorize designated Shorter Catechism answers** (table below).
- **Know the hot topics** and be ready to state and defend your view from Scripture, the Standards, and Assembly decisions (table below).

## If Time Is Short: Study in Tiers

Each question on these pages carries a **Tier** number in its first column. Chapell does not rank the theology questions, except to say that the Doctrines and Proofs are so frequently asked that you should have them "down pat." The tiers below are a study judgment: what comes up most, and what the rest builds on.

!!! success "Tier 1: know cold (the foundations; most likely to be asked)"
    The **Five Points**, the **ordo salutis**, and a **gospel outline** with proofs; the **doctrine of Scripture** (inspiration, authority, sufficiency, inerrancy, canon); the **Trinity**, the **attributes** and **decrees** of God, **creation** and **providence**; **sin** and **original sin**; the **covenants** of works and grace; the **person and work of Christ**; **effectual calling, justification, adoption, sanctification**; **faith, repentance, perseverance, assurance**; the **law and Christian liberty**; the **regulative principle** and the **Sabbath**; the **marks of the church** and Presbyterian government; the **last things**; and the **sacraments** (baptism and the Lord's Supper).

!!! warning "Tier 2: second pass"
    Reformed-versus-Arminian comparisons; the finer points on the decrees (supralapsarianism, Amyraldianism); theodicy; the early **Christological heresies**; the covenants with Noah and Moses; **hot-topic positions** and their pros and cons; the Holy Spirit's work and spiritual gifts; the main views of the millennium; answers to Mormons and Jehovah's Witnesses; and the practical cases of baptism and communion.

!!! note "Tier 3: if time allows"
    The origin of the soul, the *extra Calvinisticum*, and other specialized questions that are asked less often.

### Method

- **Define, prove, apply.** For every doctrine: a one-sentence definition, one or two proof texts, and one sentence of application.
- **Know the opposing view.** Chapell's questions keep asking "distinguish X from Y" (Reformed and Arminian, Lutheran and Zwinglian). Learn the error alongside the truth.
- **Say it out loud.** Exams are usually oral.
- **Quote the Standards.** Learn the Shorter Catechism answers above word for word; their wording is exactly what examiners listen for.
- **Overlap with the Bible exam:** the [NT Key Topics](../bible/new-testament/topics.md) page doubles as a proof-text list for these answers.

*The Westminster Standards are in the public domain and are summarized here in the writer's own words, with direct quotation of only a few Shorter Catechism answers. Scripture references are from the ESV where quoted.*
