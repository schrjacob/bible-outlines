# Covenants and Christ

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

Chapell, theology section E, "God's Way of Salvation." Standards: **WCF 7-8; WLC 20, 30-56; WSC 12, 20-28.** The **Tier** column ranks each question (1 = know cold, 2 = second pass, 3 = if time allows).

## The Covenants

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is a covenant? | A **solemn, binding relationship** that God sovereignly establishes with his people, with **promises, obligations, and sanctions** (blessings and curses), often sealed by a sign (Gen. 15; 17; Ex. 24; Jer. 31:31-34). The Confession notes that the distance between God and creatures is so great that they could have no fruition of him "but by some voluntary condescension on God's part," which he has expressed by way of covenant (WCF 7.1). |
| 1 | What is the Covenant of Works (Life)? Who are its parties? | WSC 12: God entered into a **covenant of life** with Adam, promising life on condition of perfect obedience and forbidding the tree of the knowledge of good and evil on pain of death. **Parties:** God and **Adam as the representative of all humanity** (WCF 7.2; 19.1). Gen. 2:15-17; Hos. 6:7 (some translations "like Adam they transgressed the covenant"); Rom. 5:12-19; Rom. 10:5; Gal. 3:12. Adam broke it. |
| 2 | Is there a present validity to the Covenant of Works (Life)? What is it? | It is **no longer a way of salvation** for fallen sinners (Gal. 2:16; 3:10-12), but its **demands remain** for everyone who is not in Christ: the law's requirement of perfect obedience stands, and its penalty falls on all in Adam (Rom. 5:12; 3:19-20; John 3:18, 36). **Christ, the last Adam, fulfilled it** for his people by perfect obedience and by bearing its curse (Rom. 5:18-19; Gal. 4:4-5; 3:13), and so it is the foundation of the active and passive obedience of Christ. |
| 2 | What is the Covenant of Redemption? Who are its parties? | The **eternal agreement within the Trinity** to save the elect: the **Father** appoints and sends the Son, giving him a people; the **Son** agrees to become man, keep the law, and die for them; the **Holy Spirit** applies the work. John 6:38-40; 17:2, 4-6, 9, 24; Eph. 1:3-14; Ps. 2:7-9; Ps. 40:6-8 with Heb. 10:5-10; Isa. 53:10-12; Titus 1:2; 2 Tim. 1:9. Implied in WCF 8.1; WLC 31. |
| 1 | Discuss the Covenant of Grace. | After the fall, God made a second covenant to save sinners: he **freely offers life and salvation** through Jesus Christ, requires faith in him, and promises the Holy Spirit to all who are ordained to life (WCF 7.3). Promised first in **Gen. 3:15**; made with Christ as the second Adam and in him with the elect (WLC 31); one covenant throughout history, **administered differently** under the law (promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, Passover) and under the gospel (the Word and the sacraments of baptism and the Lord's Supper) (WCF 7.5-6). Gen. 17:7; Jer. 31:31-34; Heb. 8-10; Rom. 4. |
| 1 | How is the Covenant of Grace related to the Abrahamic Covenant? | The Abrahamic covenant (Gen. 12; 15; 17) is a **principal administration of the covenant of grace**: God promises to be "God to you and to your offspring" (Gen. 17:7), with the **sign of circumcision**, which Paul calls "a seal of the righteousness that he had by faith" (Rom. 4:11). It is fulfilled in **Christ**, the offspring of Abraham (Gal. 3:16), and all who believe are Abraham's children (Gal. 3:7-9, 29). **Baptism** replaces circumcision as the sign (Col. 2:11-12), which is the basis of infant baptism. |
| 2 | How is the Covenant of Grace related to the Mosaic Covenant? | The Mosaic (Sinai) covenant is another **administration of the covenant of grace** (redemption first, then law: Ex. 20:2), with the **law, tabernacle, and sacrifices** pointing to Christ (Heb. 8-10) and the nation's temporal blessings tied to its obedience (Deut. 28). It **did not annul** the Abrahamic promise (Gal. 3:17-18); the law was a **guardian** until Christ came (Gal. 3:23-25). The moral law continues; the ceremonial law is fulfilled in Christ; the civil law expired with the nation (WCF 19). Some Reformed writers see an element of the covenant of works "republished" at Sinai; know the debate. |
| 2 | How is the Covenant of Grace related to the Noahic Covenant? | The Noahic covenant (Gen. 8:20-9:17) is a covenant of **preservation** with all creation: God promises never again to destroy the world by flood, with the rainbow as its sign; it is the **common-grace** setting in which redemption unfolds. It is distinct from the covenant of grace in its scope (all creatures) but upholds it by preserving the world until Christ's return (Gen. 6:18; 8:22; 2 Pet. 3:5-13). |
| 1 | Discuss the relationship between the Old and New Covenants. Defend from Scripture. | **Continuity:** one covenant of grace, one Mediator (Christ), one way of salvation (grace through faith), one people of God (WCF 7.5-6; Rom. 4; Gal. 3:6-9; Heb. 11). **Difference:** the OT administration used types and shadows; the NT gives the substance, fuller light, and greater power, extended to all nations (Heb. 8:6-13; 10:1; Col. 2:16-17; 2 Cor. 3:7-11; Jer. 31:31-34; Acts 2). The "old" covenant is **not abolished but fulfilled**. |

## The Person of Christ

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain and defend (with Scripture proofs) the orthodox doctrine of the person of Christ. | Jesus Christ is **one person with two natures**, truly God and truly man, "without confusion, change, division, or separation" (Council of Chalcedon, 451; WCF 8.2; WSC 21: "being the eternal Son of God, became man, and so was, and continueth to be, God and man, in two distinct natures, and one person, for ever"). **God:** John 1:1-3, 14; 8:58; 20:28; Rom. 9:5; Phil. 2:6; Col. 1:15-20; 2:9; Titus 2:13; Heb. 1:1-3. **Man:** Luke 2:52; John 4:6; 11:35; Heb. 2:14-17; 4:15; 1 Tim. 2:5. |
| 2 | Was Christ a human person? | Christ is **one person, the eternal Son of God**, who assumed a **human nature** (not a second, human person). He is fully human in body, mind, and will, but the personal subject is the Son. Saying that he is two persons is **Nestorianism**. |
| 2 | Does Christ have a soul? | **Yes**: a **true body and a reasonable soul** (WSC 22). Matt. 26:38 ("My soul is very sorrowful"); Luke 23:46; John 12:27; Heb. 2:14-17. Denying it is **Apollinarianism**. |
| 2 | What is kenosis? | From Phil. 2:7: Christ "**emptied himself**, by taking the form of a servant." Orthodox view: he emptied himself by **taking on humanity and humiliation**, setting aside his glory (John 17:5) and the use of his prerogatives, not by ceasing to be God. Heretical **kenotic theories** say he gave up some divine attributes. |
| 2 | Did Christ lay aside any of his divine attributes at the incarnation? | **No**: he remained **fully God** in every attribute (Col. 2:9; John 1:14; Heb. 13:8). He did, however, **veil his glory** and live in humble circumstances, and in his human nature he was limited (Luke 2:52; Mark 13:32; John 4:6). What his human nature did not know or do, the one person is rightly said not to know or do (the **communication of properties**). |
| 2 | Briefly explain and defend against the challenges to orthodox Christology posed by: Docetism, Arius, Nestorius, Apollinarius, Eutyches. | **Docetism:** Christ only seemed to be human (answered by 1 John 4:2-3; Luke 24:39; Heb. 2:14). **Arius:** the Son is a created being, not eternal (Council of **Nicea**, 325; John 1:1-3; Col. 1:16-17; John 8:58; Isa. 9:6). **Apollinarius:** the Logos took the place of a human mind (Council of **Constantinople**, 381; Heb. 2:17; Matt. 26:38; Luke 2:52). **Nestorius:** two persons loosely joined (Council of **Ephesus**, 431). **Eutyches:** the two natures merged into one (Council of **Chalcedon**, 451). |
| 2 | Are any of the early heresies regarding the natures of Christ held today? If so, by whom? | Yes: **Arianism** among Jehovah's Witnesses (Jesus is a created being), and in Mormonism, where Jesus is a created spirit-brother of Lucifer; **docetism** in some Gnostic and New Age teaching, and in Christian Science; **adoptionism** and the denial of the deity in liberal Protestantism; and a **practical Eutychianism or Apollinarianism** in popular piety that treats Jesus as God "walking around" with no real human limits. **Modalism** (a Trinitarian heresy) is held by Oneness Pentecostals. |
| 2 | Explain and defend the doctrine of the "communication of properties." | Because both natures belong to the **one person**, what is true of either nature may be said of the person: "the Lord of glory" was crucified (1 Cor. 2:8); God purchased the church "with his own blood" (Acts 20:28); the Son of Man "descended from heaven" and is "in heaven" (John 3:13). The natures are not mixed; each keeps its own properties (WCF 8.2, 8.7). |
| 3 | What is the "extra Calvinisticum"? | The Reformed teaching that the **eternal Son**, though fully united to the human nature, is **not confined to it**: he continued to fill, uphold, and govern all things even in the incarnation (John 3:13; Col. 1:17; Heb. 1:3; Ps. 139:7-10). Lutherans, who hold that Christ's body can be everywhere, reject it; it underlies the difference in the doctrine of the Lord's Supper. |
| 1 | Define the following names: Jesus, Christ, Lord, Son of Man, Son of God, Lamb of God. | **Jesus** ("the LORD saves," Matt. 1:21); **Christ** (Greek for the Hebrew *Messiah*, "anointed one," as prophet, priest, and king, Acts 2:36; Dan. 9:25); **Lord** (Greek *kyrios*, used for *Yahweh* in the Greek OT, Rom. 10:9; Phil. 2:9-11; Acts 2:36); **Son of Man** (Jesus' favorite self-title: Dan. 7:13-14, the heavenly king, and also the suffering servant, Mark 10:45); **Son of God** (the eternal Son, John 1:18; 20:31; and the messianic king, Ps. 2:7; Matt. 16:16; Rom. 1:4); **Lamb of God** (the sacrifice for sin, John 1:29; Isa. 53:7; 1 Pet. 1:19; Rev. 5:6-12). |
| 1 | How was Christ born? | **Conceived by the Holy Spirit in the womb of the virgin Mary, and born of her, yet without sin** (WSC 22; Luke 1:26-38; Matt. 1:18-25; Gal. 4:4). Born in Bethlehem (Mic. 5:2; Luke 2:1-7). |
| 1 | Explain and defend (including Scripture proofs) the Virgin Birth. | Jesus was conceived by the **Holy Spirit without a human father**. **Isa. 7:14** (the virgin shall conceive); **Matt. 1:18-25** (quotes Isa. 7:14); **Luke 1:34-35**. It matters because it shows his **deity** (Luke 1:35 "the Son of God") and the **sinlessness** of his human nature, which did not descend from Adam by ordinary generation (WSC 22). It also shows that salvation is **God's work**, not man's. The two Gospels that record his birth both affirm it. |
| 2 | Trace revelation of the person and work of Christ from the beginning of the Old Testament. | **Gen. 3:15** (the woman's offspring); the promise to **Abraham** (Gen. 12:3; 22:18; Gal. 3:16); **Judah's scepter** (Gen. 49:10); the **Passover lamb** (Ex. 12); the **prophet like Moses** (Deut. 18:15-18); the **tabernacle, priesthood, and sacrifices** (Lev.; Heb. 9-10); **Melchizedek** (Gen. 14; Ps. 110; Heb. 7); **David's son and king** (2 Sam. 7; Ps. 2; 110); the **suffering Servant** (Isa. 52:13-53:12); the **child to be born** (Isa. 7:14; 9:6-7); **Bethlehem** (Mic. 5:2); **Daniel's Son of Man** (Dan. 7:13-14); the Lord's **messenger** (Mal. 3:1). Jesus himself said all the Scriptures speak of him (Luke 24:25-27, 44; John 5:39, 46). |

## The Work of Christ

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the humiliation of Christ? | WSC 27: "Christ's humiliation consisted in his being born, and that in a low condition, made under the law, undergoing the miseries of this life, the wrath of God, and the cursed death of the cross; in being buried, and continuing under the power of death for a time." Phil. 2:6-8; 2 Cor. 8:9; Gal. 3:13; 4:4. |
| 1 | Define and distinguish the active and passive obedience of Christ. | **Active obedience:** his perfect keeping of the law for us in his life, whose righteousness is credited to us (Matt. 3:15; Rom. 5:19; 2 Cor. 5:21; Gal. 4:4-5; Heb. 4:15). **Passive obedience:** his suffering of the penalty of the law in our place, culminating in his death (Isa. 53:4-6; Gal. 3:13; Phil. 2:8; 1 Pet. 2:24). ("Passive" is from the Latin *pati*, to suffer; his whole life was obedient and his death was a voluntary act.) Together they are the ground of our justification. |
| 1 | What happened in the resurrection of Christ? | On the third day he **rose bodily** from the dead, with the same body now glorified (Luke 24:39-43; John 20:24-29), the tomb was empty, and he appeared to many (1 Cor. 15:3-8). It **vindicates** him (Rom. 1:4), **confirms** that his sacrifice was accepted (Rom. 4:25), is the **firstfruits** of our resurrection (1 Cor. 15:20-23), and began his **exaltation** (WSC 28). |
| 2 | Discuss "glorified body." | The risen body is **a real, physical body**, now perfected: imperishable, glorious, powerful, and "spiritual," meaning wholly under the Spirit's control (1 Cor. 15:42-49; Phil. 3:21), not immaterial. Christ's resurrection body is both continuous with his earthly one (the wounds; eating, Luke 24:42-43) and transformed (appearing and disappearing). Believers will have a body like his (Phil. 3:21; 1 John 3:2). |
| 1 | What are the offices of Christ? | **Prophet, priest, and king** (WSC 23-26; WCF 8.1). **Prophet:** Deut. 18:15; Heb. 1:1-2; John 1:18. **Priest:** Ps. 110:4; Heb. 4:14-5:10; 7:25-27. **King:** Ps. 2; 110:1; Luke 1:32-33; Rev. 19:16. |
| 1 | How does Christ execute the offices? | **Prophet:** reveals the will of God for our salvation by his Word and Spirit (John 15:15; 1 Pet. 1:10-11). **Priest:** offered himself once as a sacrifice to satisfy divine justice and reconcile us to God, and makes continual **intercession** for us (Heb. 7:25-27; 9:24-28; Rom. 8:34). **King:** subdues us to himself, rules and defends us, and restrains and conquers his and our enemies (Ps. 110; 1 Cor. 15:25; Eph. 1:20-23). |
| 1 | Who is the only redeemer of God's elect? | WSC 21: "The only Redeemer of God's elect is the Lord Jesus Christ, who, being the eternal Son of God, became man, and so was, and continueth to be, God and man, in two distinct natures, and one person, for ever." Acts 4:12; 1 Tim. 2:5; John 14:6. |
| 2 | What is a redeemer? | One who **buys back** by paying a price: delivering captives or debtors from bondage by a ransom. In Israel, the **kinsman-redeemer** (a near relative, *go'el*) bought back lost property, freed a relative, and avenged him (Lev. 25:25, 47-49; Ruth 3-4; Job 19:25). Christ became our kinsman and paid the ransom with his blood (Mark 10:45; Eph. 1:7; 1 Pet. 1:18-19; Gal. 3:13). |
| 1 | What is the atonement? | Christ's **sacrificial death** by which he satisfied God's justice, bore the penalty of sin, and reconciled sinners to God (Rom. 3:21-26; Isa. 53; Heb. 9:11-28; 1 John 2:2; 4:10). Literally "at-one-ment." |
| 2 | Was the atonement necessary? Explain and defend (include Scripture proofs). | It was necessary **if God was to save sinners and still be just**: "so that he might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus" (Rom. 3:26). Heb. 9:22 (without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness); Gal. 2:21 ("if righteousness were through the law, then Christ died for no purpose"); 3:21; Matt. 26:39 (Gethsemane). Reformed theologians have differed on whether it was **absolutely necessary** (God's justice requires satisfaction) or only **necessary on the supposition of God's decree to save**. |
| 1 | Why isn't a good life enough to gain salvation? | God's standard is **perfection** (Matt. 5:48; James 2:10; Gal. 3:10); past sins have already incurred a **debt** that future good cannot pay (Rom. 3:19-20, 23); our best works are defiled (Isa. 64:6); salvation is **by grace, not works** (Eph. 2:8-9; Gal. 2:16, 21; Titus 3:5). |
| 1 | Define: expiation. | The **removal of guilt**: sin is covered and taken away by sacrifice (Lev. 16; Heb. 9:26; 10:4, 11-12; Ps. 103:12). |
| 1 | Define: propitiation. | The **turning aside of God's wrath** by a sacrifice that satisfies his justice (Rom. 3:25; Heb. 2:17; 1 John 2:2; 4:10). God himself provides it. |
| 1 | Define: reconciliation. | The **restoration of peace** between God and sinners, ending enmity (Rom. 5:10-11; 2 Cor. 5:18-21; Col. 1:20-22; Eph. 2:14-16). |
| 1 | Define: redemption. | **Buying back** from slavery by the payment of a ransom (Mark 10:45; Eph. 1:7; 1 Pet. 1:18-19; Gal. 3:13; Titus 2:14). |
| 1 | Define: imputation. | **Crediting to someone's account**: our sin was counted to Christ (2 Cor. 5:21; Isa. 53:6), his righteousness is counted to us (Rom. 4:3-8; 5:19; Phil. 3:9), and Adam's sin is counted to his descendants (Rom. 5:12-19). |
| 1 | What is the nature of the atonement? Discuss "penal substitutionary atonement." | Christ took the place of sinners and **bore the penalty** that their sin deserved: it is **penal** (a punishment, satisfying justice) and **substitutionary** (in our place). **Isa. 53:5-6:** "But he was pierced for our transgressions; he was crushed for our iniquities; upon him was the chastisement that brought us peace, and with his wounds we are healed. All we like sheep have gone astray; we have turned—every one—to his own way; and the LORD has laid on him the iniquity of us all." 2 Cor. 5:21; Gal. 3:13; Rom. 3:25-26; 1 Pet. 2:24; 3:18; Mark 10:45. Other views (moral influence, *Christus Victor*, ransom to Satan, governmental) each hold part of the truth but fail to explain the cross without this one. |
| 2 | What of Christ's work remains to be done? | The work of **atonement is finished** (John 19:30; Heb. 10:12-14). Still to come: the **application** of redemption to the elect (his intercession, Heb. 7:25; the Spirit's work in sanctification), his **return** and the **final judgment**, the **resurrection** of the dead, and the **new heavens and new earth** (1 Cor. 15:24-28; Heb. 9:28; Rev. 21-22). |
| 1 | Were Old Testament believers saved by Christ? Explain and defend (include Scripture proofs). | **Yes**: the same covenant of grace, the same Savior, and the same way: faith in the promised Redeemer (WCF 7.5; 8.6). Gen. 15:6 with **Rom. 4:1-8, 22-25**; Heb. 11; Gal. 3:6-9; John 8:56; 1 Pet. 1:10-12; Rom. 3:25 (God had passed over former sins in his divine forbearance); Heb. 9:15. |
| 1 | Will any for whom Christ died be lost? Explain and defend (include Scripture proofs). | **No**: his death **secured** the salvation of those for whom he died (WCF 8.5; Rom. 8:32-34; John 6:37-39; 10:11, 15, 26-29; Isa. 53:11; Matt. 1:21; Eph. 5:25; Heb. 9:12). He did not make salvation merely possible. Universal-sounding texts (John 3:16; 1 John 2:2; 1 Tim. 2:4-6; 2 Pet. 3:9) speak of **all kinds of people**, the **world** of the elect from every nation, or God's revealed will. The atonement is **sufficient for all, efficient for the elect**, and offered freely to all in the gospel. |
