# The Christian Life

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

Chapell, theology section H. Standards: **WCF 19-20, 22-24; WLC 91-152, 154-196; WSC 39-84, 88-107.** Several of Chapell's "hot topics" appear here (theonomy, regulative principle, Sabbath, civil disobedience, divorce and remarriage). On those, Chapell asks you to state and defend **your own** view; the notes below give the Standards' position and the main alternatives. The **Tier** column ranks each question (1 = know cold, 2 = second pass, 3 = if time allows).

## The Law

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the moral law? | God's **perpetual rule of righteousness**, written on Adam's heart at creation and delivered as the **Ten Commandments** at Sinai (WCF 19.1-2; Ex. 20:1-17; Rom. 2:14-15). It binds everyone forever, both justified persons and others (WCF 19.5; Matt. 5:17-19; Rom. 3:31; James 2:8-12). WSC 40-42. |
| 2 | What laws besides the moral law did God give his people under the old covenant? | **Ceremonial laws** (worship, sacrifices, feasts, purity; types of Christ, now fulfilled and abrogated: Heb. 9-10; Col. 2:16-17; Mark 7:19; Acts 10:9-16; WCF 19.3) and **judicial or civil laws** for Israel as a nation (expired with that state; their "general equity" still binds, WCF 19.4). |
| 1 | Are believers under obligation to the law today? Explain and defend with Scripture. | **Yes, to the moral law, as a rule of life**, not as a covenant of works (WCF 19.5-7). Christ has freed believers from the law's **condemnation** and as a way of justification (Rom. 6:14; 8:1-4; Gal. 3:13), but not from its **authority**: Matt. 5:17-19; Rom. 3:31; 13:8-10; James 2:8-12; 1 John 5:3. The law (a) shows sin and drives us to Christ (Gal. 3:24; Rom. 3:20), (b) restrains evil (1 Tim. 1:8-11), and (c) guides the believer in grateful obedience (Ps. 119; Rom. 7:12, 22). The Spirit enables the obedience (Rom. 8:3-4). |
| 2 | What is "theonomy?" Evaluate it biblically. | **Theonomy** ("God's law"; Rushdoony, Bahnsen: Christian Reconstruction) holds that the OT **civil laws and penal sanctions** still bind civil magistrates today unless the NT clearly modifies them (appealing to Matt. 5:17-19). **Critique:** the Confession says Israel's judicial laws "expired together with the State of that people" and bind only in their "general equity" (WCF 19.4); Israel was a unique theocracy typifying Christ's kingdom; the NT gives the magistrate the "sword" but does not enforce the first table; the church's power is spiritual (John 18:36; 2 Cor. 10:4). It also risks collapsing the **distinction of church and state**. Know the Assembly's study reports and your presbytery's view. **Hot topic: Theonomy.** |
| 1 | Where is the law of God summed up? | In the **Ten Commandments** (Ex. 20:1-17; Deut. 5:6-21), and, in brief, in the **two great commandments**: love God, love your neighbor (Matt. 22:37-40; Rom. 13:8-10; Gal. 5:14). The first four commandments concern our duty to God, the last six our duty to man. WSC 41-42. |
| 1 | Can believers keep God's law? Explain and defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **Not perfectly in this life** (Eccl. 7:20; 1 John 1:8; Rom. 7:18-19; WSC 82: "no mere man since the fall is able in this life perfectly to keep the commandments of God, but doth daily break them in thought, word, and deed"), but by the Spirit they **truly desire and begin to obey** it, and are accepted in Christ despite their imperfection (Rom. 8:3-4; Phil. 2:13; Ezek. 36:27; 1 John 5:3; WCF 16.6; 19.6). |
| 2 | What is at stake in the "Lordship Controversy?" Explain and defend your view. | Whether a person can be saved by receiving Jesus as **Savior** and only later, if at all, as **Lord** ("free grace" view), or whether saving faith **necessarily includes submission** to Christ as Lord and results in a changed life ("Lordship salvation"). The Reformed answer: faith receives and rests on the **whole Christ**, prophet, priest, and king (WCF 14.2; WSC 86); saving faith is not "faith alone" in isolation but is **never alone** (James 2:14-26; Rom. 10:9; Luke 6:46; Titus 2:11-14). Yet justification is **by faith alone, not by obedience**, and believers may be disobedient and weak (Rom. 7). The stakes: the nature of faith, assurance, and discipleship. |
| 1 | What are the "means of grace?" | The outward and ordinary means by which Christ communicates to his people the benefits of redemption: **the Word, the sacraments, and prayer** (WSC 88; WLC 154), with fellowship and discipline. They are effectual only by the **blessing of Christ and the working of the Spirit** (WSC 89: the Word, "especially the preaching of the word," is made "an effectual means of convincing and converting sinners, and of building them up in holiness and comfort, through faith, unto salvation"). Acts 2:42; Rom. 10:17; 1 Pet. 2:2. |

## Christian Liberty and Worship

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is "Christian liberty?" Explain and defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | The freedom Christ purchased for believers (WCF 20.1): **from** the guilt of sin and the condemning wrath of God, the curse of the moral law, the present evil world, bondage to Satan, the dominion of sin, the sting of death, and the **ceremonial law**; **to** free access to God and to obedience "not out of slavish fear but a childlike love and a willing mind." Gal. 5:1; Rom. 8:1; Gal. 3:13; Titus 2:14; Heb. 10:19-22. It is **not license** (Gal. 5:13; 1 Pet. 2:16). |
| 1 | Who or what is Lord of the conscience? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **God alone** (WCF 20.2): he has left the conscience free from the doctrines and commandments of men that are **contrary to his Word, or beside it** in matters of faith or worship. Matt. 15:9; Col. 2:20-23; James 4:12; Rom. 14:4; 1 Cor. 7:23; Acts 4:19; 5:29. |
| 2 | How does Christian liberty bear on the Christian's obligation to the state? | It does **not** exempt believers from lawful civil authority, which God has ordained (WCF 20.4; Rom. 13:1-7; 1 Pet. 2:13-17); but because only God is Lord of the conscience, believers may **not obey the state when it commands what God forbids** (Acts 4:19; 5:29; Dan. 3; 6). |
| 1 | What is the "regulative principle?" Defend it from Scripture. | The acceptable way of worshiping God "is instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men, nor the suggestions of Satan... or any other way not prescribed in the Holy Scripture" (WCF 21.1). **What God commands, we do; what he does not, we do not.** Ex. 20:4-6; Lev. 10:1-3 (Nadab and Abihu); Deut. 12:32; Matt. 15:9; Col. 2:20-23; John 4:23-24. It applies to **elements** of worship; **circumstances** (time, place, order) are governed by Scripture's general rules (WCF 1.6). Debated applications: instruments, hymns versus exclusive psalmody, holy days, drama. **Hot topic: Regulative principle.** Contrast the **normative principle** (whatever Scripture does not forbid). |
| 1 | Who is to be worshiped? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **God alone**: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit; not angels, saints, or any creature (WCF 21.2; Ex. 20:3-5; Matt. 4:10; Rev. 22:8-9; Col. 2:18; John 5:23; Matt. 28:19), and **through Christ alone** as Mediator (John 14:6; 1 Tim. 2:5). |
| 1 | What are the ordinary parts or elements of worship? | WCF 21.3-5: **prayer**, the **reading of Scripture**, **sound preaching and conscientious hearing**, **singing of psalms** (with hymns and spiritual songs, Col. 3:16; Eph. 5:19), and the **administration of the sacraments**. Extraordinary or occasional: religious **oaths and vows**, **solemn fastings**, and **thanksgivings**. Acts 2:42; 1 Tim. 2:1; 4:13; 2 Tim. 4:2; 1 Cor. 11:23-26; 16:1-2. |
| 1 | Are Christians under obligation to keep a Sabbath? Defend with Scripture proofs. | **Yes**: the fourth commandment is a **moral and perpetual** commandment, rooted in creation (Gen. 2:2-3; Ex. 20:8-11; Mark 2:27), changed from the last day to the **first day of the week**, the Lord's Day, in honor of Christ's resurrection (WCF 21.7; Acts 20:7; 1 Cor. 16:2; Rev. 1:10). WSC 57-62. Heb. 4:9-10 (a Sabbath rest remains). Objections (Col. 2:16-17; Rom. 14:5-6) concern the ceremonial sabbaths and festival days. **Hot topic: Sabbath.** |
| 1 | If so, how should the Sabbath be kept? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | The Westminster view (WCF 21.8; WSC 60-61): the whole day is a **holy rest**, from one's worldly employments and recreations (except **works of necessity and mercy**), and the time is spent in **public and private worship**. Isa. 58:13-14; Matt. 12:1-14 (necessity and mercy); Luke 13:10-17; Lev. 23:3; Heb. 10:24-25. |
| 2 | Differentiate between the "Continental" and "Westminster" views of the Sabbath. | **Westminster:** the Sabbath is a **moral** commandment, a creation ordinance binding on all, and the Christian Sabbath is the **whole Lord's Day** kept holy, including cessation from ordinary recreations. **Continental** (Dutch Reformed: Heidelberg Catechism 103; Synod of Dort): the commandment's **ceremonial** aspect is fulfilled in Christ, while its principal and perpetual aim is that people regularly **attend public worship, hear the Word, and rest** for that purpose, with less stress on the rest of the day. Both believe in the Lord's Day worship; they differ on the extent of the rest. |
| 1 | Should a Christian ever take an oath or vow? Explain. Defend with Scripture proofs. | **Yes**, lawfully: oaths in solemn matters, in the name of God, in truth and righteousness, for example in court and at ordination (WCF 22.1-3; Deut. 6:13; 10:20; Heb. 6:16; 2 Cor. 1:23; Matt. 26:63-64). **Vows** to God must be made carefully and kept (WCF 22.5-7; Ps. 76:11; Eccl. 5:4-5; Num. 30:2). Not **rash**, **frivolous**, or in everyday speech (Matt. 5:33-37; James 5:12), not by creatures, nor to do what is unlawful or beyond our power. Marriage vows and ordination vows are examples. |

## The State, Marriage, and Divorce

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What should be our attitude toward civil authorities? Defend with Scripture proofs. | **Honor, obey, and pray** for them: they are ordained by God (WCF 23.1; Rom. 13:1-7; 1 Pet. 2:13-17; 1 Tim. 2:1-2; Titus 3:1; Matt. 22:21). |
| 2 | What is the relationship between church and state? | **Distinct spheres** under Christ. The church's power is **spiritual, ministerial, and declarative** (the keys of the kingdom: word, sacraments, discipline); the state bears the **sword** (Rom. 13:4; Matt. 22:21; John 18:36; Acts 5:29). The civil magistrate may not take upon himself the administration of the Word and sacraments, or the power of the keys, or interfere in matters of faith (WCF 23.3), and synods and councils are not to intermeddle with civil affairs, except by humble petition in extraordinary cases or by advice if asked (WCF 31.4). The magistrate should protect the church and its people and give no denomination preference (WCF 23.3, as amended in American Presbyterianism). |
| 2 | What are the proper duties of civil authorities? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | To **keep the peace and secure justice**, "to punish evildoers and to praise those who do good" (Rom. 13:3-4; 1 Pet. 2:14), to protect the innocent and defend the weak (Ps. 82:3-4; Prov. 31:8-9), to wage **just war** (WCF 23.2; Luke 3:14), and to protect the church and religious liberty (WCF 23.3). |
| 2 | What obligations do believers have to civil authorities? Defend with Scripture proofs. | To **honor** and **pay** them tribute and taxes, to **obey** their lawful commands for conscience's sake, and to **pray** for them (Rom. 13:5-7; Matt. 22:21; 1 Pet. 2:13-17; 1 Tim. 2:1-2; WCF 23.4). When the state commands what God forbids, or forbids what he commands, believers must **obey God rather than men** (Acts 4:19; 5:29; Ex. 1:15-21; Dan. 3; 6), accepting the penalty respectfully. **Hot topic: Civil disobedience.** |
| 1 | By whom and for what purpose was marriage designed? Defend with Scripture proofs. | **Instituted by God** at creation (Gen. 2:18-25; Matt. 19:4-6) for **mutual help** of husband and wife, **increase of mankind** with legitimate offspring and a godly seed, and **preventing uncleanness** (WCF 24.2; Gen. 1:28; Mal. 2:15; 1 Cor. 7:2-5). It is a picture of Christ and the church (Eph. 5:22-33). |
| 1 | Who may (and may not) lawfully be married? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **One man and one woman** (WCF 24.1; Gen. 2:24; Matt. 19:4-6; 1 Cor. 7:2): no polygamy, no same-sex marriage (Rom. 1:26-27; Lev. 18:22). Those capable of giving **consent** (24.3), and "only in the Lord," that is, believers should marry believers (1 Cor. 7:39; 2 Cor. 6:14). Not within the degrees of **kindred or affinity** forbidden in the Word (Lev. 18; WCF 24.4). |
| 2 | What is divorce? | The **legal dissolution of the marriage covenant** (the dissolving of the marriage bond), which God permits only on biblical grounds. God "hates" it (Mal. 2:16) and permitted it for the hardness of men's hearts (Matt. 19:8), but it was not so "from the beginning." |
| 1 | Under what circumstances is divorce permissible? Defend your answer with Scripture. | Two grounds (WCF 24.5-6): **adultery or sexual immorality** (Matt. 5:32; 19:9) and **willful desertion** by an unbelieving spouse that cannot be remedied by the church or civil magistrate (1 Cor. 7:12-16). In either case the innocent party is **free** (not required) to divorce, and reconciliation and forgiveness are always preferred (Hos. 1-3; Matt. 18:21-35). See also [NT Key Topics](../new-testament/topics.md) #18. |
| 2 | Is divorce an option for those abused by their spouses (physically or otherwise)? Defend. | The Standards name only adultery and desertion, so the answer is debated. First, the victim's **safety** is paramount and **separation** for protection is warranted (Prov. 22:3; Ps. 11:5); abuse should be brought to the **church** and, where criminal, to the **civil authorities** (Rom. 13:4), and the abuser called to repentance and **church discipline** (Matt. 18:15-20). Many Reformed writers hold that persistent, unrepentant abuse is an **abandonment** of marital vows (cf. 1 Cor. 7:15; Ex. 21:10-11) which can warrant divorce, and others hold that Scripture permits only the two named grounds. Know your view and your presbytery's, and the Assembly's study report. **Hot topic: Divorce and remarriage.** |
| 1 | May those who have been divorced remarry? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | The Confession allows the **innocent party** to remarry "as if the offending party were dead" (WCF 24.5; Matt. 19:9; 1 Cor. 7:15), only "in the Lord" (1 Cor. 7:39). The status of the **guilty party** and of those divorced on **non-biblical grounds** is debated: some hold that remarriage is forbidden while the former spouse lives (Mark 10:11-12; Luke 16:18; Rom. 7:2-3), others allow it after **genuine repentance** and under the oversight of the session (Luke 15; 1 Cor. 6:9-11). Pastoral care and church discipline apply either way. **Hot topic: Divorce and remarriage.** |
