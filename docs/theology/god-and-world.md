# God and His World

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

Chapell, theology section C. Standards: **WCF 2-5; WLC 7-19; WSC 4-11.** The **Tier** column ranks each question (1 = know cold, 2 = second pass, 3 = if time allows).

## God

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is God? | WSC 4: "God is a Spirit, infinite, eternal, and unchangeable, in his being, wisdom, power, holiness, justice, goodness, and truth." Only one living and true God (Deut. 6:4; Jer. 10:10); spirit (John 4:24); the Creator and sovereign Lord of all (Ps. 115:3; Acts 17:24-28). WCF 2.1. |
| 1 | Briefly explain and defend (including Scripture proofs) the doctrine of the Trinity. | There is **one God** who exists eternally in **three persons**, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, the same in substance, equal in power and glory (WSC 6; WCF 2.3). **One:** Deut. 6:4; Isa. 45:5. **Three persons, each God:** Father (1 Cor. 8:6), Son (John 1:1; 20:28; Heb. 1:8), Spirit (Acts 5:3-4; 1 Cor. 3:16). **Distinct yet together:** Matt. 3:16-17 (baptism of Jesus); Matt. 28:19 ("name," singular, of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit); 2 Cor. 13:14; John 14:16-17. Errors: **modalism** (one person in three masks), **tritheism**, **Arianism/subordinationism** (the Son is a creature). |
| 1 | What are the attributes of God? (Be prepared to offer Scripture proofs.) | Aspects of God's character as he has revealed himself. Grouped as **incommunicable** (not shared by creatures) and **communicable** (shared in a creaturely way). Other lists (e.g., "natural" and "moral") exist; know a classification and be able to use it. |
| 1 | Which are communicable? | Attributes in which we are made to reflect God: **wisdom** (Rom. 16:27), **power** (Gen. 17:1; Job 42:2), **holiness** (Isa. 6:3), **justice** (Deut. 32:4), **goodness and love** (Ps. 145:9; 1 John 4:8), **truth** (Num. 23:19; John 17:3), **mercy and grace** (Ex. 34:6-7), **wrath** (Rom. 1:18). Also spirituality (John 4:24) and personality (Ex. 3:14). |
| 1 | Which are incommunicable? | Attributes that belong to God alone: **independence or self-existence** (Ex. 3:14; Acts 17:24-25), **immutability** (Mal. 3:6; James 1:17), **infinity**, including **eternity** (Ps. 90:2; 1 Kings 8:27), **omnipresence** (Ps. 139:7-12), **omniscience** (Ps. 139:1-6; Isa. 46:10), and **omnipotence** (Gen. 18:14; Jer. 32:17), **unity and simplicity** (Deut. 6:4). |
| 1 | What are the decrees of God? | WSC 7: "The decrees of God are his eternal purpose, according to the counsel of his will, whereby, for his own glory, he hath foreordained whatsoever comes to pass." They are wise, free, holy, unchangeable, and include the **means** as well as the ends (WCF 3.1). Eph. 1:11; Isa. 46:10; Ps. 33:11; Dan. 4:35; Acts 2:23; 4:27-28. God is **not the author of sin**, and no violence is done to the will of creatures, nor is liberty or contingency of second causes taken away (WCF 3.1). |
| 2 | What is the difference between infralapsarian and supralapsarian views of God's decrees? | Both ask the **logical order** of God's decrees concerning creation, the fall, election, and reprobation. **Supralapsarian** (*supra* = above/before): God first decreed to elect some and reprobate others, then to create and permit the fall as the means. **Infralapsarian** (*infra* = after): God first decreed to create and permit the fall, then to elect some out of fallen humanity and pass over the rest. The Synod of Dort and most Reformed theologians are infralapsarian; the Westminster Standards use language compatible with infralapsarianism but do not require it. |
| 2 | What is an Amyraldian view of God's decrees? | **Amyraldism** (Moses Amyraut, 17th-century France; "hypothetical universalism," "four-point Calvinism"): God decreed that Christ die for all people on condition of faith, then, knowing none would believe, elected some to receive faith. Reformed theology has historically rejected it because it divides the Trinity's purpose in redemption and makes Christ's death not effectual for those it was meant to save; the Standards teach that Christ redeemed the elect (WCF 3.6; 8.5; WLC 59). |
| 1 | How does God execute his decrees? | WSC 8: "God executeth his decrees in the works of creation and providence." |
| 1 | What is God's work of creation? | WSC 9: "The work of creation is God's making all things of nothing, by the word of his power, in the space of six days, and all very good." (*Ex nihilo.*) Gen. 1-2; Ps. 33:6, 9; Heb. 11:3; Col. 1:16; Rev. 4:11. By the triune God (Gen. 1:1-2; John 1:3; Col. 1:16), for his glory, with man as its crown. WCF 4.1. |
| 2 | Briefly discuss the length of the days of creation. | The Standards say creation was "in the space of six days" (WCF 4.1; WSC 9). Evangelical Reformed views: **24-hour days** (the plain sense of the narrative, Ex. 20:11), **day-age** (long periods), the **framework hypothesis** (the days are a literary structure), and **analogical days**. PCA presbyteries differ in how much latitude they allow; **know the views, know yours, and know your presbytery's expectations** and the Assembly's study report on creation. Whatever the view, hold that God created all things from nothing, that Adam and Eve were real, specially created people (Gen. 1:26-27; 2:7, 21-22), and that death came by sin (Rom. 5:12). |
| 2 | Evaluate biblically the theory of evolution. | Distinguish **scientific claims** (observed change, adaptation) from the **philosophy** that the universe and all life came about without God's purpose. Scripture teaches a purposeful, personal Creator (Gen. 1; Ps. 104; Rom. 1:20) and the **special creation of Adam**, from whom all humanity descends (Gen. 2:7; Rom. 5:12-19; 1 Cor. 15:21-22, 45; Acts 17:26). A fully naturalistic evolution contradicts these teachings, and theistic evolution must explain how it can hold to a historical Adam, a historical fall, and death as the penalty for sin. A related hot topic: **Creation.** |
| 1 | What is God's work of providence? | WSC 11: "God's works of providence are his most holy, wise, and powerful preserving and governing all his creatures, and all their actions." Ps. 103:19; Matt. 10:29-31; Col. 1:17; Heb. 1:3; Prov. 16:9; Rom. 8:28; Gen. 50:20. He ordinarily works through **means** (secondary causes), but can work above, beyond, or against them (WCF 5.1-3). |
| 2 | What is a miracle? | An act of God in the world that is **extraordinary, outside the usual course of providence**, and a sign of his power and presence, often accompanying new revelation (Ex. 7-12; Mark 2:10-12; John 2:11; 20:30-31; Heb. 2:3-4). |
| 2 | Do miracles occur today? Explain. | God is free to work miraculously, and he does (above all in the new birth, Eph. 2:4-6; and in answer to prayer, James 5:14-16). The disagreement is over whether **miracles that attest new revelation** (as in the age of Moses, the prophets, Christ, and the apostles) continue. **Cessationists** say the sign gifts ceased with the completion of the canon (WCF 1.1; Heb. 2:3-4; 2 Cor. 12:12); **continuationists** say they may continue. See [The Church and the Spirit](church-spirit.md). |
| 1 | Is God responsible for sin? | **No.** God ordains all things, yet he is not the author of sin, nor does he approve it or tempt anyone to it (WCF 3.1; 5.4; Hab. 1:13; James 1:13-15; 1 John 1:5). The blame for sin lies with the sinful creature, who acts from his own evil desires. See the next three rows. |
| 1 | Did he decree it? | Yes: the decrees include "whatsoever comes to pass," and God decreed to **permit** the fall (WCF 3.1; 6.1). Gen. 50:20 (Joseph's brothers meant evil, God meant it for good); Acts 2:23; 4:27-28 (the crucifixion was by God's "definite plan" yet done by "lawless men"). |
| 1 | Did he permit it? | Yes, but not by a bare permission: his providence extends "even to the first fall, and all other sins," and involves a "most wise and powerful bounding, and otherwise ordering and governing of them" for his own holy ends, "yet so, as the sinfulness thereof proceedeth only from the creature, and not from God" (WCF 5.4). Job 1:12; 2:6; 2 Sam. 24:1, 10; Isa. 10:5-15. |
| 1 | Who is the ultimate cause of all things? | **God** is the first and ultimate cause of all things (Rom. 11:36; Eph. 1:11), but he acts through **secondary causes** (creatures, who are real causes of their own acts). He is not the cause of sin in the sense of doing it or approving it; the secondary cause (the sinner) is. |
| 2 | What is theodicy? | From Greek *theos* ("God") and *dike* ("justice"): the **defense of God's goodness and justice** in view of the existence of evil. The Reformed answer: God is good, holy, and sovereign; he ordains even evil for good purposes beyond our understanding (Gen. 50:20; Rom. 8:28; 9:14-24; Job 38-42), without being its author, and he has dealt with evil in the cross and will end it (Rev. 21:4). |
| 2 | If God is good and all-powerful, explain AIDS, deformed babies, natural disasters. | These are part of a **fallen, cursed world** (Gen. 3:14-19; Rom. 8:20-22). Particular suffering is **not always** punishment for a particular sin (John 9:1-3; Luke 13:1-5), yet all suffering is bound up with the fall. God's reasons are often hidden (Deut. 29:29; Isa. 55:8-9; Job 38-42), but he is sovereign (Rom. 8:28), is near to the afflicted (Ps. 34:18), entered our suffering in Christ (Heb. 4:15), and promises to wipe every tear (Rev. 21:4). Answer pastorally: with sorrow, not as an argument to win. |
| 1 | Discuss the biblical teaching regarding predestination, election, and reprobation. | **Predestination:** God's eternal decree about the destiny of angels and men (WCF 3.3). **Election:** his choice in Christ, before the world began, of some people to eternal life, out of his free grace and love, without any foreseen faith or good works (WCF 3.5; Eph. 1:3-6, 11; Rom. 8:29-30; 9:11-23; 2 Thess. 2:13; Acts 13:48). **Reprobation:** God passes by the rest and ordains them to dishonor and wrath **for their sin** (WCF 3.7; Rom. 9:17-22; 1 Pet. 2:8; Jude 4). Election is the cause of salvation; sin is the cause of condemnation (the two are not symmetrical). |
| 2 | Distinguish the Reformed and Arminian understandings of these doctrines. | **Arminian:** God elects on the basis of **foreseen faith** (conditional election); Christ died for all equally; grace is resistible; believers can fall away. **Reformed:** election is unconditional and based on God's good pleasure; Christ's death effectually saves the elect; the Spirit's call is effectual; the saints persevere. See the [Five Points](core-doctrines.md). Key texts for the Reformed view: Rom. 9; Eph. 1:3-14; John 6:37-44. |
| 1 | Are these doctrines compatible with belief in real human choice and responsibility? | **Yes.** Scripture teaches both: God's sovereignty and human responsibility (Acts 2:23; Phil. 2:12-13; Gen. 50:20; Isa. 10:5-15; Prov. 16:9). The Standards say God's decree does not do **violence** to the will, and does not take away "the liberty or contingency of second causes" (WCF 3.1; 5.2; 9.1). People choose freely according to their own desires, and are responsible for their choices, even though God ordains what they choose. This is the **compatibility** of sovereignty and freedom. Admit that there is mystery here (Deut. 29:29; Rom. 11:33-36). |
| 2 | What should be some of the practical results of believing the doctrine of election? | WCF 3.8: it gives "praise, reverence, and admiration of God," and "humility, diligence, and abundant consolation to all that sincerely obey the gospel." It also produces **assurance** (Rom. 8:28-39), **gratitude** and **worship** (Eph. 1:3-6), **humility** (1 Cor. 4:7), **holiness** (Eph. 1:4), and **confidence in evangelism** (Acts 18:9-10; 2 Tim. 2:10). It must be handled with prudence (WCF 3.8). |
| 2 | Is there a real, personal devil? Explain from Scripture. | Yes: a fallen angel, personal and powerful but limited. Gen. 3:1-5; Job 1-2; Matt. 4:1-11 (Jesus tempted by him); John 8:44 ("a murderer from the beginning"); 1 Pet. 5:8; Eph. 6:11-12; Rev. 12:9; 20:10. He is under God's rule (Job 1:12; 2:6), **defeated at the cross** (Col. 2:15; Heb. 2:14-15; 1 John 3:8), and will be cast into the lake of fire (Matt. 25:41; Rev. 20:10). Resist him with truth, faith, and prayer (James 4:7; Eph. 6:10-18). |
