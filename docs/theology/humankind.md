## Creation and the Image of God

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the chief end of man? | WSC 1: "Man's chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him for ever." 1 Cor. 10:31; Rom. 11:36; Ps. 73:24-28; Isa. 43:7. |
| 1 | What is the supreme activity of man? (Catechism: "What does God require of man?") | To **love, worship, and glorify God**: knowing him and delighting in him. WSC 3: the Scriptures teach "what duty God requires of man"; WSC 39: "The duty which God requireth of man, is obedience to his revealed will." Mic. 6:8; Deut. 10:12-13; Matt. 22:37-38 (the first and great commandment). |
| 1 | What does the Bible teach about the creation of human beings? | WSC 10: God created man, male and female, after his own image, in knowledge, righteousness, and holiness, with dominion over the creatures. Man is formed from dust and made a living soul (Gen. 2:7), body and soul together, male and female created equal in value (Gen. 1:27; 2:18-25). God made him good (Gen. 1:31) and in covenant with himself. WCF 4.1-2. |
| 1 | In what way is man created in the image of God? | Man is like God and represents him: in **knowledge, righteousness, and holiness** (Col. 3:10; Eph. 4:24), with **dominion** over creation (Gen. 1:26-28), as a rational, moral, relational person (Gen. 2:18-24). Many Reformed writers distinguish a **narrow** sense (original righteousness, lost in the fall and restored in Christ) and a **broad** sense (human nature, reason, morality, relationships, still present after the fall). Christ is the perfect image (2 Cor. 4:4; Col. 1:15; Heb. 1:3), and believers are being renewed into it (Rom. 8:29; Col. 3:10; 2 Cor. 3:18). |
| 2 | Are all men in the image of God? If so, to what extent? | **Yes**, all people bear God's image (Gen. 9:6; James 3:9), which is the basis of human dignity and the prohibition of murder; but in fallen people the image is **marred**: they have lost original righteousness and are spiritually dead (Eph. 2:1; 4:18), though they retain human nature, reason, conscience, and moral awareness. It is **renewed** in believers (Col. 3:10). |
| 2 | How does the doctrine of the image of God influence debates about gender roles? | Man and woman are **equally** made in God's image (Gen. 1:27; Gal. 3:28), with equal dignity, value, and standing before God. Complementarians hold that equality is consistent with **distinct roles** given in creation, before the fall (Gen. 2:18-23; 1 Cor. 11:3, 7-9; Eph. 5:22-33; 1 Tim. 2:11-14), the man bearing the responsibility of headship in the home and church. Egalitarians argue that the shared image means interchangeable roles. The PCA holds that the offices of elder and deacon are for qualified men. See [The Church and the Spirit](church-spirit.md). |
| 3 | Where did man's soul come from? When? | Scripture says God **breathed** life into Adam (Gen. 2:7) and the Westminster Standards say God "created" the souls (WCF 4.2). For each later person, **creationism** holds that God creates a new soul at conception or by his direct act (Eccl. 12:7; Zech. 12:1; Heb. 12:9), while **traducianism** holds that it comes from the parents with the body (Gen. 5:3; Heb. 7:9-10; Rom. 5:12). Reformed theologians have held both. Scripture treats the unborn child as a person from conception (Ps. 51:5; 139:13-16; Jer. 1:5; Luke 1:41-44). The Standards require neither view. |

## Sin and the Fall

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is sin? (See WSC 14.) Include Scripture proofs. | WSC 14: "Sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, the law of God." **Transgression** (1 John 3:4) and **falling short** (Rom. 3:23); sins of **omission** and **commission** (James 4:17; Matt. 25:41-46). Sin is rebellion against God, not only against his law (Ps. 51:4; Gen. 3:1-6). |
| 1 | Discuss the guilt of sin and the corruption of sin. | **Guilt** is liability to punishment under God's justice (Rom. 3:19; 5:16, 18; 6:23; Eph. 2:3). **Corruption** is the inward pollution and bent of the whole person toward sin (Rom. 3:10-18; 7:18; Eph. 4:17-19; Jer. 17:9). Guilt needs **pardon** (justification); corruption needs **renewal** (sanctification). WLC 25; WSC 18. |
| 1 | Define and discuss original sin (include Scripture proofs). | **Original sin:** the sin of Adam, our covenant head, imputed to all his descendants (guilt), and the **corrupt nature** inherited from him (WCF 6.3; WSC 18: "the guilt of Adam's first sin, the want of original righteousness, and the corruption of his whole nature"). **Rom. 5:12-19** (through one man sin entered the world; by one trespass many died; by one man's disobedience many were made sinners); 1 Cor. 15:21-22; Ps. 51:5; 58:3; Eph. 2:1-3 ("by nature children of wrath"); Gen. 5:3. Exceptions: Christ, conceived by the Spirit (WSC 22). |
| 2 | How can original sin be reconciled to the canons of justice, either human or divine? | God is just in his dealings because **Adam was our representative head**, chosen by God in the covenant of works, in the best possible circumstances (Gen. 2:15-17; Rom. 5:12-19). The same **principle of representation** is the basis of the gospel: Christ, the last Adam, takes the place of those he represents (Rom. 5:18-19; 1 Cor. 15:45-49). Also, every person is guilty of his own actual sins (Rom. 3:23), and God owes salvation to no one. Rom. 9:20 reminds us not to cross-examine God (Rom. 11:33-36). |
| 2 | Is there any good remaining in a fallen, sinful man? Explain. | **No spiritual good** that pleases God: sin has affected the whole person (Rom. 3:10-12; 8:7-8; Eph. 2:1-3; Gen. 6:5). Yet by **common grace**, God restrains sin and enables fallen people to do outward "civil good" and to have conscience (Rom. 2:14-15; Matt. 7:11; Gen. 4:20-22; Rom. 13:3-4); people still bear God's image (Gen. 9:6), and he gives good gifts to all (Matt. 5:45; Acts 14:17). Such works are not truly good before God, since they do not flow from faith or aim at his glory (WCF 16.7). |
| 2 | In what ways does man sin? | In **thought, word, and deed** (Matt. 5:21-28; 12:36-37; Mark 7:21-23), by **omission** and **commission** (James 4:17), against God and neighbor; in heart attitudes (Prov. 4:23), knowingly or in ignorance (Lev. 4; Num. 15:27-31), by sins of weakness and willful sins. WLC 24; the Ten Commandments show each category (WSC 41-81). |
| 1 | What are some of the consequences of sin? | **Death** in all its forms: spiritual (Eph. 2:1), physical (Rom. 5:12), eternal (Rom. 6:23; Rev. 20:14); **guilt** and condemnation (Rom. 5:16); **loss of communion with God** (Gen. 3:8-10, 23-24; Isa. 59:2); **corruption** and bondage (John 8:34; Rom. 6:16); **a cursed creation** (Gen. 3:17-19; Rom. 8:20-22); **broken relationships** (Gen. 3:7, 12, 16; 4:8). WSC 19: all mankind lose communion with God, are under his wrath and curse, and are liable to all miseries in this life, to death itself, and to the pains of hell forever. |

## The Will of Man

Chapell: *"Is the will of man free? Explain (include Scripture proofs)."* WCF 9 gives the answer in four states.

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the will of man free? Explain (include Scripture proofs). | Man's will has **natural liberty**: it is not forced to good or evil by any absolute necessity of nature (WCF 9.1; Matt. 17:12; James 1:14; Deut. 30:19). But the **moral ability** of the will varies with man's state (WCF 9.2-5): innocence, sin, grace, and glory. "Free" means free to choose what we **desire**, not free from the influence of our sinful nature. The question is not whether people choose, but **what their choices are and why**. |
| 1 | Was Adam's will (before he sinned) free? Explain (include Scripture proofs). | **Yes**: in innocence Adam had "freedom and power to will and to do that which is good and well-pleasing to God," yet **mutably**, so that he might fall from it (WCF 9.2; Gen. 1:31; 2:16-17; 3:1-6; Eccl. 7:29). |
| 1 | Can a sinner do anything good? Explain (include Scripture proofs). | The sinner can do **nothing spiritually good**: man "hath wholly lost all ability of will to any spiritual good accompanying salvation" (WCF 9.3). Rom. 3:10-12; 8:7-8 (the mind set on the flesh "does not submit to God's law; indeed, it cannot"); John 15:5 ("apart from me you can do nothing"); 1 Cor. 2:14; Jer. 13:23; Eph. 2:1-3. He may do things that are good in a **civil** or outward sense (see above). |
| 1 | Is a sinner's will free to believe? Explain (include Scripture proofs). | **No, not apart from grace**: he is "dead in trespasses" (Eph. 2:1, 5), "no one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him" (John 6:44, 65), and the natural person "does not accept the things of the Spirit of God" (1 Cor. 2:14). He **will not** come (John 5:40) and therefore **cannot** (the inability is moral: he hates the light, John 3:19-20). God must give a new heart, repentance, and faith (Ezek. 36:26-27; Acts 11:18; 16:14; Eph. 2:8-9; Phil. 1:29). Contrast the Arminian view of **prevenient grace** that restores everyone's ability to choose. |
| 1 | Does a believer have free will? Explain (include Scripture proofs). | **Yes, restored and growing**: when God converts a sinner and translates him into the state of grace, he frees him from bondage to sin, and enables him freely to will and to do what is spiritually good, yet not perfectly, since "by reason of his remaining corruption, he doth not perfectly, nor only, will that which is good, but doth also will that which is evil" (WCF 9.4). Phil. 2:12-13; Rom. 6:17-18; 7:14-25; Gal. 5:16-17. In **glory**, the will is made "perfectly and immutably free to good alone" (WCF 9.5; Rev. 21:27; 1 John 3:2). |
