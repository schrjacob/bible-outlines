Chapell, church government sections G ("Principles of Discipline," chapters 27-30), H ("The Process of Discipline," chapters 31-38), and I ("Review and Control; Jurisdiction," chapters 39-46). This is the **Rules of Discipline**. The **Tier** column ranks each question (1 = know cold, 2 = second pass, 3 = if time allows). ## G. Principles of Discipline (Chapters 27-30)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is church discipline? | "Discipline is the **exercise of authority given the Church by the Lord Jesus Christ to instruct and guide its members and to promote its purity and welfare**." It has two senses: (a) the whole **government, inspection, training, guardianship and control** the church maintains over its members, officers, and courts; and (b) the restricted, technical sense, **judicial process** (BCO 27-1). All baptized persons, being members, are subject to it and entitled to its benefits (27-2). It is "systematic training under the authority of God's Scripture" (27-4). |
| 1 | What is the goal of church discipline? | Discipline maintains **the glory of God, the purity of his Church, and the keeping and reclaiming of disobedient sinners** (27-3). "Its ends, so far as it involves judicial action, are the **rebuke of offenses, the removal of scandal, the vindication of the honor of Christ, the promotion of the purity and general edification of the Church, and the spiritual good of offenders themselves**" (27-3). "The power which Christ has given the Church is for **building up, and not for destruction**... as under a dispensation of mercy and not of wrath" (27-4). |
| 1 | What are its proper steps? | BCO 27-5 (Matt. 18:15-16; Gal. 6:1): (a) **instruction in the Word**; (b) the individual's responsibility to **admonish one another**; (c) if the admonition is rejected, **calling one or more witnesses**; (d) if rejection persists, the Church acts **through her court** unto admonition, suspension, excommunication, and deposition. "Steps (a) through (d) must be followed in proper order." |
| 2 | Who is primarily responsible for disciplining noncommuning members? | The **parents**: "The spiritual nurture, instruction and training of the children of the Church are committed by God **primarily to their parents**. They are responsible to the Church for the faithful discharge of their obligations" (BCO 28-1). The church supports them with Sunday schools, Bible classes, catechizing, and sympathetic relations (28-2, 28-3). Non-communing members are under the care of the church to which their parents belong (28-5). |
| 1 | List and define church censures. | BCO 30-1: the censures are **admonition, suspension from the Sacraments, excommunication, suspension from office, and deposition from office**. **Admonition:** "the formal reproof of an offender by a church court, warning him of his guilt and danger, and exhorting him to be more circumspect and watchful" (30-2). **Suspension from the Sacraments:** "the temporary exclusion from those ordinances," **indefinite** as to duration; there is no definite suspension from the sacraments (30-3). **Suspension from office:** the exclusion of an officer from his office, **definite or indefinite** (30-3); definite suspension is for one who has repented but the credit of religion requires it; indefinite is for the impenitent. **Excommunication:** "the excision of an offender from the communion of the Church," inflicted only for **gross crime or heresy** when the offender is "incorrigible and contumacious" (30-4). **Deposition:** "the degradation of an officer from his office" (30-5). Admonition or definite suspension are for the repentant and conclude the process; indefinite suspension or excommunication for those who remain impenitent (30-1). |

## H. The Process of Discipline (Chapters 31-38)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | With what court should charges be filed? | The court of **original jurisdiction**: the **presbytery** of which a minister is a member, and the **session** of the church of which a member (including ruling elders and deacons) is a member (BCO 31-1; 33-1; 34-1). If a session refuses to act in doctrinal cases or public scandal and two other sessions in the presbytery request it, the presbytery assumes jurisdiction (33-1); likewise for presbytery and the General Assembly in cases against ministers, on the request of two other presbyteries (34-1). |
| 1 | What must be done before formal charges are brought? | "An **injured party** shall not become a prosecutor of personal offenses without having tried the means of **reconciliation and of reclaiming the offender**, required by Christ" (Matt. 18:15-16) (BCO 31-5). Those to whom private offenses are known must first try private means (31-5). Courts have a duty to demand satisfactory explanations of reports affecting Christian character and to **investigate**; if the investigation raises "**a strong presumption of the guilt** of the party involved, the court **shall institute process**, and shall appoint a prosecutor" (31-2). When a court itself institutes process, the Matt. 18 steps are not required, though a committee often speaks privately with the offender first (31-7). |
| 2 | When must a formal judicial process be pursued? | When the investigation "result[s] in raising a **strong presumption of the guilt**" of the party, the court "shall institute process" (BCO 31-2). Process may not begin unless someone undertakes to make out the charge, or the court finds it necessary for the honor of religion to take that step itself (32-2). A person may also come forward and confess; the court then renders judgment **without process** with a written confession approved by the accused and the court (38-1). |
| 1 | How many witnesses are required? | "The testimony of **more than one witness** shall be necessary in order to establish any charge; yet if, in addition to the testimony of one witness, **corroborative evidence** be produced, the offense may be considered to be proved" (BCO 35-4; Deut. 19:15; Matt. 18:16; 1 Tim. 5:19). |
| 1 | How should a censure be administered? | "With all tenderness... in the spirit of meekness" (BCO 36-1), **suited to the nature of the offense** (36-2). **Admonition** is administered **privately** by one or more members of the court if the offense is known to few and not aggravated; if public, by the **moderator in the presence of the court**, and it may be announced publicly (36-3). **Suspension** (definite or indefinite) is administered in the presence of the court alone or in open session, with prescribed words and **prayer**, and indefinite suspension "with added solemnity" (36-4, 36-5). **Excommunication** follows the form for indefinite suspension, or is inflicted in public, with a statement of the steps taken and the authority from Matt. 18:15-18 and 1 Cor. 5:1-5 (36-6). **Deposition** is administered by the moderator in the prescribed words, with solemnities like excommunication (36-7). |
| 1 | What is the goal of discipline in regard to the offender? | His **spiritual good and restoration**: the censure is to bring him to repentance (36-6; 27-3), and the church is to converse and pray with an indefinitely suspended person "that it would please God to give him repentance" (37-2). When the court is satisfied of repentance he is **restored** to the sacraments and office, or, if excommunicated, to the communion of the church after a confession and three questions (37-3, 37-4; Gal. 6:1; 2 Cor. 2:5-11). |
| 3 | What are the basic time requirements in a case of process? | A **citation** must be served at least **ten days** before the meeting at which the accused must appear (32-3, 32-7); once he pleads, the **trial** may not be sooner than **fourteen days** after citation (32-3). An accused who refuses to obey a second citation or to cooperate is dealt with for **contumacy** (32-6; 33-2). |

## I. Review and Control; Jurisdiction (Chapters 39-46)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Define "review and control." | The right and duty of every court above the session "to **review, at least once a year, the records of the court next below**" (BCO 40-1). The higher court examines whether the proceedings have been **correctly recorded, regular and in accordance with the Constitution, wise, equitable and suited to promote the welfare of the Church**, and whether its injunctions have been obeyed (40-2). It may approve, disapprove, or correct, and where there is serious irregularity may require correction (40-3). If a court neglects its duty or acts in grossly unconstitutional ways, the higher court may cite it and reverse, redress, or censure it (40-4, 40-5). Review and control is one of the **four modes** of supervision: review and control, reference, appeal, and complaint (39-1). |
| 2 | Define "reference." | "A written representation and application made **by a lower court to a higher** for advice or other action on a matter pending before the lower court" (BCO 41-1). Proper subjects are matters that are "new, delicate or difficult," on which the court is seriously divided, or which involve the Constitution (41-2). A lower court may ask for advice only or for final disposition, including that the higher court take original jurisdiction in a judicial case (41-3). The higher court need not accede but should ordinarily give advice (41-5). |
| 2 | Define "appeal." How quickly must an appeal be filed? | "The **transfer to a higher court of a judicial case** on which judgment has been rendered in a lower court," allowable **only to the party against whom the decision was rendered** (the appellant and appellee), and only to the next higher court (BCO 42-1). Written notice with supporting reasons must be filed with both clerks "**within thirty (30) days of notification** of the last court's decision" (42-4). Notice of appeal **suspends** the lower court's judgment until the case is finally decided (42-6). |
| 2 | Define "complaint." How quickly must a complaint be filed? | "A **written representation made against some act or decision of a court** of the Church," which any **communing member in good standing** may make against any action of a court to whose jurisdiction he is subject, except that no complaint is allowable in a judicial case after process has commenced (BCO 43-1). It is made **first to the court whose act is alleged to be in error**, with written notice and reasons filed with its clerk "**within sixty (60) days** following the meeting of the court" (43-2). If denied (or not considered at the next stated meeting), the complainant may take it to the **next higher court** by written notice within **thirty (30) days** of notification of the last court's decision (43-3). A complaint does **not** suspend the action unless one-third of those present when the action was taken vote for suspension (43-4). |
| 3 | What are dissents, protests, and objections? | A member of a court who had the right to vote and is dissatisfied may have a **dissent** (a different opinion) or **protest** (a more solemn, formal declaration) recorded; a member who could not vote on an appeal or complaint may record an **objection** (BCO 45-1 to 45-4). They must be filed within **30 days** and be in temperate and respectful language (45-1, 45-5). |
| 2 | What should happen when a member moves out of the community? | It is his duty to **transfer his membership** by a **certificate of dismission** from his session to the church he wishes to join (BCO 46-1). The elders of his church should, as far as possible, continue pastoral oversight and tell him he should transfer as soon as practicable to the church in whose bounds he lives, and they should notify that church's elders (46-2). Members dismissed remain under the jurisdiction of the dismissing session until they form a regular connection with the new church (46-3). A certificate is valid testimony for only **one year** (46-7). |
| 2 | What should be done when a member neglects the church for a year or more? | BCO 38-4: when a member "has **willfully neglected the church for a period of one year**, or has made it known that he has no intention of fulfilling the church vows," the session, "continuing to exercise **pastoral discipline**... in the spirit of Galatians 6:1," shall **remind him**, in person and in writing, of his membership vows and **warn him** that if he persists **his name shall be erased from the roll**. After diligent pastoral discipline and due delay, if the session judges that he will not fulfill his obligations, it **erases his name from the roll**, an act of pastoral discipline **without process**. If the member makes a **written request for process**, the session must grant it. For a member who moved and did not transfer within a year, see 46-2; for one whose residence has been unknown for a year, the name is removed (46-2). |
| 3 | What is an associate member? | "Believers **temporarily residing in a location other than their permanent homes**. Such believers may become associate members of a particular church without ceasing to be communicant members of their home churches. An associate member shall have **all the rights and privileges of that church, with the exception of voting in a congregational or corporation meeting, and holding an office** in that church" (BCO 46-4). |
