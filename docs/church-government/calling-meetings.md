## E. Church Orders (Chapters 16-24)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How is a man called to office in the church? | By a **double call**: the **inward call**, God's work in the man's heart giving him desire, gifts, and conviction (1 Tim. 3:1; Acts 13:2); and the **outward call**, the church's recognition of those gifts, through **election by the congregation** (or, for a teaching elder, a call from a congregation approved by presbytery) and **ordination** by the laying on of hands (BCO 16; Acts 6:3-6; 14:23; 1 Tim. 4:14; 5:22). |
| 2 | What is a candidate? | A **candidate** is a man who has expressed his intention to enter the ministry and has been **enrolled under the care of a presbytery** as he prepares (BCO 18). |
| 2 | What is required to become a candidate? | He must be a **communing member in good standing** of a particular church, be **recommended by his session**, and be **examined by the presbytery** (or its committee) on his **Christian experience, call, and motives**, and be received and enrolled; he then remains **under the care of presbytery**, with periodic review of his preparation (BCO 18; verify). |
| 2 | What is a licentiate? | A **licentiate** is a candidate whom the presbytery has **examined and licensed to preach** the gospel; he may preach and lead, but he **may not administer the sacraments** or be ordained until he is **called** to a work and **examined for ordination** (BCO 19). |
| 1 | In what areas is a prospective licentiate examined? | BCO 19-2: **Christian experience and inward call**; **basic knowledge of biblical doctrine** (as in the Confession of Faith and Catechisms); **practical knowledge of Bible content**; **basic knowledge of the government** of the PCA (the BCO); and a **written sermon** on an assigned passage, which is presented before presbytery. |
| 3 | What is internship? | A period of **supervised ministry experience** in a church, usually under a pastor or session, that is part of the candidate's preparation for licensure and ordination (BCO 19; presbytery policies vary; verify). |
| 1 | What steps must a church take in calling a pastor? | (1) With the permission of **presbytery**, the **session** calls a **congregational meeting**, and a **pulpit (search) committee** or the session finds and examines candidates. (2) The **congregation** meets, hears the candidate, and **votes** (a majority). (3) The **call** is prepared in writing (with salary and terms) and **presented to the presbytery** for approval. (4) The minister **accepts**, and the presbytery **installs** him (BCO 20; verify the details). |
| 1 | In what areas must a candidate for ordination be examined? | BCO 21-4: **experimental religion**, especially his **personal character and family management** (1 Tim. 3:1-7; Titus 1:6-9); **knowledge of Greek and Hebrew** (or a seminary degree that includes them); **Bible content**; **theology**; **sacraments**; **church history**; **the history of the PCA**; and **the principles and rules of government and discipline**. He must also prepare a **thesis** on an assigned theological topic, an **exegesis** of an assigned passage, and **preach a sermon** before presbytery. |
| 1 | What vows must he take? | Ordination vows (BCO 21-5), in substance: (1) Do you **believe the Scriptures** of the Old and New Testaments to be the **Word of God, the only infallible rule of faith and practice**? (2) Do you **sincerely receive and adopt the Confession of Faith and Catechisms** as containing the **system of doctrine taught in Scripture**? (3) Do you **approve the government and discipline** of the PCA? (4) Do you promise **subjection to your brethren in the Lord**? (5) Have you been induced to seek the office from **love for God and zeal for his glory**? (6) Do you promise to be **zealous and faithful in maintaining the truth** of the gospel and the **purity, peace, and unity of the church**? (7) Will you be **faithful and diligent** in all your duties, public and private? Know the current wording of each vow from the BCO (verify), and what you will say of any exception to the Standards. Ruling elders and deacons take parallel vows. |
| 1 | Who has the final say in establishing or dissolving a pastoral relationship? | The **presbytery**: the congregation **calls**, but the presbytery must **approve and install**, and **only the presbytery can dissolve** the pastoral relationship (BCO 20, 22). |
| 2 | How may a pastoral relation be dissolved? | By the **presbytery**, on (a) the **pastor's request** (he asks to be released, e.g., to accept another call, or to retire); (b) the **congregation's request**, after notice and a hearing; (c) **mutual consent**; or (d) the **presbytery's own action** for cause, including **discipline** (BCO 23; verify the steps and the notice required). |
| 2 | What is the difference between an associate pastor and an assistant pastor? | An **associate pastor** is a **teaching elder called by the congregation**, working alongside the pastor in a shared pastoral ministry and **serving with the pastor** (in the session); he is installed by presbytery. An **assistant pastor** is **called by the session** (not by the congregation), serves **under the direction of the pastor and session**, is a **member of presbytery** but not of the session, and does not succeed the pastor by right (BCO 22; verify). |
| 1 | What steps must be followed in electing ruling elders or deacons? | The **congregation** (by a meeting called by the session) **elects** them: nominations (by the session or from the floor), **vote** by the communing members present (a majority). The **session examines** each nominee, then **instructs, ordains, and installs** those elected, who take their **vows** before the congregation (BCO 24; Acts 6:3-6). |
| 1 | In what areas is a nominee to be examined? | His **Christian experience and call**, his **knowledge of the Bible and doctrine** and the Standards, his **understanding of the government and discipline** of the church (BCO), and his **qualifications and family life** (1 Tim. 3; Titus 1) (BCO 24; verify). He must also **be willing to take the vows**, including adopting the Standards. |

## F. Congregational Meetings; Amending the Constitution (Chapters 25-26)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | How may a congregational meeting be called? | By the **session**, on its own authority, or when **requested** by the members (or the presbytery) as the BCO provides, with **notice announced** on the **Lord's Day** before and the purpose stated (BCO 25; verify the notice and the number of members who can require a meeting). |
| 2 | Who can vote? | The **communing members** of that church **in good standing**. Non-communing members cannot vote (BCO 25, 6). |
| 2 | What officers are required? | A **moderator**, who must be a **teaching elder** (the pastor, or one appointed by the session or presbytery), and a **clerk** (BCO 25). |
| 2 | Who owns the property of the local church? | The **particular (local) church** owns its property, **not the presbytery or the General Assembly**. (Contrast denominations with a "trust clause.") The congregation holds the property for its own use and may dispose of it as the BCO permits (BCO 25; verify). |
| 3 | How may the Book of Church Order be amended? | By a process involving **both the General Assembly and the presbyteries**: an amendment is **proposed** (by an overture from a presbytery, or by the Assembly), **approved by the Assembly**, **submitted to the presbyteries**, and **adopted** only if a **required majority** of presbyteries (a two-thirds vote, verify) approve, then **enacted** by the following Assembly (BCO 26). |
| 3 | How may the doctrinal standards be amended? | By a **more demanding** process than the BCO: a **two-thirds** vote of the Assembly to propose, approval by **three-fourths** of the presbyteries, and a **two-thirds** vote of the next Assembly to enact (BCO 26; verify all three thresholds). The Standards are therefore harder to change than the BCO. |
