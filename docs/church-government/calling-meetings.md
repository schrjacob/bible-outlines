## E. Church Orders (Chapters 16-24)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How is a man called to office in the church? | BCO 16-1: "Ordinary vocation to office in the Church is the **calling of God by the Spirit**, through the **inward testimony of a good conscience**, the **manifest approbation of God's people**, and the **concurring judgment of a lawful court** of the Church." "No man can be placed over a church in any office without the **election, or at least the consent, of that church**" (16-2). Every candidate for office must be **sound in the faith and godly in life** and is approved by the court that ordains him (16-3). Ordination is "the authoritative admission of one duly called to an office... accompanied with **prayer and the laying on of hands**" (17-2), and only to "a definite work" (17-3). |
| 2 | What is a candidate? | "A member of the Church in full communion who, believing himself to be called to preach the Gospel, **submits himself to the care and guidance of the Presbytery** in his course of study and of practical training" (BCO 18-1). |
| 2 | What is required to become a candidate? | He puts himself under the care of presbytery (ordinarily the one with jurisdiction over his church); his **session gives an endorsement** (testimonials of Christian character and promise of usefulness, with a brief evaluation of his ministry activity); he must have been a **member of that congregation for at least six months** (except in extraordinary cases); he files an application with the presbytery clerk (18-2). The presbytery **examines him on experiential religion and his motives**, and he answers two questions (to maintain a becoming Christian character and prepare diligently; to submit to presbytery's supervision), after which his name is recorded on the roll of candidates (18-3). He remains a member of his church under its session's jurisdiction, with presbytery overseeing his preparation (18-4), and he reports to presbytery at least **once a year** (18-6). |
| 2 | What is a licentiate? | A man the presbytery has **examined and licensed to preach the Gospel** (BCO 19-1 to 19-4). No one may preach regularly in PCA pulpits without license (19-1). The license **expires after four years** unless renewed (19-6) and may be terminated by presbytery by majority vote. A licentiate (and an intern) **may not administer the sacraments** (19-15; 8-5). |
| 1 | In what areas is a prospective licentiate examined? | BCO 19-2: (a) a statement of his **Christian experience and inward call** to preach, in writing and/or orally; (b) a written and/or oral examination for his **basic knowledge of biblical doctrine** as outlined in the Confession and Catechisms, his **practical knowledge of Bible content**, and his **basic knowledge of the government of the PCA** as defined in the BCO; (c) an oral examination before presbytery on his views in those areas; and (d) a **written sermon** on an assigned passage, explanation and application, presented orally. He must also **state any differences** with the Standards, which the court may except only if they are not hostile to the system and do not strike at the vitals of religion (19-2.e, f). He then answers four **licensure questions** (19-3). |
| 3 | What is internship? | "A candidate for ordination must serve an **internship**" of **at least one year**, which may be during or after his formal theological education (BCO 19-7). It is a period of **trial and training** that is both "practical instruction and testing by the Presbytery," in a work the presbytery approves, closely supervised, involving "the full scope of the duties of any regular ministerial calling" (19-7). Interns report to presbytery at least annually (19-12). An intern is not a member of the session and **may not administer the sacraments**, but may conduct funerals (19-15). |
| 1 | What steps must a church take in calling a pastor? | BCO 20-2 to 20-8: (1) The church should seek a pastor "without delay"; the **session calls a congregational meeting** to elect a **pulpit committee**, which recommends a pastoral candidate who meets the constitutional requirements. (2) The session orders a **congregational meeting** at the regular place of worship, with **public notice at least one week** before. (3) **Communing members in good standing** vote; a **majority of all votes cast** (excluding blanks and abstentions) is required (20-3, 20-4). (4) If a large minority is averse, the moderator tries to dissuade the majority (20-5). (5) The **call** is written, in the prescribed form, with financial arrangements and assurance of liberty to proclaim "the whole counsel of God" as understood in the Westminster Confession (20-1, 20-6). (6) **Commissioners are appointed to prosecute the call before presbytery** (20-8). (7) Presbytery must approve the call before the man receives it (21-1) and then **ordains and installs** him (21). |
| 1 | In what areas must a candidate for ordination be examined? | BCO 21-4.c: **experiential religion** (especially his **personal character and family management**, 1 Tim. 3:1-7 and Titus 1:6-9); **knowledge of Greek and Hebrew**; **Bible content**; **theology**; **the Sacraments**; **church history**; **the history of the PCA**; and **the principles and rules of the government and discipline of the church**. He prepares a **thesis** on an assigned theological topic, an **exegesis** of an assigned passage in the original language, and **preaches a sermon**. Educational requirements are set in 21-4.a, and presbytery may omit parts of the trials only "in extraordinary cases" and by **three-fourths approval** (21-4.c). He must **state any differences with the Standards**; exceptions are granted only if "neither hostile to the system nor strikes at the vitals of religion" (21-4.f, g). |
| 1 | What vows must he take? | BCO 21-5 (the **eight questions for ordination**): (1) Do you believe the Scriptures of the OT and NT, **as originally given, to be the inerrant Word of God**, the only infallible rule of faith and practice? (2) Do you **sincerely receive and adopt the Confession of Faith and the Catechisms** as containing the **system of doctrine** taught in Scripture, and promise that if you find yourself **out of accord with any of the fundamentals** of this system you will, **on your own initiative, make known to your Presbytery** the change? (3) Do you **approve the form of government and discipline** of the PCA, in conformity with the general principles of biblical polity? (4) Do you promise **subjection to your brethren in the Lord**? (5) Have you been induced to seek the office from **love to God and a sincere desire to promote his glory** in the gospel? (6) Do you promise to be **zealous and faithful in maintaining the truths of the Gospel and the purity and peace and unity of the Church**, whatever persecution may arise? (7) Do you engage to be **faithful and diligent in all your duties** as a Christian and minister, adorning the gospel in your manner of life? (8) Are you now willing to **take the charge of this church**, and promise to discharge the duties of a pastor? The **congregation** also answers four questions (21-6). |
| 1 | Who has the final say in establishing or dissolving a pastoral relationship? | The **presbytery**: it **establishes the pastoral relation and dissolves it** at the request of one or both of the parties, or "where the interest of religion imperatively demands it" (BCO 13-9.c). The congregation calls, but no minister may receive a call without his presbytery's permission (21-1), and a pastor "must not physically leave the field until the Presbytery... has dissolved the relation" (23-1). |
| 2 | How may a pastoral relation be dissolved? | BCO 23-1: when a minister tenders his **resignation** to presbytery, the presbytery **cites the church** to appear and show cause why it should or should not be accepted; if the church fails to appear or its reasons are insufficient, the relation is dissolved. If a **church** wants to be relieved of its pastor, a similar procedure is followed. In either case "there shall always be a **meeting of the congregation** called and conducted in the same manner as the call of the pastor." (Presbytery may also act in the interest of religion, 13-9.c, or by discipline, 34-9.) |
| 2 | What is the difference between an associate pastor and an assistant pastor? | BCO 22-2 to 22-4: the **pastor and associate pastor** are **elected by the congregation** (using the form of call in 20-6) and, being so elected, "**become members of the Session**." An **assistant pastor** is **called by the Session**, with the permission and approval of presbytery; he "is **not a member of the Session**," though he may be invited to attend without vote (12-1) and appointed on special occasions to moderate (12-4). The relation of the associate to the church is determined by the congregation, that of the assistant by the Session; both dissolutions are governed by BCO 23. **Succession:** associate and assistant pastors "may not normally succeed the senior pastor without an intervening term of service in a different field," unless the congregation petitions by secret ballot with a **four-fifths** majority and presbytery grants the exception by **three-fourths** (23-1). |
| 1 | What steps must be followed in electing ruling elders or deacons? | BCO 24-1 to 24-5: (1) At times set by the **session**, **communicant members may submit names**; each prospective officer should be an active male member meeting the qualifications of 1 Tim. 3 and Titus 1. (2) **Nominees receive instruction** and are **examined by the session**. (3) The session reports those eligible to the congregation with **at least 30 days' notice** of a **congregational meeting** for elections. (4) **Communing members in good standing** vote; a **majority of votes cast** (excluding blanks and abstentions) is required (24-3). (5) The session **ordains and installs** those elected, with a sermon, the vows of 24-6, the congregation's acknowledgment (by raised hand), prayer and the **laying on of hands**, and the right hand of fellowship. If **one-fourth** of voters request, the session must call a meeting to elect additional officers (24-1). |
| 1 | In what areas is a nominee to be examined? | BCO 24-1: (a) his **Christian experience**, especially his **personal character and family management** (1 Tim. 3:1-7; Titus 1:6-9); (b) his knowledge of **Bible content**; (c) his knowledge of the **system of doctrine, government, and discipline** in the Constitution; (d) the **duties of the office** nominated to; and (e) his **willingness to assent to the questions required for ordination** (24-6). The session may judge him ineligible at any point on the matter of Christian experience. The six **ordination vows for ruling elders and deacons** parallel the minister's (24-6). |

## F. Congregational Meetings; Amending the Constitution (Chapters 25-26)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | How may a congregational meeting be called? | "Whenever it may seem for the best interests of the church... the **Session shall call** such meeting and give **public notice of at least one week**. **No business shall be transacted** except what is stated in the notice" (BCO 25-2). The session **must** call one when **requested in writing** by: **one-fourth** of the communing members (churches of up to 100 members); **one-fifth** (101-300); **one-sixth** (301-500); **one-seventh** (501-700); or **100** members (over 700). If the session does not act within **30 days**, any member may file a complaint (25-2). |
| 1 | Who can vote? | "The congregation consists of **all the communing members** of a particular church, and **they only** are entitled to vote" (BCO 25-1). In elections of officers and pastors: communing members "in good and regular standing" (20-3; 24-3). |
| 2 | What officers are required? | A **moderator**: the **pastor** "by virtue of his office"; if impracticable or there is no pastor, the session appoints one of its number to open the meeting, and the congregation elects its presiding officer, who may be a minister or ruling elder of the PCA or any **male member** of that church (25-4; 24-2). A **clerk** is elected by the congregation to keep minutes and send a copy to the session (25-5). A **quorum** is **one-fourth** of the resident communing members for churches of up to 100 members, and **one-sixth** for larger ones (25-3). |
| 2 | Who owns the property of the local church? | The **particular church** (through its trustees or corporation officers, or, if unincorporated, those entitled to represent it) "shall have **sole title** to its property," and **superior courts may receive money or property only by free and voluntary action** of the local church (BCO 25-8). "All particular churches shall be entitled to hold, own and enjoy their own local properties, **without any right of reversion** whatsoever to any Presbytery, General Assembly or any other courts" (25-9). The Church "as a whole promises **never to attempt to secure possession** of the property of any congregation against its will" (25-10). (If presbytery dissolves a church and its property is not disposed of within six months, it goes to the presbytery, 25-12.) |
| 3 | How may the Book of Church Order be amended? | BCO 26-2: (1) approval by a **majority of those present and voting** in the General Assembly and its recommendation to the presbyteries; (2) the **advice and consent of two-thirds of the presbyteries**; and (3) approval and enactment by a **subsequent** General Assembly by a majority of those present and voting. Presbyteries may not divide the parts of an amendment (26-4). |
| 3 | How may the doctrinal standards be amended? | BCO 26-3: (1) approval by **three-fourths** of those present and voting in the General Assembly; (2) the advice and consent of **three-fourths of the presbyteries**; and (3) approval and enactment by a subsequent General Assembly by **three-fourths** of those present and voting. The same three-fourths process governs **organic union** with another church body (26-5). |
