## C. Church Officers (Chapters 7-9)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What permanent officers has Christ given to his Church? | Two ordinary and perpetual offices: **elder** (in two classes, **teaching elders**, also called ministers or pastors, and **ruling elders**) and **deacon** (BCO 7; Phil. 1:1; Acts 14:23; 1 Tim. 3). The extraordinary offices (apostle, prophet) were temporary (Eph. 2:20). |
| 1 | What are the qualifications for an elder? | The qualifications of **1 Tim. 3:1-7** and **Titus 1:5-9**: **above reproach**, husband of one wife, sober-minded, self-controlled, respectable, hospitable, not a drunkard or violent, not quarrelsome or a lover of money, managing his household well, not a recent convert, well thought of by outsiders, a lover of good, **holding firm the trustworthy word**. A teaching elder must be **able to teach** (1 Tim. 3:2; Titus 1:9). The PCA requires officers to be qualified men (BCO 7-2, 8; verify). Officers must also receive and adopt the Standards (the ordination vows, BCO 21-5 for teaching elders; ruling elders and deacons take parallel vows). |
| 1 | What are an elder's duties? | **Shepherd and oversee** the flock (Acts 20:28; 1 Pet. 5:1-4), **rule** (1 Tim. 5:17; Heb. 13:17), **guard doctrine** (Titus 1:9), **pray for and visit the sick** (James 5:14), **admit, instruct, and discipline** members, and take part in the courts of the church. The teaching elder in addition **preaches the Word, administers the sacraments, and leads worship** (1 Tim. 4:13-16; 5:17; 2 Tim. 4:2). |
| 1 | How are ruling and teaching elders alike? How are they different? | **Alike:** both are **elders** (one office, two classes): same qualifications in principle, same authority to rule, same vote in the courts, ordained, **installed** (BCO 8; Acts 15:6; 1 Tim. 5:17). **Different:** the **teaching elder** is called to preach and teach, administer the sacraments, and give his full time to the ministry of the Word, and is a member of the **presbytery**; the **ruling elder** is elected by a congregation from among its members, serves primarily in rule and oversight, and is a member of the **session**. |
| 1 | What is meant by the "parity" of ruling and teaching elders? | **Parity** means that they are **equal in rank and authority** in government and discipline: a ruling elder's vote counts as much as a teaching elder's in every court, and both are **elders** (Acts 15:2, 6, 22; 1 Tim. 5:17). Their **functions** differ. This is a hallmark of Presbyterian government over against episcopacy. |
| 1 | What are the duties of deacons? | To **minister to the needy, the sick, and the suffering** in the church, and to oversee the church's **temporal and material affairs** as the session directs (Acts 6:1-6; BCO 9). The deacons **serve under the oversight and direction of the session**; they do not rule and are not members of the session (but may be invited to attend). |
| 1 | What are the qualifications for deacons? | **1 Tim. 3:8-13** and Acts 6:3: **dignified**, not double-tongued, not addicted to much wine or greedy for dishonest gain, holding the **mystery of the faith** with a clear conscience, **tested** first, **blameless**, husband of one wife, managing children and household well; Acts 6:3: "of good repute, full of the Spirit and of wisdom." The PCA limits the office to qualified men; **women** serve in unordained roles (verify; see the [Church and the Spirit](../theology/church-spirit.md) page). |

## D. Church Courts (Chapters 10-15)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name the courts of the church. | The **session** (of a particular church), the **presbytery** (of a region), and the **General Assembly** (of the whole denomination). (Some denominations also have a **synod**, which the PCA does not.) Each is made up of elders and acts as a **court of review** over the one below it (BCO 10-11; Acts 15). |
| 1 | What is the proper or original jurisdiction of each? | **Session:** over the **particular church** (its members, its ruling elders and deacons, its worship, its discipline, its property and finances). **Presbytery:** over the **teaching elders** of its bounds and over the **churches** in its bounds (ordination, licensure, installation, organizing churches, review of session records). **General Assembly:** over **matters common to the whole church** (doctrine, order, the permanent committees and agencies) and as the **final court of appeal**; it exercises original jurisdiction over presbyteries and some other bodies as the BCO provides. |
| 1 | What officers must each court have? How are they chosen? | Each court has a **moderator** (to preside) and a **clerk** (to keep the minutes). **Session:** the moderator is the **pastor** (or a teaching elder appointed); the clerk is a ruling elder elected by the session. **Presbytery:** moderator and clerk elected by the presbytery. **General Assembly:** a **moderator** elected annually by the commissioners and a **stated clerk** (verify the particulars in BCO 12-14). |
| 1 | Who are the members of the church session? What are the main duties of the session? | **Members:** the **pastor(s)** (teaching elders called to that church) and the **ruling elders** of the congregation (BCO 12). **Duties:** to **govern the congregation**: admit, dismiss, and discipline members; **oversee worship** (including who preaches); **guard the doctrine** and teaching; **oversee the deacons**, finances, and property; **call congregational meetings**; **keep records**; elect delegates to presbytery; and **care for the flock** (Acts 20:28; Heb. 13:17). |
| 1 | Who are the members of presbytery? | All the **teaching elders** whose membership is in that presbytery, and **one ruling elder from each church** of its bounds (BCO 13). |
| 1 | To what church does a minister belong? | A **teaching elder belongs to the presbytery**, not to a particular church: his **membership is in the presbytery**, and he is called to work in a church **under presbytery's oversight**. (Ruling elders and deacons are members of their local church.) |
| 2 | How is an ordained minister received into a presbytery? | **From another presbytery of the PCA** by a letter of **transfer** (dismissal) and the receiving presbytery's vote. **From another denomination**, he must be **examined** by the presbytery (in the areas of ordination: his Christian experience, call, theology and the Standards, Bible, government, and sacraments), and he must **adopt the Standards** and take the **ordination vows**; the presbytery decides by vote (BCO 13; 21-4; verify). |
| 2 | How is a church received into a presbytery? | By **petition to presbytery**: either a new church **organized by the presbytery** from a mission church, or an **existing congregation** from another body that applies, **adopts the PCA's Standards and BCO**, and is **approved by the presbytery** (BCO 13; verify the procedure and the examination of its officers). |
| 1 | What are the main duties and responsibilities of the presbytery? | To **ordain, license, install, and discipline teaching elders**, to **organize, supervise, and dissolve churches**, to **examine candidates**, to **review the records of sessions**, to **hear appeals and complaints**, to **resolve questions** of doctrine and order referred to it, to **elect commissioners** to the General Assembly, and to **oversee mission work** in its bounds (BCO 13). |
| 2 | What are the main duties and responsibilities of the General Assembly? | The General Assembly is the **highest court**: it **decides** questions of doctrine and discipline brought to it, serves as the **final court of appeal**, **reviews the records of presbyteries**, **proposes and enacts amendments** to the Constitution under the BCO, **elects and oversees** the **permanent committees and agencies**, receives **overtures**, and **speaks for the denomination** on matters within its authority (BCO 14). |
| 3 | What are the permanent committees and agencies of the General Assembly? | The Assembly carries out its work through **permanent committees**, in the PCA these include **Mission to the World** (overseas missions), **Mission to North America** (church planting and ministry in North America), the **Committee on Discipleship Ministries** (formerly Christian Education and Publications), **Reformed University Ministries**, and the **Administrative Committee**, along with **agencies** such as **Covenant College** and **Covenant Theological Seminary** and the Committee on **Retirement and Benefits**. (The list changes; verify the current one.) |
| 3 | What is a committee of commissioners? | A group of **commissioners to the General Assembly**, elected from the presbyteries, who meet during the Assembly to **review the work of a permanent committee or agency** and bring **recommendations** to the Assembly. |
| 3 | How do a committee of commissioners and a permanent committee or agency differ? | The **permanent committee or agency** does the **ongoing work** between Assemblies (missions, education, and so on) under the Assembly's direction; the **committee of commissioners** is a **temporary reviewing body at the Assembly** that evaluates that work, hears from the committee, and **reports to the Assembly**. It checks the committee; it does not run the work. |
| 2 | What is the difference between a committee and a commission? | A **committee** studies, investigates, and **reports with recommendations**; it has **no authority to decide** for the court. A **commission** is given the **court's own authority to act or decide** on a particular matter (for example, a judicial case or a pressing matter between meetings) and **its decisions are the court's decisions**, subject to the usual review (BCO 15). |
