## C. Church Officers (Chapters 7-9)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What permanent officers has Christ given to his Church? | "The ordinary and perpetual classes of office in the Church are **elders and deacons**. Within the class of elder are the two orders of **teaching elders and ruling elders**. The elders jointly have the government and spiritual oversight of the Church, including teaching... The office of deacon is not one of rule, but rather of **service** both to the physical and spiritual needs of the people. In accord with Scripture, these offices are **open to men only**" (BCO 7-2; 1-4; Phil. 1:1; Acts 14:23). The extraordinary offices (apostles and others related to new revelation) "have no successors" (7-1). |
| 1 | What are the qualifications for an elder? | BCO 8-2: "a competency of human learning," **blameless in life, sound in the faith and apt to teach**; sobriety and holiness of life becoming the gospel; conforming to the biblical requirement of **chastity and sexual purity** in his self-description, convictions, character, and conduct; ruling his own house well; "a good report of them that are outside the Church." See also 1 Tim. 3:1-7; Titus 1:5-9, and 16-4 (officers must be "above reproach," affirming the sinfulness of fallen desires and the hope of progressive sanctification). Only those specially gifted, called, and trained to preach may serve as teaching elders (7-2). |
| 1 | What are an elder's duties? | BCO 8-3: "to **watch diligently over the flock**... that no corruption of doctrine or of morals enter therein"; to **exercise government and discipline**; to take oversight of the spiritual interests of the church and of the Church generally; to **visit the people at their homes, especially the sick**; to instruct the ignorant, comfort the mourner, nourish and guard the children; to be zealous to evangelize and make disciples, and show hospitality; and to **pray with and for the people**. The teaching elder in addition is to "**feed the flock by reading, expounding and preaching the Word of God and to administer the Sacraments**" (8-5; Acts 20:28; 1 Pet. 5:1-4). |
| 1 | How are ruling and teaching elders alike? How are they different? | **Alike:** both are **one office**, elder: "Elders being of one class of office, ruling elders possess the **same authority and eligibility to office in the courts** of the Church as teaching elders" (8-10); both share government, oversight, and discipline (8-3; 7-2). The titles bishop, presbyter, and teacher "do not indicate different grades of office, but all describe one and the same office" (8-1). **Different:** the teaching elder, specially gifted and trained to preach, has the added duties of **reading, expounding, and preaching the Word and administering the sacraments** (8-5); he holds his membership in **presbytery** (13-2); the ruling elder is elected by a congregation from among its members (8-9) and sits on the **session** of that church. Ruling elders should cultivate their aptness to teach (8-10). |
| 1 | What is meant by the "parity" of ruling and teaching elders? | They are **equal in authority and eligibility** in the courts of the church: "ruling elders possess the same authority and eligibility to office in the courts of the Church as teaching elders" (BCO 8-10). A ruling elder's vote in session, presbytery, and Assembly counts the same as a teaching elder's. Functions differ; the office does not (8-1). |
| 1 | What are the duties of deacons? | BCO 9-2: "to **minister to those who are in need, to the sick, to the friendless, and to any who may be in distress**"; to develop liberality in the members, devise methods of collecting gifts, and distribute them; to have **care of the property** of the congregation. They are "under the **supervision and authority of the Session**" (9-2). The office is "one of **sympathy and service**, after the example of the Lord Jesus" (9-1). Deacons are organized as a **Board** and meet at least quarterly (9-4). Men and women may be appointed by the session to **assist** the deacons, but "are not officers of the church" and are not ordained (9-7). |
| 1 | What are the qualifications for deacons? | BCO 9-3: "men of **spiritual character, honest repute, exemplary lives, brotherly spirit, warm sympathies, and sound judgment**," conforming to the biblical requirement of chastity and sexual purity. See also Acts 6:3 and 1 Tim. 3:8-13. The office is open to men only (7-2). |
| 3 | What are evangelists, and are there other special classes of teaching elder? | A teaching elder appointed to labor as an **evangelist** in foreign countries or where there are no other PCA churches within reasonable distance is commissioned for a **renewable twelve-month term** to preach, administer the sacraments, receive and dismiss members of mission churches, and train potential officers (8-6). Teaching elders may also be approved to labor with organizations outside the PCA, including as chaplains (8-7, 8-8). |

## D. Church Courts (Chapters 10-15)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name the courts of the church. | "These courts are church **Sessions, Presbyteries, and the General Assembly**" (BCO 10-2). They are "in regular gradation," and are "all, nevertheless, Presbyteries, as being composed exclusively of presbyters" (10-1). |
| 1 | What is the proper or original jurisdiction of each? | "The **Session** exercises jurisdiction over a **single church**, the **Presbytery** over what is common to the **ministers, Sessions, and churches within a prescribed district**, and the **General Assembly** over such matters as concern the **whole Church**" (BCO 11-4). **Original jurisdiction** (the right first to hear and decide) in relation to **ministers** is in the **presbytery** of which he is a member; in relation to **church members** it is in the **session** of the church to which they belong (31-1). Each court has exclusive original jurisdiction over matters especially belonging to it, and lower courts are subject to the **review and control** of higher courts (11-4). All courts are "one in nature" and differ only as the Constitution provides (11-3). |
| 1 | What officers must each court have? How are they chosen? | A **moderator** and a **clerk** (10-3, 10-4). **Session:** "the pastor is, by virtue of his office, the moderator" (12-2); if there is no pastor, a minister appointed by presbytery with the session's consent, or one invited by the session, or one of its own members (12-3). **Presbytery:** the moderator is elected at each stated meeting, or for up to one year (10-3). **General Assembly:** the moderator is chosen at each stated meeting (10-3). **Clerks** are elected by the session, presbytery, and General Assembly "to serve for a definite period as determined by the court" (10-4). |
| 1 | Who are the members of the church session? What are the main duties of the session? | **Members:** "the pastor, associate pastor(s), if there be any, and the **ruling elders**" (12-1). Assistant pastors are not members but may be invited to attend and participate without vote (12-1). **Duties** (12-5): to maintain the **spiritual government** of the church: inquire into the knowledge and conduct of members and **censure** the delinquent; **receive and dismiss members**; see that parents present their children for baptism; **examine, ordain, and install ruling elders and deacons**, and oversee the deacons' records; approve the budget and actions of special importance affecting property; **call congregational meetings**; control Sunday schools, Bible classes, and groups in the church; promote missions; exercise authority over the **time and place of worship**, the music, and the use of the building; ensure the Word is preached "only by such men as are sufficiently qualified"; and carry out the injunctions of higher courts. The session meets at least **quarterly** and keeps records for **annual review by presbytery** (12-6, 12-7). |
| 1 | Who are the members of presbytery? | "The Presbytery consists of **all the teaching elders and churches within its bounds** that have been accepted by the Presbytery. When the Presbytery meets as a court it shall comprise **all teaching elders and ruling elders as elected by their Session**." Each congregation is entitled to **two ruling elder representatives for the first 350 communing members** or fraction thereof, and **one additional ruling elder for each additional 500** (BCO 13-1). |
| 1 | To what church does a minister belong? | To the **presbytery**, not a local church: "A minister shall be required to hold his **membership in the Presbytery** within whose geographical bounds he resides" (BCO 13-2). A minister is ordinarily received only on a **call to a definite work** within the presbytery's bounds (13-5). (When deposed or divested he is assigned to membership in a particular church, 46-8.) |
| 2 | How is an ordained minister received into a presbytery? | **From another PCA presbytery** (13-6a): he is examined on **Christian experience** (including personal character and family management) and on his views in **theology, the sacraments, and church government**. **From another denomination** (13-6b): the presbytery examines him "thoroughly as required by the trials listed in BCO 21-4" and requires him to answer affirmatively the **ordination questions**. In every case he must state the **specific instances in which he differs** with the Confession and Catechisms, and the court judges those differences (13-6c; 21-4.f-g). Ministers subscribe the ordination obligations (13-7). |
| 2 | How is a church received into a presbytery? | The presbytery designates a **commission** to meet with the church's ruling elders "to make certain that the elders understand and can sincerely adopt the doctrines and polity of the Presbyterian Church in America"; in the commission's presence the ruling elders must "**answer affirmatively the questions required of officers at their ordination**" (BCO 13-8). A non-PCA church may instead come in as a **mission church** (5-2b). A new church is organized only by presbytery's authority (5-9). |
| 1 | What are the main duties and responsibilities of the presbytery? | BCO 13-9: it receives and issues **appeals, complaints, and references**; **receives candidates, examines and licenses** them, and **receives, dismisses, ordains, installs, removes, and judges ministers**; **reviews the records of sessions**; **establishes and dissolves the pastoral relation**; sets apart evangelists; sees that higher courts' injunctions are obeyed; **condemns erroneous opinions**, visits churches, **unites, divides, forms, receives, and dissolves churches**; devises measures to enlarge the church; and proposes measures to the Assembly. It meets at least **twice a year** (13-12) and reports annually to the General Assembly (13-11). |
| 2 | What are the main duties and responsibilities of the General Assembly? | The General Assembly is "the **highest court**" and "the bond of union, peace and correspondence" among all the courts and congregations (BCO 14-1). It has power (14-6): to **receive and issue appeals, references, and complaints**; to **bear testimony** against error and decide controversies of doctrine and discipline; to give **advice and instruction**; to **review the records of presbyteries**; to **erect, unite, and divide presbyteries**; to **institute and superintend** the agencies for evangelization; to receive other ecclesiastical bodies (with the consent of **three-fourths of the presbyteries**); to correspond with other churches; and to superintend the whole church. It consists of **all teaching elders in good standing** and **ruling elders elected by their sessions** (same formula as presbytery) (14-2). Its judicial decisions bind the parties and are to be given "due and serious consideration" in similar cases (14-7). |
| 3 | What are the permanent committees and agencies of the General Assembly? | BCO 14-1 (principle 12): the permanent committees are the **Administrative Committee**, the **Committee on Discipleship Ministries**, the **Committee on Mission to North America**, the **Committee on Mission to the World**, and the **Committee on Reformed University Fellowship**. The agencies represented on the Administrative Committee include **Covenant College, Covenant Theological Seminary, the PCA Foundation, Geneva Benefits Group, and Ridge Haven Conference Center**. The Assembly also elects a **Standing Judicial Commission** (15-4) and a **Theological Examining Committee** that examines first- and second-level administrative officers (14-1, principle 14). |
| 3 | What is a committee of commissioners? | The body through which business ordinarily reaches the floor of the Assembly: "All business shall ordinarily come to the floor of the Assembly for final action through **committees of commissioners**," except reports of the Standing Judicial Commission, the Committee on Constitutional Business, the Committee on Review of Presbytery Records, the Nominating Committee, and ad interim committees (BCO 14-1, principle 15). A committee of commissioners is made up of commissioners to that Assembly and reviews the work of a permanent committee or agency and brings recommendations. |
| 3 | How do a committee of commissioners and a permanent committee or agency differ? | The **permanent committees and agencies** carry out the Assembly's work between meetings: "The Assembly's committees are to **serve and not to direct** any Church judicatories. They are not to establish policy, but rather **execute policy** established by the General Assembly" (14-1, principle 7). A **committee of commissioners** is a temporary body of commissioners that reviews their work at the Assembly and reports for the Assembly's action. |
| 2 | What is the difference between a committee and a commission? | "A **committee** is appointed to **examine, consider, and report**, a **commission** is authorized to **deliberate upon and conclude** the business referred to it" (BCO 15-1). Commissions may take testimony in judicial cases, ordain and install ministers, visit disordered churches, and organize new churches (15-2). A presbytery commission has at least **two teaching elders and two ruling elders** (15-2). A commission keeps a record submitted to the court that appointed it (15-1). The General Assembly's **Standing Judicial Commission** (24 members) is the final judicial body for cases (15-4, 15-5). |
