# Church Government

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

This section follows Bryan Chapell and Jim Meek, ***Preparing for Licensure and Ordination Exams*** (PCA Christian Education and Publications, 2002), section IV, "Study Questions on Church Government and Discipline." Chapell's questions follow the order of the chapters of the **Book of Church Order (BCO)**, and so do these pages. The BCO has three parts: the **Form of Government** (chapters 1-26), the **Rules of Discipline** (27-46), and the **Directory for Worship** (47-63).

!!! info "Checked against the BCO text"
    These answers were checked against the text of the BCO published by the PCA's Administrative Committee at pcaac.org, which includes amendments through the 53rd General Assembly (2026). The BCO is amended almost every year, so **check the current text** and your presbytery's manual before the exam. Section numbers (for example BCO 21-4) are cited so you can look each one up.

## If Time Is Short: Study in Tiers

Each question on these pages carries a **Tier** number in its first column. The tiers are a study judgment: what examiners ask most often and what everything else builds on.

!!! success "Tier 1: know cold"
    **Christ is the only head** and Scripture the only rule; the **key principles** of Presbyterian government; the **three kinds of officers** and their qualifications and duties; **parity** of elders; the **three courts** and what each does; **how a man is called, examined, and ordained**; the **goal and steps** of discipline (Matt. 18) and the **censures**; and the **elements of worship** and who admits to the Lord's Supper.

!!! warning "Tier 2: second pass"
    The **powers of church and state compared**; **mission churches**; the **members of each court** and how churches and ministers are received; **committees** versus **commissions**; **candidates, licentiates, and internship**; **dissolving a pastoral relation**; **congregational meetings** and **property**; and **review and control** (reference, appeal, complaint).

!!! note "Tier 3: if time allows"
    Details of amendment procedures; the **associate member**; the **permanent committees** of the General Assembly; and the finer points of procedure.

## A. Preface and Principles (Preface and Chapter 1)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who is the head of the church? How does he exercise his authority? | **Jesus Christ**, "the King and Head of the Church" (Preface I; Eph. 1:20-23; Col. 1:18). "It belongs to His Majesty from His throne of glory to **rule and teach the Church through His Word and Spirit by the ministry of men**; thus **mediately** exercising His own authority and enforcing His own laws." He has given the Church "officers, oracles and ordinances," and has ordained its doctrine, government, discipline, and worship, "either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary inference" (Preface I). |
| 1 | List at least two key principles of Presbyterian church government. | From the **Preliminary Principles** (Preface II) and BCO 1: (1) **God alone is Lord of the conscience** (PP 1). (2) The Church may declare the terms of communion and the qualifications of officers (PP 2). (3) Christ has appointed **officers** to preach, administer the sacraments, and **exercise discipline** (PP 3). (4) The power to **elect officers** resides in the society, that is, in the people (PP 6; BCO 3-1). (5) "All church power... is **only ministerial and declarative**," Scripture being the only rule; no judicatory may make laws to bind the conscience (PP 7). (6) Church discipline is **moral or spiritual** with no civil effect (PP 8). (7) Government is by **teaching and ruling elders and deacons**, in **courts** in which "ecclesiastical jurisdiction is not a several, but a joint power" (BCO 1-4, 1-5). |
| 1 | Can the church "bind" men's consciences? Why or why not? | **No.** "God alone is Lord of the conscience and has left it free from any doctrines or commandments of men" that are contrary to his Word or, in faith and worship, not governed by it (PP 1). "All church power... is only ministerial and declarative since the Holy Scriptures are the only rule of faith and practice. **No church judicatory may make laws to bind the conscience.** All church courts may err" (PP 7; BCO 11-2: courts "can make no laws binding the conscience," but may frame symbols of faith, bear testimony against error, decide cases of conscience, and make rules for government and worship "agreeable to" Scripture). Courts do have the right to require obedience to the laws of Christ (BCO 11-2). |
| 1 | What is the Constitution of the Presbyterian Church in America? | The Constitution, "**subject to and subordinate to the Scriptures of the Old and New Testaments, the inerrant Word of God**," consists of its **doctrinal standards** (the Westminster Confession of Faith with the Larger and Shorter Catechisms) and the **Book of Church Order**, comprising the **Form of Government, the Rules of Discipline, and the Directory for Worship** (Preface III; BCO 26-1). |

## B. The Church and Its Members (Chapters 2-6)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the visible church? Who are its members? | BCO 2-1: the visible church, "before the law, under the law, and now under the Gospel, is one and the same and consists of **all those who make profession of their faith in the Lord Jesus Christ, together with their children**." (BCO 1-3 adds: and "promise submission to His laws.") Denominations that "maintain the Word and Sacraments in their fundamental integrity are to be recognized as **true branches** of the Church of Jesus Christ" (2-2). |
| 2 | What is the power of the church? How does it differ from that of the state? | The power Christ gave the church vests in the **whole body**, rulers and ruled, "a spiritual commonwealth," and extends to **choosing its officers** (3-1). It is **wholly spiritual** and **twofold**: the **power of order** (officers acting severally: preaching, sacraments, reproof, visiting the sick) and the **power of jurisdiction** (jointly in church courts) (3-2). The church's sole functions are "to **proclaim, to administer, and to enforce** the law of Christ revealed in the Scriptures" (3-3). "The power of the Church is **exclusively spiritual**; that of the State includes the exercise of **force**." The church may not frame a government for the state, nor the state a creed or polity for the church, "as planets moving in concentric orbits" (3-4; Matt. 22:21). Church courts have "no power to inflict temporal pains and penalties" (11-1). |
| 1 | What is a particular church? | "A particular church consists of **a number of professing Christians, with their children, associated together for divine worship and godly living, agreeable to the Scriptures, and submitting to the lawful government of Christ's kingdom**" (BCO 4-1). Its officers are its teaching and ruling elders and deacons (4-2), and its jurisdiction is lodged in the **session** (4-3). |
| 2 | What is a mission church? | A church that is described as a particular church (4-1) but "has **no permanent governing body**, and thus must be governed or supervised by others"; its goal is to mature and be **organized as a particular church** (5-1). Mission churches are ordinarily established by **presbyteries** (5-2). Presbytery provides a **temporary government**: an **evangelist** (8-6), a **mother-daughter** arrangement with a particular church's session, or a **commission** acting as a temporary session (5-3). A new church can be organized **only by the authority of presbytery** (5-9). |
| 1 | What two kinds of members does the church have? What are their respective rights and privileges? | **Non-communing members:** "the children of believers... through the covenant and by right of birth" (6-1); entitled to **baptism** and to "the pastoral oversight, instruction and government of the church"; all baptized persons are entitled to the church's watchful care even if adults who have not professed faith (6-3). **Communing members:** those who "have made a **profession of faith** in Christ, have been **baptized**, and have been **admitted by the Session to the Lord's Table**" (6-2). **Only communing members** are entitled to **all the rights and privileges** of the church (6-4), including voting in congregational meetings (25-1) and holding office. |
