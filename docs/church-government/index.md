# Church Government

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

This section follows Bryan Chapell and Jim Meek, ***Preparing for Licensure and Ordination Exams*** (PCA Christian Education and Publications, 2002), section IV, "Study Questions on Church Government and Discipline." Chapell's questions follow the order of the chapters of the **Book of Church Order (BCO)**, and so do these pages. The BCO has three parts: the **Form of Government** (chapters 1-26), the **Rules of Discipline** (27-46), and the **Directory for Worship** (47-63).

!!! warning "Verify against the current BCO"
    The BCO is amended almost every year. These answers state the principles and the usual procedures. Always check the exact wording, vote thresholds, notice periods, and deadlines against the **current BCO** and your presbytery's manual before the exam. Where a detail is the kind that changes, the answer says **(verify)**. Chapter numbers below are Chapell's.

| **Church government** | "Basic knowledge of the government of the Presbyterian Church in America as defined in the Book of Church Order" | "The principles and rules of the government and discipline of the church" |

Are you aware of any of its principles about which you have reservations?* Read the whole BCO, with the Preface and the Preliminary Principles, at least once. Keep a list of any point you question, and be ready to state it honestly.

## If Time Is Short: Study in Tiers

Each question on these pages carries a **Tier** number in its first column. The tiers are a study judgment: what examiners ask most often and what everything else builds on.

!!! success "Tier 1: know cold"
    **Christ is the only head** and Scripture the only rule; the **key principles** of Presbyterian government; the **three kinds of officers** and their qualifications and duties; **parity** of elders; the **three courts** and what each does; **how a man is called, examined, and ordained**; the **goal and steps** of discipline (Matt. 18) and the **censures**; and the **elements of worship** and who admits to the Lord's Supper.

!!! warning "Tier 2: second pass"
    The **powers of church and state compared**; **mission churches**; the **members of each court** and how churches and ministers are received; **committees** versus **commissions**; **candidates, licentiates, and internship**; **dissolving a pastoral relation**; **congregational meetings** and **property**; and **review and control** (reference, appeal, complaint).

!!! note "Tier 3: if time allows"
    Details of amendment procedures; the **associate member**; the **permanent committees** of the General Assembly; and the finer points of procedure.

## A. Preface and Principles (Preface and Chapter 1)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who is the head of the church? How does he exercise his authority? | **Jesus Christ alone** (Eph. 1:22; 4:15; 5:23; Col. 1:18; WCF 25.6). He is the church's King and Lord, and he exercises his authority **through his Word and Spirit**, by the officers he has appointed, who rule **ministerially**: they speak and apply what he has revealed, and do not make laws of their own (Matt. 28:18-20; 16:19; Eph. 4:11-16; 1 Pet. 5:1-4; Heb. 13:17). |
| 1 | List at least two key principles of Presbyterian church government. | (1) **Christ is the only head and Scripture the only rule** of faith and practice (Eph. 1:22; Isa. 8:20). (2) **Plurality and parity of elders**: every church is governed by a group of elders, equal in office (Acts 14:23; 20:17, 28; Titus 1:5-7). (3) **Representation**: officers are chosen by the people to rule on their behalf (Acts 6:3-6; 14:23). (4) **Church courts and connection**: churches are bound together in a series of courts, with review and appeal (Acts 15). (5) **Ministerial power**: the church's power is moral and spiritual, not civil (John 18:36). (6) **Majority rule** in the courts, within the bounds of Scripture. |
| 1 | Can the church "bind" men's consciences? Why or why not? | **No**, not beyond Scripture: **God alone is Lord of the conscience** (WCF 20.2; Matt. 15:9; Rom. 14:4; James 4:12), and the church's authority is **ministerial and declarative**, limited to what God's Word teaches (WCF 31.3; BCO Preliminary Principles). Members are bound to obey the church's lawful decisions **as they agree with Scripture** and for the Lord's sake (Heb. 13:17); the church may also set rules for decent order (1 Cor. 14:40). |
| 1 | What is the Constitution of the Presbyterian Church in America? | The **Constitution** consists of the **doctrinal standards**, the **Westminster Confession of Faith** with the **Larger and Shorter Catechisms** (the PCA's "system of doctrine"), and the **Book of Church Order**, made up of the Form of Government, the Rules of Discipline, and the Directory for Worship (BCO Preface; Preamble). The Standards are subordinate to Scripture. |

## B. The Church and Its Members (Chapters 2-6)

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the visible church? Who are its members? | The **visible church** is made up of all throughout the world who **profess the true religion, together with their children** (BCO 2; WCF 25.2; Acts 2:39; 1 Cor. 7:14). It is **mixed** (Matt. 13:24-30). Its members are **professing believers and their baptized children**. See also the [theology answer](../theology/church-spirit.md). |
| 2 | What is the power of the church? How does it differ from that of the state? | The church's power is **spiritual, ministerial, and declarative** (the keys of the kingdom, Matt. 16:19; 18:18); it is exercised by **persuasion, teaching, and discipline up to excommunication**, and never by the sword. The **state's** power is **civil and coercive** (Rom. 13:4). The church does not hold civil jurisdiction; the state does not govern the church's doctrine, worship, or discipline (John 18:36; Matt. 22:21; WCF 23.3; 31.4). |
| 1 | What is a particular church? | A **particular church** is "a number of professing Christians, with their children, associated together for divine worship and godly living, agreeably to the Holy Scriptures, and submitting to a certain form of government" (BCO 4-1, in the traditional Presbyterian wording; verify). It is a visible part of the whole church. |
| 2 | What is a mission church? | A **mission church** is a group of believers gathered for worship and growth that **is not yet organized** as a particular church (no session of its own); it is **under the oversight** of a presbytery (or a session acting for it) until it is ready to be organized (BCO 5; verify). |
| 1 | What two kinds of members does the church have? What are their respective rights and privileges? | **Communing** members: baptized persons who have made a **public profession of faith**, been received by the session, and are admitted to the Lord's Supper; they may **vote** in congregational meetings and may be elected **to office**. **Non-communing** members: **baptized children** and others baptized but not yet making a profession; they are under the **care, instruction, and discipline** of the church, but may not take the Lord's Supper, vote, or hold office until they profess faith and are admitted (BCO 6; verify). |
