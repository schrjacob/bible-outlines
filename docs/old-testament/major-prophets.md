# Major Prophets

!!! info "Exam level: Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Daniel need outlines"
    Chapell expects **outlines** for Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Daniel. Lamentations needs only a **summary**. Learn the top-level divisions first, then the key chapters.

## Isaiah (66)

*Salvation Is of the LORD*

Isaiah prophesied in Jerusalem c. 740-681 BC, under Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah (1:1).

### Themes

- "The Holy One of Israel"
- Tension between God's judgment on sin and his promise of redemption
- The messianic King and the suffering Servant
- Authorship: a "Deutero-" or "Trito-Isaiah" is unnecessary; Isaiah can prophesy Cyrus by name (44:28; 45:1). The NT quotes all parts of the book as Isaiah's (e.g., John 12:38-41).

### Outline

- **1-39** Prophecies about events in Isaiah's lifetime (judgment; Assyria)
    - **1-5** Judah's sins condemned (Song of the Vineyard, **5**)
    - **6** Isaiah's commissioning and vision of God ("Holy, holy, holy," 6:3)
    - **7-12** The "Book of Immanuel": God's grace through judgment (Syro-Ephraimite war)
        - **7:14** The virgin shall conceive... Immanuel
        - **9:6-7** "To us a child is born... Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God"
        - **11:1-10** The shoot from the stump of Jesse
    - **13-27** Oracles against the nations
    - **28-35** Woes to the wicked and promise of future glory (trust the LORD, not Egypt)
    - **36-39** Historical transition: Hezekiah's deliverance from Sennacherib; envoys from Babylon
- **40-55** The "Book of Comfort" for God's exiles (Babylonian captivity)
    - **40** "Comfort, comfort my people"; a voice in the wilderness (40:3); "they shall mount up with wings like eagles" (40:31)
    - **44:28-45:1** Cyrus, the LORD's anointed
    - The Servant Songs:
        - **42:1-4** "He will not cry aloud or lift up his voice..."
        - **49:1-6** "I will make you as a light for the nations..."
        - **50:4-11** "I gave my back to those who strike..."
        - **52:13-53:12** "He was pierced for our transgressions..."
    - **55** "Come, everyone who thirsts"
- **56-66** Preparing for the coming glory (prophecies for all times until the end)
    - **61:1-2** The messianic mission ("The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me"; read by Jesus in Luke 4)
    - **65:17; 66:22** New heavens and new earth

## Jeremiah (52)

*God's Judgment on Covenant Infidelity and Worldwide Sin; Restoration through a New Covenant*

The "weeping prophet," c. 627-586 BC, from Josiah to the fall of Jerusalem.

### Themes

- The new covenant (31)
- God's sovereignty over Judah, Babylon, and all nations
- Call to repentance and return to the LORD

### Outline

- **1** Call of Jeremiah ("Before I formed you in the womb I knew you," 1:5)
- **2-25** Prophecies and warnings to Judah
    - **2-6** Indictment of Judah for idolatry and covenant unfaithfulness ("broken cisterns," 2:13)
    - **7-10** Temple sermon: warning against false security in ritual
    - **11-20** Jeremiah's struggles: his "confessions" and complaints to God; potter and clay (**18**)
    - **21-25** Judgment on kings and false prophets; the righteous Branch (23:5-6); 70 years of exile (25:11)
- **26-45** Narratives of conflict and judgment
    - **26-29** Conflict with false prophets; letter to the exiles ("seek the welfare of the city," 29:7) and instruction to submit to Babylon
    - **30-33** The "Book of Consolation": the restoration of Israel and Judah foretold
        - **31:15** Rachel weeping for her children
        - **31:29-30** No longer "the fathers have eaten sour grapes..."
        - **31:31-34** Promise of the new covenant
    - **34-39** Siege and fall of Jerusalem
    - **40-45** After the fall: Gedaliah's murder, flight to Egypt, word to Baruch (**45**)
- **46-51** Prophecies against the nations (Egypt, Philistia, Moab, Ammon, Edom, Damascus, Kedar, Elam, Babylon)
- **52** Historical appendix: the fall of Jerusalem, confirming the earlier prophecies

## Lamentations (5)

*Hope in the Steadfast Love of the LORD in Tribulation*

Five poems (acrostics in 1-4) mourning the fall of Jerusalem in 586 BC.

### Themes

- Consequences of sin and disobedience
- God's covenant faithfulness despite judgment
- Sovereignty of God
- The importance of hope and lament in the Christian life

### Outline

- **1** Jerusalem's misery and confession of sin
- **2** God's wrath as the cause of destruction
- **3** Hope in the midst of affliction ("his mercies... are new every morning; great is your faithfulness," 3:22-23)
- **4** Severity of the siege and judgment
- **5** Communal prayer for restoration and repentance

### Four Parts of Biblical Lament

- **Turn** toward God in prayer, recognizing his power, presence, and character
- **Complain:** share your pain and struggles with God
- **Ask:** bring specific, bold requests based on his character and promises
- **Trust:** choose to trust in God's goodness, leading to praise despite continued pain

## Ezekiel (48)

*God's Holiness Vindicated by the Judgment and Restoration of Israel*

A priest exiled to Babylon in 597 BC; prophesied there c. 593-571 BC.

- **1-3** Inaugural vision (the glory of God on the throne-chariot) and commissioning
    - **3:16-21** The watchman
- **4-24** Judgment on Jerusalem and Judah
    - **4-11** A promise of judgment and hope; the glory departs from the temple (**10-11**)
    - **12-16** The case for judgment opened
    - **17-24** The case for judgment closed; individual responsibility (**18**, "the soul who sins shall die")
- **25-32** Oracles against the nations
- **33** The fall of Jerusalem reported (the "hinge" of the book); the watchman (33:1-9)
- **34-39** Hope after the fall of Jerusalem
    - **34** False shepherds and the true Shepherd (David)
    - **36** A new heart and a new spirit (36:26-27)
    - **37** Valley of dry bones; two sticks become one
    - **38-39** The last battle (Gog and Magog)
- **40-48** Vision of restoration
    - **40-46** The new temple; the glory returns (**43**)
    - **47-48** The river of life; the new land ("The LORD is there," 48:35)

## Daniel (12)

*God's Sovereignty over History, Kings, and Empires*

Taken to Babylon in 605 BC; served into the reign of Cyrus (1:21).

- **1-6** Daniel and his three friends at the Babylonian court (stories)
    - **1** Prologue: faithfulness in food
    - **2** Nebuchadnezzar's dream of a statue (four kingdoms; the stone kingdom)
    - **3** Nebuchadnezzar's statue and the fiery furnace
    - **4** Nebuchadnezzar humbled
    - **5** Belshazzar's feast; writing on the wall; Babylon falls (539 BC)
    - **6** Darius's decree and the lions' den
- **7-12** Daniel's visions
    - **7** Four beasts, the Ancient of Days, the Son of Man (7:13-14)
    - **8** The ram, the goat, and the little horn
    - **9** Daniel's prayer about the 70 years; Gabriel's answer (70 "weeks")
    - **10-12** Visions of the final conflict
        - **10** A heavenly messenger brings news of heavenly conflict
        - **11** A vision of future earthly conflicts
        - **12** Promise of resurrection to glory or shame (12:2); how long until the end?

The four kingdoms (2, 7) are traditionally Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, and Rome.
