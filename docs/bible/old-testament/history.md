# Historical Books

!!! tip "Exam level: book contents (summary)"
    Chapell lists Joshua through Esther under **Book Contents**: be able to say in a word or phrase what each book is about. The outlines and key chapters below are a bonus. Spend your time on the **one-line summary, key events, and dates**. See the [OT Timeline](timeline.md).

## Joshua (24)

*Faithfulness and Possession of the Promised Land*

- **1-5** Entering
- **6-12** Conquest
- **13-22** Division of the land
- **23-24** Joshua's farewell address

### Key Themes

- **21** "Not one word of all the good promises... had failed" (21:45)
- **13** "There remains yet very much land to possess" (13:1)
- **23** Enjoyment of the land conditional on obedience

### Key Chapters

- **1** Joshua's commission ("Be strong and courageous")
- **2** Rahab and the spies
- **3-4** Crossing the Jordan; memorial stones
- **6** The fall of Jericho
- **7** Achan's sin; defeat at Ai
- **8** Law read on Mts. Ebal and Gerizim
- **10** Sun stands still at Gibeon
- **24** Covenant renewal at Shechem

### Key Verses

- **1:5-6** "I will not leave you or forsake you. Be strong and courageous..."
- **24:15** "Choose this day whom you will serve... But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."

## Judges (21)

*Failure, Depravity, and the Need for a King*

- **1-2** Israel forsakes the LORD
- **3-16** Outward oppression
- **17-21** Inward idolatry and immorality

**Recurring cycle** (2:11-19):

1. **Sin:** Israel does evil and serves the Baals.
2. **Sorrow:** God hands them over to oppressors.
3. **Supplication:** The people cry out for mercy.
4. **Salvation:** God raises up a judge (deliverer).
5. **Silence:** The land has rest until the judge dies, and the cycle repeats (usually worse than before).

### Key Chapters and Figures

- **2** Failure to drive out Canaan's inhabitants
- **3** **Ehud:** left-handed victory over Eglon
- **4-5** **Deborah and Barak; Jael and Sisera**
- **6-8** **Gideon:** fleece, 300 men, fall into idolatry
- **11** **Jephthah:** rash vow
- **13-16** **Samson:** "failed" judge who represents Israel; gifted by God but led astray by his eyes
- **17-21** The "double epilogue"
    - Religious anarchy (Micah's idol; Danite migration)
    - Moral anarchy (the Levite's concubine; civil war with Benjamin)

### Key Verse

- **21:25** "In those days there was no king in Israel. Everyone did what was right in his own eyes."

## Ruth (4)

*God's Providence and Covenant Kindness*

Set "in the days when the judges ruled" (1:1).

- **1** Ruth's wise choice: "Your people shall be my people, and your God my God" (1:16)
- **2-4** Ruth's reward
    - **2** Material support: gleaning in Boaz's field
    - **3** The threshing floor
    - **4** Redemption; genealogy (Obed > Jesse > David)

### Key Themes

- ***Hesed*:** "steadfast love," covenant faithfulness, shown by both Ruth and Boaz
- ***Go'el* (kinsman-redeemer):** Boaz buys back the land and preserves the family line
- **Inclusion of Gentiles:** Ruth the Moabite is brought into the messianic line (Matt. 1:5)

## Samuel (31 + 24)

*From Judges to a King*

### Key Themes

- **Kingship:** the transition from God as sole King to a human "vice-regent" who must represent God's law
- **Davidic covenant:** unconditional promise of a descendant on David's throne forever
- **Heart vs. appearance:** God rejects Saul (outwardly impressive) and chooses David (a man after God's own heart; 1 Sam. 13:14; 16:7)

### Key Chapters

- **1 Samuel 1-2** Hannah's prayer; Samuel's birth
- **1 Samuel 3** Call of Samuel, the last judge and first great prophet
- **1 Samuel 8** Israel demands a king "like all the nations," rejecting God's direct rule
- **1 Samuel 15-16** Saul rejected for disobedience ("to obey is better than sacrifice," 15:22); David anointed by Samuel
- **1 Samuel 17** David and Goliath
- **1 Samuel 31** Death of Saul and Jonathan
- **2 Samuel 5-6** David king over all Israel; Jerusalem captured; the ark brought to Jerusalem
- **2 Samuel 7** The Davidic covenant
- **2 Samuel 11-12** Bathsheba; Nathan's rebuke; repentance (Ps. 51)
- **2 Samuel 14-20** Absalom's rebellion

## Kings (22 + 25)

*A history for exiles: "How did we get here?" From Solomon to exile in 400 years.*

### Key Themes

- **The retributive principle:** a king's success or failure is tied directly to his faithfulness to the Mosaic law (especially Deuteronomy)
- **Prophetic witness:** as the kings fail, God raises prophets (like Elijah and Elisha) to confront the throne
- **The split:** the theological and political reasons behind the divided kingdom
- **Commitment to David's house**

### Key Chapters

- **1 Kings 3** Solomon asks for wisdom
- **1 Kings 8** Temple dedication
- **1 Kings 11** Solomon's disobedience and downfall (wealth, women, power, idolatry)
- **1 Kings 12** Rehoboam's folly divides the kingdom
    - Israel (north): Jeroboam son of Nebat (golden calves at Dan and Bethel)
    - Judah (south): Rehoboam
- **1 Kings 18** Elijah's contest on Mount Carmel
- **2 Kings 17** Israel falls to Assyria in **722 BC**
- **2 Kings 22-23** Josiah's reforms after finding the Book of the Law
- **2 Kings 25** Judah falls to Babylon in **586 BC**

### Characters

- **Elijah** (1 Kings 17 - 2 Kings 2)
- **Elisha** (2 Kings 2-13)
- **Hezekiah** and Isaiah (2 Kings 18-20): delivered from Sennacherib; illness and recovery; folly with Babylonian envoys
- **Josiah** (2 Kings 22-23): Book of the Law found, reforms, Passover

## Chronicles (29 + 36)

*A history for returnees: "Is God still with us?"*

Written after the exile, Chronicles highlights spiritual rather than national failures, to encourage the returned community to rebuild worship and pursue restoration through faithful service.

### Key Themes

- **Hope and continuity:** genealogies from Adam to David show that God's plan is still on track
- **Judah and the temple:** the northern kingdom is largely ignored; the focus is the Davidic line and proper worship
- **Prayer and restoration:** emphasizes repentant kings (e.g., Manasseh, 2 Chron. 33)

### Key Chapters

- **1 Chronicles 1-9** Extensive genealogies (establishing identity after the exile)
- **1 Chronicles 17** Davidic covenant
- **1 Chronicles 29** David's final prayer and the offering for the temple
- **2 Chronicles 7:14** "If my people who are called by my name humble themselves, and pray and seek my face and turn from their wicked ways, then I will hear from heaven and will forgive their sin and heal their land."
- **2 Chronicles 36** The decree of Cyrus (**538 BC**) allowing the return

## Ezra (10)

*Return from Exile and Rebuilding the Temple*

Ezra the scribe highlights God's faithfulness in fulfilling his promise (made through Jeremiah) to bring the exiles home.

### Key Themes

- Rebuilding the altar and temple
- **Covenant continuity:** God keeps his covenant with the remnant of Israel
- **Centrality of the Word:** restoration requires a return to the law
- **Separation and holiness:** intermarriage with pagan neighbors forbidden

### Key Chapters and Events

- **1** Decree of Cyrus (Persia) allows the Jews to return to Jerusalem in **538 BC**
- **3** Altar rebuilt, temple foundations laid; joy and weeping
- **4-6** Opposition; Haggai and Zechariah; second temple completed and dedicated (**516 BC**)
- **7** Ezra arrives in Jerusalem (**458 BC**)
- **9-10** Intermarriage, confession, reform

**Ezra's personal mission statement:** "Ezra had set his heart to study the Law of the LORD, and to do it and to teach his statutes and rules in Israel" (7:10).

## Nehemiah (13)

*Rebuilding Jerusalem's Walls and Spiritual Reform*

Nehemiah focuses on the civil and physical security of Jerusalem, showing God's providence through administrative leadership.

### Key Themes

- **Providence and prayer:** Nehemiah models "praying while working"
- **Opposition:** threats from Sanballat and Tobiah, and internal strife
- **Covenant renewal:** the people publicly recommit themselves to God's law
- **Protection:** God protects his people while they do his work (**sword and trowel**)

### Key Chapters and Events

- **1-2** Nehemiah's prayer for Jerusalem and his request to King Artaxerxes to return (**445/444 BC**)
- **6** The walls completed in just 52 days
- **8** Ezra reads the law to the assembly, who weep and then celebrate the **Feast of Booths**
- **9** Corporate prayer of confession, tracing God's faithfulness from Abraham to the present
- **13** Reforms regarding the Sabbath and foreign marriages

### Three Returns in Ezra-Nehemiah

1. Led by Zerubbabel (rebuild the temple), 538 BC
2. Led by Ezra (rebuild the people), 458 BC
3. Led by Nehemiah (rebuild the walls), 445/444 BC

## Esther (10)

*God's Providence Saving His People in Persia*

- **1-2** Introduction
    - Vashti's downfall
    - Esther chosen queen
    - Mordecai foils a plot against the king
- **3-9** Main action
    - **3** Haman plots to kill the Jews
    - **4** "Who knows whether you have not come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" (4:14)
    - **5** Esther received by the king; Haman plots against Mordecai
    - **6** Turning point: Xerxes honors Mordecai
    - **7** Haman executed
    - **8** Esther wins the Jews' right to self-defense
    - **9** The Jews destroy their enemies
- **9:20-10:3** Conclusion
    - Feast of **Purim**
    - Mordecai's rank and rule

### Key Themes

- Providence of God (God's name never appears, yet he rules everything)
- Responsibility of man (initiative, courage)
- The (self-)destruction of the wicked
