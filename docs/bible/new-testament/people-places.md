## Key People and Groups

| Person / group | Where | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Herod the Great** ★ | Matt. 2; Luke 1:5 | Idumean king of Judea under Rome (37-4 BC); rebuilt the temple; ordered the slaughter of Bethlehem's infants. His death (4 BC) dates Jesus' birth. |
| **Herod Antipas** ★ | Matt. 14; Mark 6; Luke 3:19-20; 13:31-32; 23:6-12 | Herod's son; tetrarch of Galilee and Perea (4 BC-AD 39); beheaded John the Baptist; Jesus called him "that fox"; Jesus was sent to him at his trial. |
| Herod Agrippa I | Acts 12 | Grandson of Herod; killed James the son of Zebedee, imprisoned Peter, and was struck down by an angel (AD 44). |
| Herod Agrippa II | Acts 25-26 | Heard Paul's defense: "In a short time would you persuade me to be a Christian?" |
| **Pharisees** ★ | Matt. 23; Luke 18; Acts 23:6-8; Phil. 3:5 | Lay "separatists," devoted to the law and the oral tradition; believed in resurrection and angels; Jesus' main opponents on Sabbath and purity; condemned for hypocrisy. Paul, Nicodemus, and Gamaliel were Pharisees. |
| **Sadducees** ★ | Matt. 22:23-33; Acts 4:1-2; 23:6-8 | Priestly aristocracy who controlled the temple; accepted only the Torah's authority; denied resurrection, angels, and spirits; collaborated with Rome. |
| Scribes | Mark 2:6; 12:38-40 | Experts in the law, mostly Pharisees. |
| Sanhedrin | Mark 14:55; Acts 4-6; 23 | The Jewish ruling council of 71, led by the high priest. |
| Herodians; Zealots | Mark 3:6; 12:13; Luke 6:15 | Supporters of the Herods; violent anti-Roman nationalists (Simon "the Zealot"). |
| Samaritans | John 4; Luke 10:25-37; Acts 8 | Mixed descendants of the northern kingdom; worshiped on Mt. Gerizim; despised by Jews. |
| **The Apostles** ★ | Matt. 10:2-4; Mark 3:16-19; Luke 6:14-16; Acts 1:13 | Peter, Andrew, James, John, Philip, Bartholomew, Thomas, Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddeus (Judas son of James), Simon the Zealot, Judas Iscariot; replaced by Matthias. See [Life of Christ](life-of-christ.md#the-twelve-apostles). |
| **Peter** ★ | Gospels; Acts 1-12, 15; Gal. 2; 1-2 Peter | Fisherman, brother of Andrew; leader of the Twelve; confessed Jesus as the Christ (Matt. 16); denied him and was restored (John 21); preached at Pentecost (Acts 2); opened the door to the Gentiles (Cornelius, Acts 10); spoke at the Jerusalem Council (15); rebuked by Paul at Antioch (Gal. 2). |
| James and John | Mark 1:19; 3:17; Acts 12:2 | Sons of Zebedee, "sons of thunder"; James was the first apostle martyred; John wrote the Gospel, the letters, and Revelation. |
| John the Baptist | Matt. 3, 11, 14; Luke 1, 3; John 1 | The forerunner ("Elijah"), prophesied by Isa. 40:3 and Mal. 3:1; baptized Jesus; "Behold, the Lamb of God"; beheaded by Antipas. |
| Mary, mother of Jesus | Luke 1-2; John 2, 19; Acts 1:14 | The virgin who bore the Messiah; the Magnificat. |
| Mary Magdalene | Luke 8:2; John 20 | Delivered from seven demons; first witness of the risen Christ. |
| Mary, Martha, Lazarus | Luke 10:38-42; John 11-12 | Family at Bethany; Lazarus raised. |
| Nicodemus | John 3; 7:50; 19:39 | Pharisee and ruler; "You must be born again"; helped bury Jesus. |
| Caiaphas | Matt. 26; John 11:49-52; 18 | High priest who presided at Jesus' trial. |
| Pontius Pilate | Matt. 27; John 18-19 | Roman governor of Judea (AD 26-36); sentenced Jesus. |
| **Stephen** ★ | Acts 6-7 | One of the Seven, "full of faith and of the Holy Spirit"; his speech indicted Israel's resistance to God; the first Christian martyr; Saul was present. |
| Philip the evangelist | Acts 6, 8, 21:8 | One of the Seven; evangelized Samaria; baptized the Ethiopian eunuch. |
| **Cornelius** ★ | Acts 10-11 | God-fearing Roman centurion in Caesarea; his household was the first Gentile conversion; the Spirit fell before baptism, proving Gentiles are accepted without circumcision (cited at the Council, Acts 15:7-11). |
| **Barnabas** ★ | Acts 4:36-37; 9:27; 11:22-30; 13-15; Gal. 2 | "Son of encouragement"; vouched for Saul; led the Antioch church; Paul's partner on the first journey; split with Paul over John Mark. |
| **Paul** ★ | Acts 7-28; 13 letters | Saul of Tarsus: Pharisee, persecutor, converted on the Damascus road (Acts 9); apostle to the Gentiles; three missionary journeys; wrote 13 epistles; martyred in Rome under Nero. See [Life of Paul](paul.md). |
| **Timothy** ★ | Acts 16:1-3; Phil. 2:19-22; 1-2 Tim. | From Lystra; Jewish mother (Eunice) and Greek father; Paul's "true child in the faith" and co-worker; Paul's delegate at Ephesus; recipient of 1-2 Timothy. |
| **James** ★ | Matt. 13:55; Acts 15; 21:18; Gal. 1:19; 2:9; 1 Cor. 15:7 | The Lord's brother; unbelieving until the resurrection; leader of the Jerusalem church; gave the decision at the Jerusalem Council; wrote the Epistle of James. (Distinguish James son of Zebedee, killed in Acts 12, and James son of Alphaeus.) |
| Silas (Silvanus) | Acts 15:22-18:5; 1 Pet. 5:12 | Paul's companion on the second journey; jailed with him in Philippi. |
| Luke | Col. 4:14; 2 Tim. 4:11; Acts 16:10 | "The beloved physician"; Paul's companion ("we" passages); wrote Luke-Acts. |
| John Mark | Acts 12:12, 25; 13:13; 15:37-39; 2 Tim. 4:11 | Left the first journey; later "useful" to Paul; wrote Mark (from Peter). |
| Apollos | Acts 18:24-28; 1 Cor. 1-4 | Eloquent Alexandrian Jew; taught more accurately by Priscilla and Aquila; ministered in Corinth ("Apollos watered"). |
| Priscilla and Aquila | Acts 18; Rom. 16:3-5 | Tentmakers; Paul's co-workers in Corinth and Ephesus; instructed Apollos; hosted a house church. |
| Lydia | Acts 16:14-15 | Seller of purple in Philippi; "the Lord opened her heart"; first convert in Europe; her household was baptized. |
| Titus | 2 Cor. 7-8; Gal. 2:3; Titus | Uncircumcised Gentile co-worker; sent to Corinth and to Crete. |
| Onesimus | Philemon; Col. 4:9 | Runaway slave converted through Paul. |
| Gamaliel | Acts 5:34-39; 22:3 | Respected Pharisee; Paul's teacher; counseled caution toward the apostles. |
| Felix, Festus | Acts 24-25 | Roman governors who held Paul in Caesarea. |

## Key Places

| Place | Where | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Bethlehem** | Matt. 2; Luke 2 | Jesus' birthplace (Mic. 5:2) |
| **Nazareth** | Luke 1:26; 4:16-30 | Jesus' hometown in Galilee; rejected him |
| **Capernaum** | Matt. 4:13; Mark 1-2 | Jesus' Galilean base; Peter's home |
| **Sea of Galilee** | Mark 4, 6; John 21 | Calling of the fishermen; storm calmed; walking on water |
| **Cana** | John 2; 4:46 | Water into wine; official's son healed |
| **Caesarea Philippi** | Matt. 16:13-20 | Peter's confession |
| **Sychar (Samaria)** | John 4 | Woman at the well (Jacob's well) |
| **Bethany** | John 11-12; Luke 24:50 | Home of Mary, Martha, and Lazarus; near the place of the ascension |
| **Jerusalem** | Gospels; Acts 1-7, 15, 21 | Temple; crucifixion; resurrection; Pentecost; Council |
| **Gethsemane / Mount of Olives** | Matt. 24-26; Acts 1:12 | Olivet Discourse; agony and arrest; ascension |
| **Golgotha (Calvary)** | John 19:17 | "Place of a skull," where Jesus was crucified |
| **Emmaus** | Luke 24:13-35 | Risen Christ explains the Scriptures |
| **Damascus** | Acts 9 | Paul's conversion |
| **Antioch (Syria)** | Acts 11:19-30; 13:1-3; 15 | First Gentile church; "Christians"; Paul's sending church |
| **Pisidian Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, Derbe** | Acts 13-14 | First journey (South Galatia) |
| **Philippi** | Acts 16; Philippians | First church in Europe: Lydia, the jailer |
| **Thessalonica, Berea** | Acts 17; 1-2 Thess. | Riot; the "noble" Bereans |
| **Athens** | Acts 17:16-34 | Areopagus sermon |
| **Corinth** | Acts 18; 1-2 Cor. | 18 months; Romans written here |
| **Ephesus** | Acts 19-20; Eph.; 1 Tim.; Rev. 2 | 3 years; riot over Artemis; hub of Asia |
| **Caesarea (Maritima)** | Acts 10; 23-26 | Cornelius; Paul imprisoned two years |
| **Malta** | Acts 28 | Shipwreck |
| **Rome** | Acts 28; Romans; Prison Epistles | Paul under house arrest; martyrdom of Paul and Peter |
| **Patmos** | Rev. 1:9 | John's exile and the Revelation |
