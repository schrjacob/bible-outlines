# People

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

Chapell, church history section E: *"Briefly identify the following people with date (century) and their significance."* These are Chapell's 41 names, in his order. The **Tier** column ranks each (1 = know cold, 2 = second pass, 3 = if time allows). Dates are approximate for early figures; verify them against a reliable text.

## The Early Church (1st-5th centuries)

| Tier | Person | Dates | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | **1. Polycarp** | c. 69-155 (2nd c.) | Bishop of **Smyrna**; a disciple of the apostle **John**; **martyred** (c. 155-156) at about 86 ("Eighty and six years have I served him, and he has done me no wrong"); a link between the apostles and the church fathers. |
| 3 | **2. Clement** (of Rome) | d. c. 100 (1st c.) | Bishop of **Rome**; wrote **1 Clement** (c. 96) to the Corinthians urging order and unity; one of the **apostolic fathers**. |
| 2 | **3. Ignatius** | d. c. 110 (2nd c.) | Bishop of **Antioch**; wrote seven letters on the way to martyrdom in Rome; stressed the **bishop**, the **real humanity** of Christ against docetism, and the unity of the church. |
| 2 | **4. Marcion** | c. 85-160 (2nd c.) | A heretic who **rejected the Old Testament** and the God of the OT, and kept only a shortened **Luke** and ten letters of Paul; his attempt at a canon pushed the church to define the **New Testament canon**. |
| 2 | **5. Justin** (Martyr) | c. 100-165 (2nd c.) | An **apologist** who defended Christianity to the emperors; taught that Christ is the **Logos**; wrote the *Apology* and *Dialogue with Trypho*; **martyred** in Rome. |
| 2 | **6. Eusebius of Caesarea** | c. 260-340 (4th c.) | The "**father of church history**": his *Ecclesiastical History* records the first three centuries; biographer of **Constantine**; a moderate at **Nicea**. |
| 2 | **7. Tertullian** | c. 160-220 (2nd-3rd c.) | North African (Carthage); the first great **Latin theologian**; coined **"Trinity"** and "person"; defended orthodoxy; later joined the **Montanists**. |
| 1 | **8. Constantine** | c. 272-337 (4th c.) | The **Roman emperor** who ended persecution (**Edict of Milan**, **313**) after his conversion (312); called the **Council of Nicea** (325); founded **Constantinople** (330); began the era of church and state alliance. |
| 2 | **9. Chrysostom** (John) | c. 347-407 (4th-5th c.) | Patriarch of **Constantinople**; "golden-mouthed" preacher of **expository sermons**; opposed the corruption of the court and was **exiled**. |
| 2 | **10. Jerome** | c. 347-420 (4th-5th c.) | A scholar in **Bethlehem** who translated the Bible into Latin, the **Vulgate**, the standard Bible of the medieval West. |
| 2 | **11. Pelagius** | c. 360-420 (4th-5th c.) | A British monk who **denied original sin** and taught that man can **keep God's commands by his own free will**; opposed by **Augustine**; condemned (418, 431). |
| 1 | **12. Augustine** | 354-430 (4th-5th c.) | Bishop of **Hippo** (North Africa); wrote the *Confessions* and *City of God*; the great teacher of **sin and grace**, **original sin**, and **predestination** against Pelagius; shaped Western theology for the Catholic and Reformed alike. |

## The Middle Ages (6th-15th centuries)

| Tier | Person | Dates | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **13. Bernard of Clairvaux** | 1090-1153 (12th c.) | A **Cistercian** abbot and mystic; preached the Second Crusade; wrote hymns and devotion centered on Christ's love; opposed **Abelard**. |
| 3 | **14. Gregory the Great** | c. 540-604 (6th-7th c.) | **Pope** (590-604); strengthened the papacy; wrote the *Pastoral Rule*; sent **Augustine of Canterbury** to England (597); the "father of the medieval papacy." |
| 3 | **15. Francis of Assisi** | 1181-1226 (12th-13th c.) | Founder of the **Franciscans**; embraced **poverty**, preaching, and love of creation; renewed the medieval church. |
| 2 | **16. Anselm** | 1033-1109 (11th-12th c.) | Archbishop of **Canterbury**; the **ontological argument** for God's existence; *Cur Deus Homo* (the **satisfaction** theory of the atonement); "faith seeking understanding"; the father of **scholasticism**. |
| 1 | **17. Aquinas** (Thomas) | 1225-74 (13th c.) | A **Dominican**; wrote the *Summa Theologiae*; the greatest **scholastic** theologian, combining **Aristotle** with Christian doctrine; his system became the official theology of the Roman Catholic Church. |
| 2 | **18. Wycliffe** (John) | c. 1330-84 (14th c.) | An Oxford scholar called "**the morning star of the Reformation**"; attacked papal authority and **transubstantiation**; sponsored the first complete **English Bible**; his followers were the **Lollards**. |
| 2 | **19. Hus** (John) | c. 1370-1415 (14th-15th c.) | A Bohemian priest at **Prague**; influenced by Wycliffe; preached against church corruption; **burned at the stake** at the **Council of Constance** (1415); his followers were the **Hussites**. |
| 2 | **20. Tyndale** (William) | c. 1494-1536 (16th c.) | Translated the **New Testament into English** from the Greek (1526); **strangled and burned** in 1536; his translation underlies the **King James Version**. |

## The Reformation and After (16th-17th centuries)

| Tier | Person | Dates | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **21. Luther** (Martin) | 1483-1546 (16th c.) | A German monk and professor; posted the **95 Theses** (**1517**); stood at the **Diet of Worms** (1521); translated the **Bible into German**; taught **justification by faith alone**; sparked the **Reformation**. |
| 2 | **22. Melanchthon** (Philip) | 1497-1560 (16th c.) | Luther's colleague at Wittenberg; wrote the first Lutheran dogmatics (*Loci Communes*, 1521) and the **Augsburg Confession** (**1530**); the scholar of the Reformation. |
| 1 | **23. Zwingli** (Ulrich) | 1484-1531 (16th c.) | The Reformer of **Zurich**; taught by **Scripture** alone; held a **memorial** view of the Lord's Supper (the break with Luther at Marburg, 1529); **killed** in battle at Kappel (1531). |
| 1 | **24. Calvin** (John) | 1509-64 (16th c.) | Reformer of **Geneva**; wrote the *Institutes of the Christian Religion* (1536; final 1559) and **commentaries**; the chief theologian of the **Reformed** tradition; shaped **Presbyterian** church order. |
| 1 | **25. Knox** (John) | c. 1514-72 (16th c.) | The Scottish Reformer; a student of Calvin; led the **Scottish Reformation** (**1560**) and the **Church of Scotland**, which became **Presbyterian**. |
| 2 | **26. Covenanters** | 17th c. | **Scottish Presbyterians** who signed the **National Covenant** (**1638**) and the **Solemn League and Covenant** (**1643**) to defend Presbyterian government against the **Stuart** kings' attempts to impose **bishops**; many suffered in the "**Killing Time**." |
| 1 | **27. Arminius** (Jacob) | 1560-1609 (16th-17th c.) | A **Dutch** Reformed theologian at Leiden; questioned predestination and taught **conditional election**; his followers (the **Remonstrants**, **1610**) were answered by the **Synod of Dort** (1618-19). |
| 3 | **28. Amyraut and the School of Saumur** | 1596-1664 (17th c.) | **Moïse Amyraut**, a French Huguenot at **Saumur**, taught **hypothetical universalism** (Christ died for all on condition of faith); opposed by orthodox Reformed (the **Helvetic Consensus**, 1675). |

## The Awakenings and the Modern Era (17th-20th centuries)

| Tier | Person | Dates | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **29. Jonathan Edwards** | 1703-58 (18th c.) | A New England Congregational pastor (**Northampton**) and theologian; leader of the **Great Awakening**; wrote *Freedom of the Will* and *Religious Affections*; preached "**Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God**" (1741); regarded as America's greatest theologian. |
| 2 | **30. Richard Baxter** | 1615-91 (17th c.) | An English **Puritan** pastor at **Kidderminster**; wrote *The Reformed Pastor* and *The Saints' Everlasting Rest*; **ejected** in 1662 for nonconformity. |
| 2 | **31. Zinzendorf** | 1700-60 (18th c.) | A German count who sheltered the **Moravians** at **Herrnhut**; pioneered **world missions**; a **heart religion** that influenced **John Wesley**. |
| 1 | **32. George Whitefield** | 1714-70 (18th c.) | An Anglican and **Calvinistic Methodist**, the great **evangelist** of the **Great Awakening** in Britain and the colonies, famous for **open-air preaching**. |
| 1 | **33. John Wesley** | 1703-91 (18th c.) | An Anglican priest and founder of **Methodism**; his **Aldersgate** conversion (**1738**); an **Arminian** with an emphasis on **holiness**; organized societies and **itinerant preaching**. |
| 3 | **34. Marrow controversy** | 1717-23 (18th c.) | A dispute in the **Church of Scotland** over *The Marrow of Modern Divinity*; the **Marrow Men** (**Thomas Boston**, the **Erskines**) held to the **free offer** of the gospel against what they saw as **legalism**; the Assembly condemned the book in 1720. |
| 2 | **35. William Carey** | 1761-1834 (18th-19th c.) | An English **Baptist**, the "**father of modern missions**"; his *Enquiry* (1792) and mission to **India** (1793); "Expect great things from God; attempt great things for God." |
| 3 | **36. George Müller** | 1805-98 (19th c.) | A **Bristol** pastor who cared for **thousands of orphans** and lived and prayed **by faith** without appeals for funds. |
| 2 | **37. Charles Spurgeon** | 1834-92 (19th c.) | A **Baptist** "**prince of preachers**" at the **Metropolitan Tabernacle**, London; a Calvinist; led the **Downgrade Controversy** (1887) against liberalism. |
| 1 | **38. Charles Hodge** | 1797-1878 (19th c.) | Professor at **Princeton Seminary**; wrote a three-volume *Systematic Theology*; leader of the **Old School** Presbyterians; defended the Reformed faith and the authority of Scripture. |
| 1 | **39. B. B. Warfield** | 1851-1921 (19th-20th c.) | Princeton professor; the defender of **biblical inerrancy** (with A. A. Hodge, 1881), a **cessationist** (*Counterfeit Miracles*); the last great figure of "**Old Princeton**." |
| 2 | **40. Charles Finney** | 1792-1875 (19th c.) | A revivalist of the **Second Great Awakening**; introduced "**new measures**" (the anxious bench); taught a **Pelagian-leaning** view of sin and a **perfectionist** sanctification; opposed by the **Old School**. |
| 2 | **41. D. L. Moody** | 1837-99 (19th c.) | A Chicago **evangelist** (with the singer **Ira Sankey**); founded the **Moody Bible Institute** (1886) and Northfield schools; shaped modern **evangelism** and Bible institute training. |
