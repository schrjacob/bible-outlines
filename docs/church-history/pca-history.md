## American Presbyterianism

| Tier | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Briefly trace the history of Presbyterianism in the United States. | **Roots:** Scottish, Scots-Irish, and English (Puritan) immigrants. **1706:** **Francis Makemie** (a Scots-Irish minister, 1658-1708, "the father of American Presbyterianism") organizes the **first American presbytery**, at Philadelphia. **1717:** the **first synod** (the Synod of Philadelphia). **1729:** the **Adopting Act**: the synod adopts the **Westminster Standards** as its confession (with the principle that ministers may declare **scruples** about non-essentials). **1741:** the **Old Side/New Side division**, over the **Great Awakening** and ministerial training (the **Tennents**' **Log College**); **reunited 1758**. **1788-89:** the **Constitution** is adopted (the Standards, with revisions on the magistrate) and the **first General Assembly** meets (1789) in Philadelphia. **1801:** the **Plan of Union** with the **Congregationalists** for the frontier. **1837:** the **Old School/New School division** over doctrine, polity, and the Plan of Union. **1857-61:** the **New School** and then the **Old School** divide over slavery and the Civil War, with a **Southern church** formed in **1861** (the **Presbyterian Church in the Confederate States**, later the **Presbyterian Church in the U.S., PCUS**). **1869:** Northern Old and New Schools **reunite**. **1958:** the **United Presbyterian Church USA** formed by merger. **1983:** the **PCUS and UPCUSA reunite** as the **PC(USA)**, the group the PCA left (in 1973) rather than join. |
| 2 | Briefly outline some of the distinctives of the Southern Presbyterian tradition. Identify in your answer James Henley Thornwell, Robert Lewis Dabney, Benjamin Morgan Palmer, and John L. Girardeau. | **Distinctives:** the **"spirituality of the church"** (the church's mission is spiritual and it should not speak on political questions); **jure divino Presbyterianism** (Presbyterian polity has Scriptural warrant); a high view of the **ruling elder**; **strict confessionalism** and the **regulative principle** (Girardeau argued against instrumental music); a **strong evangelistic** emphasis; a **conservative** theology (from Old School Princeton). **Thornwell** (1812-62, South Carolina): the great theologian of the Southern church, champion of the spirituality of the church and church boards. **Dabney** (1820-98, Union Seminary in Virginia): wrote a *Systematic Theology*, a defender of Calvinism and Stonewall Jackson's chaplain. **Palmer** (1818-1902, New Orleans): preached and wrote widely; first moderator of the PCUS General Assembly (1861). **Girardeau** (1825-98, Charleston and Columbia Seminary): pastor to Black Presbyterians, advocate of the regulative principle (*Instrumental Music in Public Worship*). **Note:** Thornwell, Dabney, and Palmer **defended slavery** and the Confederacy, a grievous error; the PCA has since **publicly confessed and repented** of racism in its history (General Assembly, 2016). |
| 1 | Discuss the controversy between modernism and fundamentalism. Be sure in your answer to identify and explain the significance of the Auburn Affirmation (who signed it? why? when?), the "five fundamentals," Harry Emerson Fosdick, and J. Gresham Machen. | **Background:** in the early 20th century, **modernism** (liberal theology that questioned the supernatural) spread through the northern **PCUSA**; **fundamentalism** defended the "fundamentals." **Five fundamentals:** the PCUSA General Assembly declared (**1910**, reaffirmed 1916 and **1923**) five **essential** doctrines: the **inerrancy of Scripture**, the **virgin birth**, the **substitutionary atonement**, the **bodily resurrection**, and the **reality of miracles**. **Fosdick:** **Harry Emerson Fosdick**, a Baptist preaching at First Presbyterian Church, New York, delivered "**Shall the Fundamentalists Win?**" (**1922**), a call for a tolerant liberal Christianity, which triggered a Presbyterian dispute. **Auburn Affirmation:** first published in **January 1924** with 150 names; the final printing of **5 May 1924** carried **1,274 signers** (ministers and elders of the PCUSA). It protested the 1923 Assembly's action, called the five points **theories** and not required doctrines, and defended **liberty of interpretation**; it marks the liberal victory in the PCUSA. **Machen:** **J. Gresham Machen** (1881-1937), New Testament professor at **Princeton**, wrote ***Christianity and Liberalism*** (**1923**), arguing that liberalism is a **different religion**; he left Princeton and founded **Westminster Theological Seminary** (**1929**), the **Independent Board for Presbyterian Foreign Missions** (**1933**), and, suspended from the PCUSA ministry (1935), the **Presbyterian Church of America**, later the **Orthodox Presbyterian Church (OPC)** (**1936**). |
| 2 | Trace the historical roots of the RPCES. From which major branch of Presbyterianism did the RPCES come? Why is the RPCES important in PCA history? Be sure in your answer to identify and explain the significance of the Independent Board for Presbyterian Foreign Missions, J. Oliver Buswell, Robert Rayburn, Francis Schaeffer, and Carl McIntire. | **RPCES** = the **Reformed Presbyterian Church, Evangelical Synod** (**1965-1982**). It was formed in **1965** by the merger of the **Reformed Presbyterian Church, General Synod** (a Covenanter body, 1833-1965) and the **Evangelical Presbyterian Church**. The EPC was the former **Columbus Synod** of the **Bible Presbyterian Church** (1956-61, renamed EPC in 1961). So the RPCES came, on one side, from the **Covenanter** (Reformed Presbyterian) branch and, on the other, from the **conservative northern PCUSA** stream that ran through **J. Gresham Machen** (the **Independent Board for Presbyterian Foreign Missions**, 1933; the OPC, 1936) and the **Bible Presbyterian Church** (organized in the late 1930s, led by **Carl McIntire**, a separatist and premillennialist). In **1956** the Bible Presbyterian Church divided into the **Collingswood Synod** (McIntire) and the **Columbus Synod** (**J. Oliver Buswell**, Wheaton's president; **Robert Rayburn**, founder of **Covenant College** and **Covenant Theological Seminary**; **Francis Schaeffer**, founder of **L'Abri**). **Importance:** the RPCES **joined the PCA in 1982** ("**Joining and Receiving**," at the Tenth General Assembly in **Grand Rapids**, June 1982, where Schaeffer gave the keynote address). The Assembly that year counted 805 churches and about 151,000 members, so the PCA became more than a Southern church and gained **Covenant Seminary and College**. (Roles of Buswell, Rayburn, Schaeffer, and McIntire in the BPC: not checked.) |
| 1 | Trace the history of the formation of the PCA. When, where, and why did the PCA begin? From what major branch of Presbyterianism did early members of the PCA come? Be sure in your answer to identify and explain the significance of the Presbyterian Evangelistic Fellowship, Concerned Presbyterians, the Presbyterian Journal, Morton H. Smith, W. Jack Williamson, Ken Keyes, and G. Aiken Taylor. | **When and where:** the **first General Assembly** met **4-7 December 1973** at **Briarwood Presbyterian Church** in **Birmingham, Alabama**, with **387 commissioners representing 215 churches**; the denomination then had 16 presbyteries and about **260 churches and 41,000 members**. It chose the name **National Presbyterian Church**; in **1974**, after learning of a possible conflict with another body's name, it became the **Presbyterian Church in America**. Its **Message to All Churches** (7 December 1973) said the separation came "only after long years of struggle" and that loyalty to Christ took precedence over an earthly institution. **Why:** conservatives in the **Southern** church (the **PCUS**) concluded that it had drifted from Scripture and the Reformed faith (liberalism and neo-orthodoxy, ecumenism and merger efforts, and the General Assembly's pronouncements) and that there was no remedy within it. **Early members** came from the **PCUS**. **Steps:** **1942**: the *Southern Presbyterian Journal* began (L. Nelson Bell, Henry B. Dendy, and others) to oppose modernism in the PCUS. **1955**: PCUS presbyteries rejected a merger with northern bodies, largely through resistance led by the *Journal*. **1958**: **Presbyterian Evangelistic Fellowship** founded (Rev. Wm. E. Hill Jr.). **1959**: **G. Aiken Taylor** became editor of what was now the *Presbyterian Journal*; he despaired of reforming the PCUS and worked toward a large, non-regional conservative denomination, and was perhaps the person most instrumental in organizing the PCA. **1965**: **Concerned Presbyterians, Inc.**, a group of ruling elders, formed under ruling elder **Kenneth S. Keyes** (likely creator of the PCA motto). **1969**: **Presbyterian Churchmen United** for ministers (executive secretary Rev. Paul Settle), whose *Declaration of Commitment* (4 October 1969) was signed by more than 500 ministers. **1971**: a **Steering Committee** planned a new denomination. **1973**: a **Convocation of Sessions** (18-19 May) voted 349-16 to call for a new denomination, and an **Advisory Convention** (7-9 August) laid the groundwork. **People:** **W. Jack Williamson**, a ruling elder, was elected **Moderator** of the first Assembly; **Morton H. Smith** was elected **Stated Clerk**. |
| 1 | What are some distinctives of the PCA? | Its own statement is "**Faithful to the Scriptures, true to the Reformed faith, and obedient to the Great Commission.**" **Distinctives:** a **confessional**, **Reformed** church (the Westminster Standards as the system of doctrine); **Presbyterian** government with **ruling and teaching elders**; **evangelism and missions** (Mission to the World, Mission to North America, Reformed University Ministries); **conservative** positions (inerrancy; male officers; the sanctity of life and marriage); **"good-faith" subscription** with exceptions declared to presbytery; a **"big tent" within the Reformed world** (creation, worship, and millennium views vary); **no ordination of women** (BCO 7-2: the offices of elder and deacon are open to men only); and the **Joining and Receiving** union with the RPCES (1982). |
