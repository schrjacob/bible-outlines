## American Presbyterianism

| Tier | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Briefly trace the history of Presbyterianism in the United States. | **Roots:** Scottish, Scots-Irish, and English (Puritan) immigrants. **1706:** **Francis Makemie** (a Scots-Irish minister, 1658-1708, "the father of American Presbyterianism") organizes the **first American presbytery**, at Philadelphia. **1717:** the **first synod** (the Synod of Philadelphia). **1729:** the **Adopting Act**: the synod adopts the **Westminster Standards** as its confession (with the principle that ministers may declare **scruples** about non-essentials). **1741:** the **Old Side/New Side division**, over the **Great Awakening** and ministerial training (the **Tennents**' **Log College**); **reunited 1758**. **1788-89:** the **Constitution** is adopted (the Standards, with revisions on the magistrate) and the **first General Assembly** meets (1789) in Philadelphia. **1801:** the **Plan of Union** with the **Congregationalists** for the frontier. **1837:** the **Old School/New School division** over doctrine, polity, and the Plan of Union. **1857-61:** the **New School** and then the **Old School** divide over slavery and the Civil War, with a **Southern church** formed in **1861** (the **Presbyterian Church in the Confederate States**, later the **Presbyterian Church in the U.S., PCUS**). **1869:** Northern Old and New Schools **reunite**. **1958:** the **United Presbyterian Church USA** formed by merger. **1983:** the **PCUS and UPCUSA reunite** as the **PC(USA)**, the group the PCA left (in 1973) rather than join. |
| 2 | Briefly outline some of the distinctives of the Southern Presbyterian tradition. Identify in your answer James Henley Thornwell, Robert Lewis Dabney, Benjamin Morgan Palmer, and John L. Girardeau. | **Distinctives:** the **"spirituality of the church"** (the church's mission is spiritual and it should not speak on political questions); **jure divino Presbyterianism** (Presbyterian polity has Scriptural warrant); a high view of the **ruling elder**; **strict confessionalism** and the **regulative principle** (Girardeau argued against instrumental music); a **strong evangelistic** emphasis; a **conservative** theology (from Old School Princeton). **Thornwell** (1812-62, South Carolina): the great theologian of the Southern church, champion of the spirituality of the church and church boards. **Dabney** (1820-98, Union Seminary in Virginia): wrote a *Systematic Theology*, a defender of Calvinism and Stonewall Jackson's chaplain. **Palmer** (1818-1902, New Orleans): preached and wrote widely; first moderator of the PCUS General Assembly (1861). **Girardeau** (1825-98, Charleston and Columbia Seminary): pastor to Black Presbyterians, advocate of the regulative principle (*Instrumental Music in Public Worship*). **Note:** Thornwell, Dabney, and Palmer **defended slavery** and the Confederacy, a grievous error; the PCA has since **publicly confessed and repented** of racism in its history (General Assembly, 2016). |
| 1 | Discuss the controversy between modernism and fundamentalism. Be sure in your answer to identify and explain the significance of the Auburn Affirmation (who signed it? why? when?), the "five fundamentals," Harry Emerson Fosdick, and J. Gresham Machen. | **Background:** in the early 20th century, **modernism** (liberal theology that questioned the supernatural) spread through the northern **PCUSA**; **fundamentalism** defended the "fundamentals." **Five fundamentals:** the PCUSA General Assembly declared (**1910**, reaffirmed 1916 and **1923**) five **essential** doctrines: the **inerrancy of Scripture**, the **virgin birth**, the **substitutionary atonement**, the **bodily resurrection**, and the **reality of miracles**. **Fosdick:** **Harry Emerson Fosdick**, a Baptist preaching at First Presbyterian Church, New York, delivered "**Shall the Fundamentalists Win?**" (**1922**), a call for a tolerant liberal Christianity, which triggered a Presbyterian dispute. **Auburn Affirmation:** published in **1924** (dated 1923), signed by about **1,274 ministers**, it protested the 1923 Assembly's action, called the five points **theories** and not required doctrines, and defended **liberty of interpretation**; it marks the liberal victory in the PCUSA. **Machen:** **J. Gresham Machen** (1881-1937), New Testament professor at **Princeton**, wrote ***Christianity and Liberalism*** (**1923**), arguing that liberalism is a **different religion**; he left Princeton and founded **Westminster Theological Seminary** (**1929**), the **Independent Board for Presbyterian Foreign Missions** (**1933**), and, suspended from the PCUSA ministry (1935), the **Presbyterian Church of America**, later the **Orthodox Presbyterian Church (OPC)** (**1936**). |
| 2 | Trace the historical roots of the RPCES. From which major branch of Presbyterianism did the RPCES come? Why is the RPCES important in PCA history? Be sure in your answer to identify and explain the significance of the Independent Board for Presbyterian Foreign Missions, J. Oliver Buswell, Robert Rayburn, Francis Schaeffer, and Carl McIntire. | **RPCES** = the **Reformed Presbyterian Church, Evangelical Synod**, formed in **1965** by the merger of the **Reformed Presbyterian Church, General Synod** (a Covenanter body) and the **Evangelical Presbyterian Church**. Its **roots** run from the **northern PCUSA** through **Machen** (the **Independent Board**, 1933; the OPC, 1936) and the split in the OPC (1937) that produced the **Bible Presbyterian Church** (led by **Carl McIntire**, a separatist and premillennialist); in 1956 the Bible Presbyterian Church divided into the **Collingswood Synod** (McIntire) and the **Columbus Synod** (**J. Oliver Buswell**, president of Wheaton College; **Robert Rayburn**, founder of **Covenant College** and **Covenant Theological Seminary**; **Francis Schaeffer**, founder of **L'Abri**), which became the Evangelical Presbyterian Church (1961) and then, after the merger, the RPCES. **Importance:** the RPCES **joined the PCA in 1982** ("**Joining and Receiving**"), bringing **Covenant Seminary and Covenant College**, a **Northern** constituency, and a **broader** heritage, so that the PCA became more than a Southern church. | 1 | Trace the history of the formation of the PCA. When, where, and why did the PCA begin? From what major branch of Presbyterianism did early members of the PCA come? Be sure in your answer to identify and explain the significance of the Presbyterian Evangelistic Fellowship, Concerned Presbyterians, the Presbyterian Journal, Morton H. Smith, W. Jack Williamson, Ken Keyes, and G. Aiken Taylor. | **When and where:** the **first General Assembly** met in **December 1973** at **Briarwood Presbyterian Church** in **Birmingham, Alabama**, organizing the **"National Presbyterian Church,"** renamed the **Presbyterian Church in America** in **1974**. **Why:** conservatives in the **Southern** church (the **PCUS**) had come to believe that it had **drifted from Scripture and the Reformed faith** (liberalism, denial of the authority of Scripture, ecumenism, the proposed reunion with the northern church, a changing view of church and politics); after years of efforts to reform from within, they **withdrew**. **Early members** came from the **PCUS (Southern Presbyterian)** church. **Groups and people:** the **Presbyterian Journal** (a conservative weekly, founded 1942 by L. Nelson Bell and others, edited by **G. Aiken Taylor**) informed and organized conservatives; **Concerned Presbyterians** and the **Presbyterian Evangelistic Fellowship** were conservative **organizations** within the PCUS that promoted evangelism and reform and prepared the way for the new church; **Morton H. Smith** (a professor at **Reformed Theological Seminary**, 1923-2016) was the first **Stated Clerk** and a principal **architect** of the PCA's Constitution (he wrote the history of PCUS decline, *How Is the Gold Become Dim*, 1973); **W. Jack Williamson** and **Ken Keyes** were pastors and leaders in the **organizing movement** (verify their specific offices). |
| 1 | What are some distinctives of the PCA? | Its own statement is "**Faithful to the Scriptures, true to the Reformed faith, and obedient to the Great Commission.**" **Distinctives:** a **confessional**, **Reformed** church (the Westminster Standards as the system of doctrine); **Presbyterian** government with **ruling and teaching elders**; **evangelism and missions** (Mission to the World, Mission to North America, Reformed University Ministries); **conservative** positions (inerrancy; male officers; the sanctity of life and marriage); **"good-faith" subscription** with exceptions declared to presbytery; a **"big tent" within the Reformed world** (creation, worship, and millennium views vary); **no ordination of women**; and the **Joining and Receiving** union with the RPCES (1982). |
