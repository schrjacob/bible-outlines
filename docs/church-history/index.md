# Church History

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

This section follows Bryan Chapell and Jim Meek, ***Preparing for Licensure and Ordination Exams*** (PCA Christian Education and Publications, 2002), section III, "Study Questions on Church History," including its section F on the history of the PCA. Ordination requires an examination in **church history** and in **the history of the Presbyterian Church in America** (BCO 21-4); licensure does not.

!!! info "How these pages were checked"
    The **history of the PCA** (page 4) and the **Auburn Affirmation** were checked against the PCA Historical Center (pcahistory.org). The denominations, events, definitions, and people on the other pages come from standard church histories (Chapell's bibliography names Walker, Latourette, and Gonzalez) and have **not** been checked against a source; confirm names and dates in one of those before the exam. Items on the PCA page marked "not checked" are likewise unverified.

## If Time Is Short: Study in Tiers

Each question on these pages carries a **Tier** number in its first column. The tiers are a study judgment.

!!! success "Tier 1: know cold"
    A **timeline of the main eras** with their key dates (AD 33, 313, 325, 451, 1054, 1517, 1563, 1618-19, 1646, 1706, 1789, 1973); the **five solas**; the **Reformers** (Luther, Zwingli, Calvin, Knox) and **Augustine, Aquinas, Constantine**; the **councils of Nicea and Chalcedon** and the Reformation creeds and confessions; the **Great Awakenings**; **Old School and New School**; and the **formation of the PCA**.

!!! warning "Tier 2: second pass"
    The origins and distinctives of the **denominations**; the remaining **events** and **definitions**; the **church fathers**, medieval figures, and Puritans; **Arminius and the Synod of Dort**; the **Southern tradition**; **modernism and fundamentalism**; and the **RPCES**.

!!! note "Tier 3: if time allows"
    Lesser-known figures and controversies (Marrow, Saumur), and finer points of PCA history.

## A. General

| Tier | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | What is the value of studying church history? | It shows the **faithfulness of God** to his church (Matt. 16:18); gives **perspective** on today's issues (we are not the first to face them); helps us **avoid old errors** (heresies recur); **roots our theology** in the creeds and confessions, so that we receive them with understanding; provides **examples** of faith, courage, and holiness (Heb. 11-12); fosters **humility** (the church has also sinned and erred); and helps us **understand other traditions** and our own. "Remember your leaders" (Heb. 13:7). |
| 1 | Briefly trace the spread of Christianity through the centuries. | **AD 33:** Pentecost and the apostles; the gospel spreads from Jerusalem to Judea, Samaria, and Rome (Acts). **1st-3rd centuries:** the church grows despite Roman **persecution**; apologists defend the faith; the New Testament canon forms. **313:** the **Edict of Milan** (Constantine) ends persecution; **325** Nicea. **4th-5th centuries:** the creeds; Augustine; the empire falls in the West (476); monasticism. **6th-10th:** missions to the Celts, Anglo-Saxons (Augustine of Canterbury, 597), and Germanic and Slavic peoples (Cyril and Methodius); the Eastern and Western churches drift apart. **1054:** the **split** of Eastern Orthodox and Roman Catholic churches. **11th-15th:** medieval papacy, Crusades, scholasticism, monastic and reform movements; Wycliffe and Hus. **1517:** the **Reformation**. **16th-17th:** Protestant churches; Catholic Reformation; missions to the Americas and Asia. **18th:** awakenings, Pietism, and the **Moravians**; **19th:** the **modern missionary movement** (Carey, 1792) and revivals; **20th:** Pentecostalism, fundamentalism and modernism, the explosive growth of the church in the **Global South** (Africa, Asia, Latin America). |
| 1 | What were the "sola's" of the Reformation? | The **five solas**: **Sola Scriptura** (Scripture alone is the final authority); **Sola Fide** (justification by faith alone); **Sola Gratia** (salvation by grace alone); **Solus Christus** (Christ alone is mediator and Savior); **Soli Deo Gloria** (to the glory of God alone). The first two were the "formal and material principles" of the Reformation. |
| 2 | Briefly discuss the development of "covenant theology." | **Early roots:** Irenaeus and Augustine see God's dealings in successive covenants. **Reformation:** **Zwingli** and **Bullinger** (Zurich) develop the unity of the covenant and the link between circumcision and baptism; **Calvin** stresses the unity of the covenant of grace across both Testaments. **Late 16th century:** **Ursinus and Olevianus** (Heidelberg) distinguish a **covenant of works** and a **covenant of grace**. **17th century:** **Cocceius** (the "federal theology") and the **Puritans** (Perkins, Ames, Owen) develop it fully; the **Westminster Confession** (1646) sets it out in chapter 7. **Later:** Witsius, Turretin, the Princeton theologians (Hodge); **Geerhardus Vos** (covenant and biblical theology); **John Murray** and **Meredith Kline** in the 20th century. |
