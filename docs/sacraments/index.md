# Sacraments

<!-- cols:1 continuous -->

Chapell, theology section L. The PCA examines the sacraments **separately** from theology (BCO 21-4). Standards: **WCF 27-29; WLC 161-177; WSC 91-97** (Chapell assigns **WSC 88 and 91-98** for memorization). Hot topics here: **re-baptism**, **paedo-communion**, and **fencing the Lord's Table**. On hot topics, state and defend your **own** view; the notes give the Standards' position and the main alternatives. The **Tier** column ranks each question (1 = know cold, 2 = second pass, 3 = if time allows).

## The Sacraments in General

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is a sacrament? Explain in layman's terms. Defend with Scripture proofs. | WSC 92: "A sacrament is an holy ordinance instituted by Christ; wherein, by sensible signs, Christ, and the benefits of the new covenant, are represented, sealed, and applied to believers." In plain terms: a **visible word**, an act using **physical elements** (water, bread, wine) that **pictures** the gospel and **confirms** God's promises to those who trust him. It is a **sign and seal** of the covenant of grace (WCF 27.1; Rom. 4:11; Gen. 17:11). Matt. 28:19; 1 Cor. 11:23-26. |
| 1 | How many sacraments are there? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | **Two**: **baptism** and the **Lord's Supper** (WCF 27.4; Matt. 28:19; 1 Cor. 11:23-26), because only these were instituted by Christ for the whole church. Rome adds five (confirmation, penance, holy orders, marriage, extreme unction) without institution by Christ. |
| 1 | Does anything really happen in a sacrament? | **Yes**: not by any power in the sacrament or in the person who administers it, but by the **working of the Holy Spirit** and the **word of institution**, grace is truly exhibited and conferred on those who receive it **by faith** (WCF 27.3; 28.6; 29.7). 1 Cor. 10:16; Rom. 6:3-4; Gal. 3:27; Col. 2:12. Against **ex opere operato** (Rome: the sacrament works automatically) and against the view that it is a **bare sign** or mere memorial (Zwingli). |
| 2 | Cite at least four New Testament references showing parallels between the Old Testament and New Testament sacraments (quote 2). | **Col. 2:11-12:** "In him also you were circumcised with a circumcision made without hands, by putting off the body of the flesh, by the circumcision of Christ, having been buried with him in baptism, in which you were also raised with him through faith in the powerful working of God, who raised him from the dead." **1 Cor. 5:7:** "Cleanse out the old leaven that you may be a new lump, as you really are unleavened. For Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed." Also **Rom. 4:11** (circumcision, "a sign or seal of the righteousness"); **1 Cor. 10:1-4** (baptized into Moses in the cloud and sea; the same spiritual food and drink); **Luke 22:15-20** (Passover and the Supper); 1 Pet. 3:20-21 (the flood and baptism); Gal. 3:27-29. |

## Baptism

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Of what is baptism a sign and seal? | WSC 94: "Baptism is a sacrament, wherein the washing with water, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, doth signify and seal our ingrafting into Christ, and partaking of the benefits of the covenant of grace, and our engagement to be the Lord's." It signifies **union with Christ**, **cleansing from sin**, **regeneration**, and **giving up to God** (WCF 28.1; Rom. 6:3-4; Gal. 3:27; Col. 2:11-12; Titus 3:5; Acts 22:16; 1 Pet. 3:21). |
| 2 | Where in Scripture are these terms ("sign" and "seal") used? | **Gen. 17:11** (circumcision, "a sign of the covenant"); **Rom. 4:11** (Abraham "received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness that he had by faith"); the Spirit as God's **seal** (Eph. 1:13; 4:30; 2 Cor. 1:22). A **sign** points to something; a **seal** confirms and guarantees it. |
| 2 | How does the latter term ("seal") relate to paedo-baptism? | Circumcision was a **seal of the righteousness of faith** (Rom. 4:11) and was nevertheless given to **infants** (Gen. 17:12), so a seal **need not wait on the recipient's faith** and does not itself guarantee that the individual has the faith it signifies. For paedo-baptists, it is God's promise and claim on the child in the covenant community, which must be received by faith. Baptists reply that a seal belongs only to those who profess faith. |
| 1 | How is baptism rightly administered? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | With **water**, **in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit**, by a **minister of the Word lawfully called** (WCF 28.2; Matt. 28:19-20). **Immersion is not necessary**: "baptism is rightly administered by pouring or sprinkling water upon the person" (28.3; Ezek. 36:25; Isa. 52:15; Acts 2:17; Heb. 9:10, 19-22; Mark 7:4). It is administered **once** (28.7). |
| 1 | Would you under any circumstances baptize someone by immersion? Explain. | Yes: the **mode** is not essential, and baptism by immersion is a **valid baptism** (the Greek word can mean washing; WCF 28.3 says immersion is "not necessary," not forbidden). A pastor may baptize by immersion to respect a person's conscience, with the **session's approval**, while teaching that sprinkling or pouring is the biblical pattern. |
| 1 | Who is to be baptized? Defend your answer with Scripture proofs. | WSC 95: those who **profess faith in Christ and obedience to him**, and the **infants of one or both believing parents** (WCF 28.4). **Covenant argument:** the promise is "to you and to your children" (Acts 2:38-39; Gen. 17:7, 12); baptism replaces circumcision (Col. 2:11-12); household baptisms (Acts 11:14; 16:15, 31-34; 1 Cor. 1:16); Christ welcomed children (Mark 10:13-16); the children of believers are "holy" (1 Cor. 7:14). |
| 2 | How would you deal with a family in your church who did not want their child baptized? | **Pastorally, not coercively.** Listen to their convictions (many are sincere Baptists), teach the covenant meaning of baptism from Scripture, pray, and give time. A child is not baptized without the parents' consent. Follow the **session's** judgment and BCO on how to receive and care for members who hold a credo-baptist view. |
| 1 | Does baptism actually save the person? Explain and defend with proofs. | **No**: baptism does not itself regenerate. Grace and salvation are **not so inseparably annexed** to it that no one can be regenerated or saved without it, or that all who are baptized are undoubtedly regenerated (WCF 28.5). The efficacy "is not tied to that moment of time wherein it is administered" (28.6). Simon the magician was baptized yet unsaved (Acts 8:13, 20-23); the thief on the cross was saved without baptism (Luke 23:43); Cornelius received the Spirit before baptism (Acts 10:44-48); Rom. 2:28-29; 1 Cor. 1:14-17. 1 Pet. 3:21 ("baptism... now saves you, not as a removal of dirt from the body but as an appeal to God for a good conscience") describes baptism as a **sign** that saves when received by faith. |
| 1 | Can a person be baptized more than once? Explain and defend with Scripture proofs. | **No** (WCF 28.7; Eph. 4:5, "one Lord, one faith, one baptism"). A valid baptism (water, in the Triune name, in a visible church, by an authorized minister) is **not repeated**. Acts 19:1-7 involved **John's** baptism, not Christian baptism. **Hot topic: Re-baptism**: those baptized as infants who later join Baptist churches, and requests from persons baptized in other traditions. |
| 2 | How would you handle a request for baptism from a previously baptized Roman Catholic? | Reformed practice has differed. The **Old School Presbyterian Assembly (1845)** declared Roman Catholic baptism invalid (Charles Hodge dissented); others (Calvin and many Reformed theologians) accepted it as valid but corrupt, since it is administered with water in the Triune name in a body that, though greatly erring, is a visible church. Know your presbytery's and the Assembly's rulings. In practice, examine whether the person understands and professes the gospel, and follow the session's decision on the baptism's validity. |
| 2 | How would you handle a request for baptism from a previously baptized former liberal? | If baptized with water in the name of the **Father, Son, and Holy Spirit** by a minister of a church that confesses the Triune God, the baptism is **valid** even though the church has erred, and is **not repeated** (WCF 28.7). The pastoral task is to ensure that the person now has a **credible profession of faith**. |
| 2 | How would you handle a request for baptism from a previously baptized Mormon? | Treat Mormon baptism as **invalid**: it is not in the name of the **Triune God** of Scripture (Mormonism teaches three separate gods and a created Christ), so it is not Christian baptism (Matt. 28:19). Baptize after a **credible profession** of faith in the biblical Christ, following the session's examination. |

## The Lord's Supper

| Tier | Question | Answer and proofs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the meaning of the Lord's Supper? | WSC 96: "The Lord's supper is a sacrament, wherein, by giving and receiving bread and wine, according to Christ's appointment, his death is showed forth; and the worthy receivers are, not after a corporal and carnal manner, but by faith, made partakers of his body and blood, with all his benefits, to their spiritual nourishment, and growth in grace." It is **remembrance** (Luke 22:19), **proclamation** (1 Cor. 11:26), **communion** with Christ and his people (1 Cor. 10:16-17), **covenant renewal**, and **anticipation** of the feast to come (Matt. 26:29; Rev. 19:9). WCF 29.1. |
| 1 | What "happens" in the Lord's Supper? | Believers, by faith, **truly and really, yet spiritually**, receive and feed upon **Christ crucified** and all the benefits of his death; Christ is spiritually present to the faith of believers, as the elements are to their outward senses (WCF 29.7). The **Spirit** lifts believers to feed on Christ (John 6:53-58, 63; 1 Cor. 10:16). Their faith is strengthened and they grow in grace. The **unworthy** receive only the outward elements, and eat and drink judgment to themselves (WCF 29.8; 1 Cor. 11:27-30). |
| 1 | Distinguish Roman Catholic, Lutheran, Reformed and Zwinglian views. | **Roman Catholic:** **transubstantiation**: the substance of the bread and wine becomes the body and blood of Christ; the Mass is a repeated sacrifice (WCF 29.2, 29.6; Heb. 9:25-28; 10:10-14). **Lutheran:** the **sacramental union**: Christ's body and blood are present "in, with, and under" the bread and wine, and received by all who partake, including the unbelieving. **Reformed:** **real spiritual presence**: Christ is truly present by the Holy Spirit and received by **faith**; his human body remains in heaven (Acts 1:11; 3:21). **Zwinglian:** a **memorial**: the Supper is a symbol and remembrance of Christ's death and our pledge. |
| 1 | Is Christ in any sense present in the Lord's Supper? If so, how? | **Yes, spiritually**: Christ is present by his Spirit to the faith of the believer who receives the elements (WCF 29.7; 1 Cor. 10:16; Matt. 28:20; John 6:63). Not bodily in the bread and wine (against Rome and Lutheranism), and not merely absent (against Zwingli). |
| 1 | How should believers celebrate the Lord's Supper? | With **self-examination** (1 Cor. 11:28), **faith**, **repentance**, **love**, and **reconciliation** with others (Matt. 5:23-24; 1 Cor. 11:20-22), **discerning the body** (11:29), in a gathered church, with a minister **setting apart the elements** with prayer, **breaking the bread**, and **giving and receiving** (WCF 29.3-4; 1 Cor. 11:23-26). Not a private mass; no adoration of the elements. |
| 1 | How is "fencing the table" practiced during communion? | The minister gives a **warning and invitation** that identifies who may and who may not come to the table: the **impenitent, scandalous, or unbelieving** are to stay away and the **baptized believers** who trust Christ and are in good standing in a true church are invited (1 Cor. 11:27-32; WCF 29.8; Matt. 7:6; 1 Cor. 5:11; 10:21). The **session** has the oversight and may bar from the table (church discipline). Practice varies on how open the invitation is, and on how visitors are to be examined. **Hot topic: Fencing the Lord's Table.** |
| 2 | (Related hot topic) What is paedo-communion? | Giving the Lord's Supper to **baptized infants and young children**. Arguments for: children shared in the Passover and the covenant meals (Ex. 12:26-27; Deut. 16:11) and household baptism implies household communion. Standards and PCA practice **against**: communicants must **examine themselves**, **discern the body**, and show their faith (1 Cor. 11:28-29; WLC 177: baptism is administered to infants, but the Supper only to those "of years and ability to examine themselves"; WCF 29.7-8). **Hot topic: Paedo-communion.** |
